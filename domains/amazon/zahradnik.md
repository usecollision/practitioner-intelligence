# Brent Zahradnik — Amazon advertising agency operator & DSP specialist

## Beliefs
- Amazon advertising is a managed discipline: hundreds of accounts' worth of data beats single-account intuition (AMZ Pathfinder; Clarity profile).
- DSP is a real, separate skill from search PPC — "someone on YouTube calling Seller Central display ads 'DSP' is wrong; DSP is programmatic buying across Amazon + third-party inventory" (QA Selling Online podcast, 2020).
- Measurement and reporting (ACoS/ROAS/TACoS trends, quarterly gameplans) are client-facing deliverables that drive optimization (AMZ Pathfinder quarterly ad report product).

## Principles
- Campaign structure scales with data: keyword rank campaigns, negatives discipline (negative products on auto campaigns was a big change he flagged, 2020).
- Bids vs negatives: some operators lower bids instead of negating; the disciplined answer depends on whether the term has strategic value (QA podcast, 2020 — question he was asked; his agency practice favors structured negatives).
- Event-day strategy: on Prime Day-type events, decide CPC-increase vs budget-increase deliberately — don't just raise everything (QA podcast, 2020).
- If you lower a keyword's CPC after 3-4 days of elevation, expect impression impact — treat bid changes as having persistence effects (QA podcast, 2020).
- Video ad rejection rules are mechanical: 45 seconds max, no URL overlay, don't start with a white screen (QA podcast, 2020).

## Frameworks
- **Full-funnel Amazon advertising** (agency operating model): SP for capture → SB for brand/category share → SD for retargeting/conquesting → DSP for upper funnel; each with its own KPIs (AMZ Pathfinder practice).
- **DSP flywheel** (Pathfinder/SellerPlex practice): DSP audiences built from product-level profitability → search efficiency improves → reallocate budget; DSP and search are one system, not separate channels (consistent with Darkroom's published flywheel).
- **Quarterly Advertising Gameplan** (AMZ Pathfinder product): quarterly review of spend/ACoS/ROAS + trend report + action plan.

## Processes
1. Audit account structure and search-term hygiene.
2. Set stage-appropriate ACoS targets (e.g., 4-month-old brand at <90% ACoS is launch-acceptable; don't panic).
3. Optimize weekly: bids, negatives, placements; SB/SD/DSP layers added as data justifies.
4. Quarterly: report + reallocate + gameplan.

## Heuristics
- ACoS for a 4-month-old brand near break-even is fine if rank is building (QA podcast).
- Non-branded revenue percentage-based pricing (his agency charged base + 1.5% of non-branded ad revenue) — aligns agency incentives with growth, not just spend.
- New-to-brand is a real acquisition signal: SB campaigns delivered 88% NTB attributed orders for a client (AMZ Pathfinder case).
- DSP requires its own expertise and access path; most sellers can't self-serve it well (QA podcast, 2020).

## Tactics
- Negative products on auto campaigns (2020-era change adoption).
- SB reports (new 2020-2022 reports) mined for NTB and placement insights (AMZ Pathfinder blog, 2022).
- AMC (Amazon Marketing Cloud) adoption for measurement beyond console (Pathfinder content, 2024-2025).
- Holiday-rush lessons → Q1 action plans (eCommerceChris podcast, Jan 2025).

## Tools
- Amazon Ads console, AMC, Prestozon API automation (Pathfinder), Helium 10 (search volume checks), Seller Central.

## Inputs
- Ad spend/ACoS/ROAS by campaign, search-term reports, NTB reports, quarterly trend data, client margin data.

## Outputs
- Campaign architectures, quarterly ad reports/gameplans, DSP strategies, measurement frameworks.

## Metrics
- ACoS (blended and by campaign — client case: reduced blended ACoS to 9%), ROAS (case: 3.25 overall ROAS / 30.7% ACoS with 125% sales growth 2020→2021 for Viter Energy), NTB%, TACoS trend.

## Decision rules
- IF brand is <6 months old and rank is climbing THEN accept ACoS near break-even; don't cut (HEURISTIC, T2).
- IF raising CPCs for an event THEN decide CPC vs budget lever per keyword and expect impression persistence when you lower again (HEURISTIC, T2).
- IF term is junk and strategically valueless THEN negate; if it's a category browse term with potential, bid-lower instead (HEURISTIC, T2).
- IF client has scale (millions managed/month) THEN layer DSP + AMC measurement (HEURISTIC, T2).
- IF video ads get rejected THEN check length ≤45s, no URL overlay, no white-screen start (FACT, T2).

## Failure modes
- Calling Seller Central display ads "DSP" — wrong tool, wrong expectations (QA podcast).
- Raising all bids during events without a lever strategy.
- Treating bid changes as stateless — impressions suffer when you drop back.
- Launch clients panicking on ACoS before rank forms.

## Contrarian beliefs
- DSP belongs in the mix for *some* mid-market brands, not just enterprise (his Viter Energy case: DSP-assisted growth at mid-market scale), vs the Darkroom <$50k threshold (disagreement noted in synthesis).

## Examples
- Viter Energy case: 3.25 ROAS / 30.7% ACoS overall + 125% sales growth (2020→2021) with DSP component (AMZ Pathfinder case study).
- ThinkGizmos: automated campaign management via API (Prestozon) (AMZ Pathfinder case).
- Ash (e-commerce owner): blended ACoS reduced to 9% (AMZ Pathfinder case).

## Conditions
- His methods assume agency-grade data volume and dedicated management; single-operator accounts should scale the discipline (weekly reports, quarterly gameplan) but skip enterprise layers.

## Limitations
- Agency case studies are self-selected (best results published); pricing/scale details UNKNOWN; DSP specifics (budgets, audiences) not published in detail.
