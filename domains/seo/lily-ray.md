## Beliefs
- Core updates are not aimed at specific sites but at "improving overall how our systems assess quality" — yet drops reliably flag problems Google has identified on the site (FACT/OPINION, synthesizing Google's framing with observed outcomes).
- Understanding search intent shifts over time is the key to staying ahead; ranking fluctuations are often intent migration, not site faults (FRAMEWORK from MozCon 2023 "Google's Just Not That Into You").
- Evidence-first: she publishes analyses of SERP changes, AI Mode behavior, and E-E-A-T patterns (OPINION).

## Principles
- Sites get flagged in core updates for recognizable site-wide patterns — these are the audit checklist (HEURISTIC, from observed cases):
  - Invasive advertising impairing experience
  - Superficial/repetitive content
  - Copious low-grade local landing pages (programmatic doorways)
  - Content lacking E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)
  - Misleading affiliate links
  - Overly sales-oriented "money pages"
- Short-term wins from duplicative low-value pages (e.g., mass local landing pages) get clawed back at core updates (EMPIRICAL).

## Processes
1. Watch update rollouts and correlate drops with update timing.
2. Audit against the known site-wide issue patterns (the six above).
3. Assess whether the drop is site-quality-driven vs SERP-intent shift by examining which queries/industries moved.
4. Remediate quality issues (content freshness, UX, E-E-A-T signals, affiliate transparency).
5. Track larger search patterns over time (intent migration) rather than single-keyword movements.

## Heuristics
- "Money pages" that are pure sales pitches without value get demoted (HEURISTIC).
- Mass-produced local landing pages are a leading core-update liability (HEURISTIC — corroborated by Joy Hawkins' local work and the 2024/2025 local/parasite updates).
- Freshness and ongoing content updates are cheap insurance (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Regularly update content; prioritize load speed, mobile-friendliness, usability (TACTIC).
- Cite authoritative sources, transparent authorship, demonstrate experience — operationalize E-E-A-T (TACTIC).
- Diversify intent coverage so intent shifts don't empty the funnel (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Google Search Console, SERP tracking tools, Amsive's organic research stack (specific toolset UNKNOWN).

## Metrics
- Rankings and traffic correlated with core update timelines; share of SERP features; AI-search visibility (in recent work).

## Decision rules
- Drop coincides with update + site exhibits ≥1 of the six site-wide patterns → treat as quality problem, remediate across the whole site (DECISION RULE).
- Drop coincides with update + site clean + queries now served by a different format → intent shift; build the format Google now rewards (DECISION RULE).
- Seeing mass low-value programmatic pages (especially local) → expect long-term core-update risk; prune or substantially differentiate (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Local landing page programs at scale ("copious low-grade local landing pages") — a repeated, named failure pattern (EMPIRICAL).
- Excessive sales orientation on money pages; misleading affiliate setups (EMPIRICAL).
- Treating intent-shift drops as quality problems and destroying content that was fine (implied by her intent work).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Much of what SEOs call "penalties" is intent migration — Google changed what it wants for the query, not what it thinks of you (OPINION, shared with Glenn Gabe's intent-shift category).

## Examples
- Documented websites that dropped after creating abundant duplicative local landing pages (EXAMPLE).
- MozCon 2023 case walkthroughs of intent migration across industries (EXAMPLE, presentation).

## Conditions
- Works best for sites with meaningful traffic history and observable SERP footprints; her pattern list is a strong audit checklist for content-heavy sites.
- Intent analysis requires SERP history tools.

## Limitations
- Pattern list is diagnostic, not predictive; no causal testing; E-E-A-T remediation effects lag by months (same long-game constraint as Gabe).
