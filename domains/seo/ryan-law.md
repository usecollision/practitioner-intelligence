## Beliefs
- Content should convert, not just rank: the Animalz canon (OPINION — shared across his essays and the Animalz school).
- For their own brand, Animalz deliberately chose thought leadership over SEO-centric content: their buyers (VPs of marketing) don't Google generic content-marketing keywords, and the niche is saturated with agencies writing about content marketing (OPINION/EXAMPLE — a documented self-experiment).
- Keyword search volume should not be the topic-selection driver; address the pain points of a precisely defined audience regardless of ranking potential (OPINION — stated in the interview).

## Frameworks
- **Problem-solving vs thought-leadership topic split** (his two groups of content topics): Group 1 solves the target audience's problems; Group 2 builds perspective/point-of-view (FRAMEWORK).
- **Content that converts**: map content to funnel stage and buying context; remove fluff (FRAMEWORK — the "brevity" school; master map notes his depth-vs-brevity disagreement with Koray).

## Processes
1. Define the precise target audience (not keyword-first).
2. Split topics into problem-solving vs thought-leadership.
3. Ignore search volume in selection; prioritize resonance with the buyer.
4. Write tight, opinionated content that demonstrates expertise (recently adapted for AI-search citation: answer structure up front, citable claims).

## Heuristics
- If your buyer doesn't search the topic, ranking for it is worthless (HEURISTIC).
- Saturated keyword spaces are negative-ROI for new entrants — pick audience-first topics (HEURISTIC).
- AI answers prefer directly answerable, well-sourced passages — structure for extraction (HEURISTIC, from his GEO work).

## Tactics
- Shorten content; remove generic filler; state answers early (TACTIC).
- Lead with a point of view rather than a definition (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Standard content stack (specifics UNKNOWN); audience research tools.

## Inputs
- Audience definition, buyer pain points, sales/customer calls, competitive content landscape.

## Outputs
- Content strategies, editorial systems, essays on SaaS content.

## Metrics
- Conversion contribution (demos, signups) over rankings; audience resonance.

## Decision rules
- Buyer searches the topic + you can add perspective → write it (DECISION RULE).
- Buyer doesn't search it → thought-leadership/PR route, not SEO route (DECISION RULE — Animalz's own choice).
- Topic saturated + no differentiation angle → skip (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Publishing SEO-driven content nobody in the audience searches for (the anti-pattern behind Animalz's own strategy choice) (EXAMPLE/OPINION).
- Long, fluff-padded content that buries the answer (his repeated critique).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Keyword volume as topic selection is over-rated — audience pain beats volume (OPINION; directly contrasts with volume-first workflows like early Soulo-era Ahrefs advice, though Soulo's Traffic Potential metric partially bridges this).

## Conditions
- Works for B2B/SaaS with well-defined ICPs and long sales cycles; where buyers consume content off-SERP (newsletters, LinkedIn, podcasts) as much as on-SERP.
- Thought-leadership path requires genuine expertise + distribution channels.

## Limitations
- His approach under-weights capture of existing demand (top-of-funnel volume); works poorly for e-commerce and transactional search; thought leadership is slower than SEO compounding.
