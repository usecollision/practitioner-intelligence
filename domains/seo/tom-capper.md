## Beliefs
- Published "ranking factor studies" are correlation studies, not causal proof — he takes personal responsibility for popularizing the genre and spends effort correcting its misinterpretation (OPINION/EMPIRICAL — explicit mea culpa).
- A ranking factor is narrowly defined: "a variable that a search engine uses to decide the best ordering of relevant, indexed results" — most things that "influence rankings" (indexation, relevance selection) are not ranking factors (FRAMEWORK — definitional rigor).
- We don't know how many ranking factors exist; Google deliberately keeps them secret because the algorithm is their competitive advantage (OPINION).

## Frameworks
- **Correlation ≠ causation taxonomy for ranking studies**: four explanations for any observed correlation (coincidence; reverse causation — well-ranking pages get seen more; common cause — popularity drives both; genuine factor) (FRAMEWORK — his Facebook-Likes example).
- **Behavioral vs declared**: Google's official line says they adjust algorithms to correlate with UX; real-world experiments suggest direct behavioral measurement (e.g., time-to-SERP-interaction, bounce-back rate) (FRAMEWORK/HYPOTHESIS).

## Processes
1. Before trusting a ranking-factor claim, ask: which of the four explanations fits?
2. Check whether the claim is about ordering (ranking factor) or inclusion (relevance/indexation) — different mechanisms.
3. Prefer experimental evidence (e.g., SearchPilot-style tests) over correlational studies.

## Heuristics
- "Not everything that influences rankings is a ranking factor" (HEURISTIC).
- Correlation between a page quality and rankings is expected — it doesn't prove Google measures that quality directly (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Run/read controlled experiments over correlation studies (TACTIC).
- Keep skepticism of vendor "ranking factor" marketing (TACTIC).

## Tools

## Outputs

## Metrics

## Decision rules
- Claim is backed only by correlation → treat as hypothesis, not fact (DECISION RULE).
- Claim confuses relevance/indexation with ranking → reframe before acting (DECISION RULE).
- A change is testable → prefer testing over factor lore (DECISION RULE — aligns with Critchlow).

## Failure modes
- Acting on ranking-factor listicles as if causal (the genre's systemic failure he critiques).
- Treating "UX is a ranking factor" as a measurable lever — UX is not a metric (warned).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Even Google-confirmed factors (HTTPS, speed) may be confirmed "as much as anything to push the industry to change the internet in a direction they'd like" — official confirmations are not neutral science (OPINION — sharp skepticism).

## Conditions
- His method applies to anyone making SEO investment decisions; the four-explanation taxonomy is a universal audit filter.

## Limitations
