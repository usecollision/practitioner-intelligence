## Beliefs
- Citations are the hardest part of local visibility done right — and doing it right takes a few hours; doing it wrong takes many hours (and possibly money) (OPINION/HEURISTIC).
- Citations are NOT links: they are mentions of name, address, phone (NAP); their value to Google is relevance/trust for a city or region, not PageRank (FRAMEWORK — a persistent industry confusion he corrects).

## Frameworks
- **NAP consistency discipline**: accurate, consistent name-address-phone across all listings is the citation baseline (FRAMEWORK).
- **Citation tiers**: prioritize by importance (Google Business Profile first, then major aggregators/data suppliers, then niche/local directories) (FRAMEWORK — Whitespark's tiered model).

## Processes
1. Audit existing citations (accuracy, duplicates, inconsistencies).
2. Prioritize citation targets (major data suppliers and aggregators first — see Blumenthal's ecosystem work).
3. Build/claim listings with exact NAP consistency.
4. Track and maintain over time (citations rot when NAP changes).

## Heuristics
- A few high-quality citations from primary data sources beat dozens of weak directory listings (HEURISTIC).
- Cheap/fast citation services create cleanup debt (HEURISTIC — "cheap citation services" warned against).

## Tactics
- Use citation-building tools (Whitespark) for discovery/management (TACTIC).
- Fix NAP errors at the primary data suppliers before niche directories (TACTIC — matches Blumenthal's ground-truth model).

## Tools
- Whitespark (citation finder/builder, local rank tracker).

## Inputs
- Business NAP, existing listing inventory, competitor citation landscapes.

## Outputs
- Citation audits, listing builds, local rank tracking reports.

## Metrics
- Citation accuracy/consistency, citation counts by tier, local rankings.

## Decision rules
- New local business → GBP + primary data suppliers first, niche directories only after (DECISION RULE).
- NAP changed (move, rebrand) → fix primary suppliers first; downstream directories follow (DECISION RULE).
- Citation service offers quantity over accuracy → refuse (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Confusing citations with link building — chasing links where consistency is the real signal (warned).
- Inconsistent NAP across the web — the #1 citation failure (warned).
- Ignoring citations entirely — invisibility in local results (warned).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Citation volume is over-rated; accuracy and source priority matter more (OPINION — moderating the "citations are dead" vs "citations are everything" debate).

## Conditions
- Local businesses with physical/service areas; multi-location chains benefit most; low-competition local niches see fastest results.

## Limitations
- Citation impact has declined relative to reviews/GBP signals (field consensus; his own tooling pivoted to rank tracking); citation-only strategies fail without GBP + reviews + landing pages (needs Joy Hawkins' triad).
