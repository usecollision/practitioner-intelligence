## Beliefs
- SEO recommendations that come from generic checklists are not strategic: even when executed perfectly, they don't achieve what decision-makers expect because they aren't tied to business goals (OPINION — core critique of checklist SEO).
- Prioritization is the core deliverable of an audit, not the findings list (FRAMEWORK).

## Frameworks
- **Impact × Effort prioritization matrix** (her public Google Sheets template): each recommendation scored by expected impact and required effort; start with highest impact/lowest effort; remove low-impact/high-effort items (FRAMEWORK).
- **Strategic, solutions-oriented recommendations**: every finding must be connected to a business goal, expressed as an action, with scope (area/page types) and resource types defined (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
1. Audit (technical, international, GEO as appropriate).
2. Convert each issue/opportunity into a recommendation: goal-aligned, solution-oriented, scoped, with impact and effort ratings.
3. Prioritize: high impact + low effort first; drop low impact + high effort.
4. Present via a structured deck: prioritized summary first, then per-recommendation "why it matters / where found / how to fix with examples".
5. Win stakeholder support and track execution.

## Heuristics
- Checklist audits produce un-actionable dumps; goal-aligned recommendations get resources (HEURISTIC).
- Communicate in the stakeholders' language (business impact), not SEO jargon (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Use her prioritization spreadsheet + slide templates as the audit deliverable format (TACTIC).
- Explicitly define resources needed (types, not just hours) per recommendation (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Google Sheets/Slides templates (published), GSC, crawlers; international SEO tooling (hreflang validation tools).

## Inputs
- Business goals and KPIs; audit data; stakeholder resource constraints.

## Outputs
- Prioritized recommendation sheets; stakeholder decks; implementation tracking.

## Metrics
- Impact scores (traffic/revenue potential per recommendation), effort scores, execution rate.

## Decision rules
- Impact high + effort low → top of backlog, do now (DECISION RULE).
- Impact low + effort high → remove (never include for padding) (DECISION RULE).
- Recommendation can't be tied to a goal → don't ship it as-is; reframe or drop (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Delivering 300-slide finding dumps (same failure Stox names; both independently converge).
- Executing SEO "best practices" with no goal linkage — effort without business result, losing stakeholder trust (warned).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Technical perfection is not the objective; strategic alignment is (OPINION, shared with Stox's "no major site is technically perfect").

## Conditions
- Works for consultant-client contexts and in-house teams reporting to non-SEO stakeholders; scales from SMB to enterprise.
- The matrix needs honest effort estimates from engineering — fails where effort can't be estimated.

## Limitations
- Impact scoring is judgment-based (no causal data); the framework prioritizes communication as much as SEO — teams needing pure technical depth get more from Stox-style engineering-ready tickets (complementary).
