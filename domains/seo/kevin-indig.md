## Beliefs
- "Programmatic SEO is the most effective way to scale SEO" — building hundreds of thousands of pages from a keyword pattern (OPINION/FACT of field practice).
- Google updates are the #1 risk to organic traffic; there is no perfect insurance, but technical hygiene + content maintenance materially reduce risk (EMPIRICAL/HEURISTIC).
- AI search (ChatGPT, AI Overviews) is shifting visibility: he runs recurring studies tracking brand citation in AI answers — competitors get cited even when the brand outranks them in classic SERPs (EMPIRICAL from his AI search studies).

## Frameworks
- **Content decay maintenance model**: new content ramps slowly while old content decays — without maintenance you run a "spinning wheels" treadmill where gains get eaten by decay (FRAMEWORK — the publish-and-forget failure mode).
- **Technical hygiene as update insurance**: 404s, crawl waste, and errors increase update-hit risk (FRAMEWORK/HEURISTIC — consistent with Mueller + Stox).

## Processes
1. Build a keyword-pattern model for programmatic pages (e.g., location/feature/use-case matrices).
2. Ensure template quality — each page must carry unique value (data, entities, differentiation), not just swapped tokens.
3. Maintain the content body over time (refresh decay, kill 404s).
4. Track both classic SERP visibility AND AI-answer citations (brand vs competitors).

## Heuristics
- New content ramps up slowly; existing content decays — most SEO growth plans ignore the decay side (HEURISTIC).
- Lots of 404s/errors = "technical waste" that makes Google crawl less often (HEURISTIC, corroborated by Mueller's model).
- If a competitor is cited in AI answers for queries you rank #1 in classic SERPs, you have an AI visibility gap (HEURISTIC from his studies).

## Tactics
- Publish programmatic pages in waves and watch indexation + quality floors before scaling (TACTIC — his documented caution).
- Refresh outdated content bodies systematically (TACTIC).
- Track AI citation share alongside rankings (TACTIC — his public studies).

## Tools
- Google Search Console, Ahrefs-class tools, his own AI search tracking methodology (published studies on ChatGPT/AI Overviews citation rates).

## Metrics
- Organic traffic growth vs decay curve; AI citation share; indexation rate of programmatic pages; core-update volatility exposure.

## Decision rules
- Building a programmatic play → require each template to produce genuinely distinct pages or expect update risk (DECISION RULE, consistent with 2024-2026 scaled-content enforcement).
- Content body aging without refresh → refresh or prune before it drags site quality (DECISION RULE).
- Ranking #1 but absent from AI answers → build citable structure (answers up front, stats, sources) (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Publish-and-forget content bodies (named as a top cause of decay; "a lot of companies publish content and then forget about it") (EMPIRICAL).
- Scaling programmatic without a quality floor — the 2024-26 scaled-content-abuse enforcement era made this the field's canonical failure (EMPIRICAL, widely documented).
- Treating rankings as the only KPI while AI citations shift elsewhere (OPINION).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Rankings are a means, not the goal: AI-answer citations can decouple from rankings, so SERP-only dashboards lie (OPINION/EMPIRICAL from his studies).

## Examples
- Recurring AI search visibility studies (Growth Memo) showing brands losing ChatGPT citations to competitors (EXAMPLE — his published data).

## Conditions
- Programmatic methods suit SaaS/e-commerce/location-based businesses with inherent page-matrix structures; AI-tracking suits B2B/SaaS where ChatGPT usage is high.
- His strategy advice assumes a growth-stage company with engineering access.

## Limitations
- AI-search study data is observational (correlation ≠ Google's ranking logic); programmatic guidance is general — per-vertical conditions vary; some Growth Memo posts are paywalled (one fetch was blocked).
