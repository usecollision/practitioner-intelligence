## Beliefs
- Digital PR should be measured by consistency, not virality: "seeing the full campaign history helps establish consistency over virality" (OPINION — MozCon 2020 core argument).
- Most campaigns produce 1-20 links — "which is normal and effective"; the 1,000+ link viral hits distort expectations, team morale, and client expectations (EMPIRICAL — from Aira's State of Link Building findings).
- Campaign failures are learning data, not shame: she publicly shares her own shortcomings to push the industry to learn from failures (OPINION).

## Frameworks
- **Digital PR newsroom model**: a repeatable content-news pipeline (three types of journalist-facing media he identified) rather than one-off stunts (FRAMEWORK — from her MozCon 2019 talk).
- **Expectation calibration**: classify campaigns into huge wins / steady performers / failures; report the full distribution (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
1. Build a newsroom pipeline: monitor news cycles and journalist beats.
2. Create stories with genuine news value (data, surveys, expert commentary, timely angles).
3. Outreach efficiently (her 5 tips: personalization, timing, follow-up discipline).
4. Track every campaign outcome; learn from steady performers and failures.
5. Report the honest distribution — including failures — to clients.

## Heuristics
- 1-20 links per campaign is a good outcome; calibrate expectations there (HEURISTIC/EMPIRICAL).
- Getting emails opened is half the battle — outreach craft matters as much as asset quality (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Newsjacking with real data angles (TACTIC).
- Journalist-list building by beat, not spray (TACTIC).
- Follow-up sequences as standard practice (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Media databases (Muck Rack-class), outreach/email tools, news monitoring.

## Inputs
- News cycle awareness, journalist beat maps, data/creative assets.

## Outputs
- Digital PR campaigns, link reports with honest distributions, newsroom processes.

## Metrics
- Links per campaign (with distribution), coverage quality, campaign consistency over time.

## Decision rules
- Media landscape saturated for your story → either sharpen the news angle or run the campaign for brand, not links (DECISION RULE — implied by her saturation talk).
- Campaign underperforms → analyze as data (angle, timing, list) and iterate; one failure doesn't kill the pipeline (DECISION RULE).
- Reporting only wins → manage expectations down by reporting the honest distribution (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Chasing virality over consistency — the industry-wide distortion she names (OPINION/EMPIRICAL).
- Single-campaign mental models that kill teams' confidence after a flop (warned).
- Outreach without open-rate craft — empty inboxes (warned, Moz talk).

## Contrarian beliefs
- The viral 1,000-link campaign is the exception that should not set the standard (OPINION — directly against the "huge wins" content that dominates LinkedIn).

## Conditions
- Works for consumer/B2B brands with stories to tell; UK/US media ecosystems; needs ongoing newsroom capacity, not one-off projects.

## Limitations
- Digital PR saturation keeps raising the bar; results are lumpy and hard to attribute; not suitable for tiny brands without news hooks; link value from PR is brand-weighted, slow to reflect in rankings.
