## Beliefs
- SEO is a product effort, not a marketing campaign: "Build the asset that the user is searching for" (FRAMEWORK — the core of *Product-Led SEO*).
- Amazon didn't win search with keywords and backlinks — it won by making the product page itself the best search result; same for Zillow, TripAdvisor (OPINION/EXAMPLE).
- Conventional keyword-and-backlink tactics are becoming obsolete; user experience alignment is the durable strategy (OPINION).
- Mid-funnel optimization is increasingly vital as AI shifts behavior (OPINION, 2025 interview).

## Frameworks
- **Product-led SEO**: treat search queries as product requirements; the searchable asset (product page, tool, calculator, database, UGC page) IS the SEO (FRAMEWORK).
- **Search as product feature**: users search for things the product itself can satisfy — build the product surface to match (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
1. Identify what users search for in the category (queries = demand for assets).
2. Determine which asset type satisfies the query best (product page vs article vs tool vs database).
3. Build the asset as product work (with engineering, not just content marketing).
4. Measure whether the asset serves intent (engagement, conversions), not just rank.
5. Iterate like a product team (ship, measure, improve).

## Heuristics
- "SEO really succeeds in aligning with Google's goal of serving users" — intent alignment is the only stable currency (HEURISTIC).
- If the searchable asset is the product, SEO spend compounds like product investment (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Build queryable assets: comparison tools, calculators, databases, UGC/product pages (TACTIC).
- Treat keyword research as feature discovery (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Standard SEO stack; product analytics for intent measurement (specifics UNKNOWN).

## Inputs
- Query demand data; understanding of the product's ability to serve intent; engineering capacity.

## Outputs
- Product-led SEO roadmaps; strategies that produce non-content assets.

## Metrics
- Organic traffic AND engagement/conversion on the asset; queries served per asset.

## Decision rules
- Query is best served by a product surface (pricing, comparison, tool) → build product, not blog post (DECISION RULE).
- Query is informational and product can't serve it → either skip or build a thin-content trap (avoid) (DECISION RULE, implied).
- Content marketing can't differentiate (category saturated) → product-led assets are the alternative (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- 1,000-word blog posts competing with real products ("you're not looking for a 1000-word blog post that uses the word USB 32 times") (OPINION/EXAMPLE).
- Keyword-optimized content with no product truth — users bounce, Google notices (implied).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Backlinks and keyword targeting are over-weighted; product-market-search alignment matters more (OPINION — a real disagreement with classic link-heavy SEO, though he doesn't deny links entirely).

## Examples
- Amazon product pages, Zillow, TripAdvisor; his client work at SurveyMonkey and as consultant (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Works when the product can genuinely serve queries (SaaS, marketplaces, e-commerce, tools); strongest for bottom-funnel and product-comparison queries; requires engineering partnership.

## Limitations
- Less applicable to pure informational/content businesses; needs product teams that accept SEO as product input; results are slow (product cycles).
