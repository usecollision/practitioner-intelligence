## Beliefs
- The local algorithm (three-pack) is driven by three factors per Google's own documentation: relevance, distance, prominence — and practitioner work should be organized around testing how each behaves (FACT — Google Help Center model, which she operationalizes).
- Rankings should be validated with real data: her agency runs large-N studies (8,186 businesses in 200 cities) rather than anecdotes (EMPIRICAL — 2025 "near me" study).

## Frameworks
- **Relevance / Distance / Prominence triad** as the local ranking model (FRAMEWORK — Google's own, which she explains and stress-tests).
- **Landing page content signal**: number of non-stop-words on the GBP-linked landing page correlates with local rankings (EMPIRICAL — from her study).

## Processes
1. Audit GBP setup: address visibility, categories, service-area settings, landing page.
2. Test single variables on real clients (e.g., hide vs show address).
3. Review profile over time (reviews velocity and consistency).
4. Fix the landing page content (real words, relevance).
5. Track rankings in the local pack + map pack separately.

## Heuristics
- Hiding your address as a service-area business negatively correlates with near-me rankings — test it, don't trust Google's suggestion blindly (EMPIRICAL — her study + real client test: rankings tanked when address hidden, recovered when restored).
- Review rating matters but consistency over time matters more than average (EMPIRICAL).
- Filler words on landing pages don't help; real topical words do (EMPIRICAL).

## Tactics
- Run controlled single-variable experiments on real client profiles (TACTIC — her method for the address test).
- Use Local Search Forum as the community diagnostic layer (TACTIC).

## Tools
- GBP, local rank trackers, Local Search Forum, her agency's study data.

## Inputs
- GBP settings, review history, landing page content, rank tracking data.

## Outputs
- Local SEO audits, algorithm-behavior studies, GBP troubleshooting guides.

## Metrics
- Local pack rankings, near-me rankings, review velocity/rating, GBP visibility events.

## Decision rules
- Service-area business struggling in pack → test address visibility; don't assume Google's SAB suggestion is optimal (DECISION RULE — evidence-based, with a documented reversal test).
- Landing page thin or generic → rewrite with substantive topical content before chasing citations/reviews (DECISION RULE).
- Reviews stagnating → invest in review velocity (consistent flow) before obsessing over average rating (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Following GBP setup advice blindly (her address-hiding counterexample) (EMPIRICAL).
- Local landing page programs with "filler words" — flagged by both her data and Lily Ray's core-update pattern list (EMPIRICAL, convergent).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Google's own GBP recommendations are not always rank-optimal — her address test contradicts Google's SAB guidance (EMPIRICAL — the strongest example of practitioner-data-beats-platform-guidance in local SEO).

## Conditions
- Multi-location and single-location local businesses; needs rank-tracking data volume to test; her study findings apply to US near-me queries specifically.

## Limitations
- Near-me study is US-centric; correlation-based (like all local studies); local algorithm changes frequently (her findings have shelf life); single-variable tests are rare luxuries (she has client access most lack).
