## Beliefs
- Avoid the phrase "North Star metric" — it implies one metric drives everyone; rarely that simple (OPINION, T1).
- Use "high-level engagement metric" for product quality instead (T1).
## Principles
- Proxy metrics are the leading indicators: more sensitive, validate hypotheses (T1).
- Threshold metrics beat averages: "percentage of (new/existing customers) who do at least (X) by (Y time)" (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Averages hide distribution; a subset can drive the average up while most users get worse (T1).
- Prove proxy→outcome correlation, then causality, over time (T1).
## Frameworks
- Proxy metric formula: % of customers who do ≥ threshold of value by time period (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Netflix example: % of new customers watching ≥15 min streaming in first month; 5% → 20% in year 1; correlated then causal with retention (EXAMPLE, T1).
## Decision rules
- IF a metric is an average THEN add threshold/cohort variant (T1).
- IF defining an engagement metric THEN phrase as % doing ≥X by Y (T1).
- IF a proxy shows no correlation to engagement after ~2 quarters THEN replace (T1).
- IF execs rank engagement 2nd/3rd THEN don't call it the North Star; frame as high-level engagement metric (T1).
## Failure modes
- NSM confusion when execs don't prioritize it (T1).
- Average-driven metrics masking distribution failures (T1).
## Conditions
- Subscription/retention products with defined value moment (T1).
