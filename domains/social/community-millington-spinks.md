- ## Beliefs — Most communities are measured on activity, but activity ≠ value: activity-boosting tactics (controversial threads, games, quizzes) raise costs more than value. Only a tiny % of interactions generate value (excl. ad-supported communities). Purpose and under-investment, not tools, kill communities (Spinks/Jones agree).
- ## Principles
  - Value behaviors are specific and measurable; loyalty comes from three drivers: (1) perceived brand value vs competitors, (2) group identity/attachment, (3) switching costs.
  - The 6 behaviors that drive retention: ask questions about the product; read product/mission info; introduce yourself; participate in private discussions; reveal personal details; answer questions to build credibility.
  - Community lifecycle: purpose → audience → activity → moderation (Jones's lifecycle + moderation science).
- ## Frameworks — ROI-vs-activity model (FeverBee); 3 loyalty drivers; 6 value behaviors; community lifecycle (Jones/Spinks).
- ## Processes — define the business purpose → pick measurable value behaviors → design spaces/rituals that trigger them → moderate for safety + inclusion → report value, not activity.
- ## Heuristics — if a discussion doesn't map to a value behavior, it's cost; brand answering questions fast = loyalty input; members making friends = retention.
- ## Metrics — value behaviors per member/month, support deflection, retention lift of community members vs non-members, questions answered fast (SLA), % members with private connections.
- ## Decision rules
  - IF community exists without a stated business purpose THEN fix purpose before adding tools/features. (FRAMEWORK, T2)
  - IF you're reporting activity (posts/DAU) as success THEN switch metrics to value behaviors + business outcomes. (FRAMEWORK, T1 as FeverBee essay)
  - IF members' questions go unanswered THEN staff an answer SLA before growing membership. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF goal = retention THEN design for the 6 behaviors (ask/read/introduce/private/self-disclose/answer), not engagement volume. (FRAMEWORK, T1)
  - IF justifying budget THEN tie community to support deflection / retention lift, not "engagement". (FRAMEWORK, T2)
- ## Failure modes — activity theater; under-staffed moderation; unclear purpose; measuring outputs not outcomes; building community before demand exists.
- ## Conditions — works for B2B SaaS/product communities with support/retention goals; ad-supported or social-only communities are the exception.
- ## Limitations — T2 (his ROI data is illustrative, not published RCTs); Spinks/Jones claims synthesized from reputation, not fetched this session (T3 for their specifics).
