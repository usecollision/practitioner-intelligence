- ## Beliefs — YouTube is an attention-retention platform; the algorithm surfaces content that brings people back and holds them. Retention graphs are the primary diagnostic tool — top creators (60-100M views/month) study them intimately.
- ## Principles
  - Value isn't enough: teach the audience to FEEL something — channels win on feeling, not one-time DIY utility.
  - Consistent posting schedule trains YouTube's suggestion system ("come back on Tuesdays/Fridays").
  - Watch time (individual + collective) is the primary value metric; CTR gets the click, retention keeps the value.
  - Make the next video your viewer wants: analytics → what to make next.
- ## Frameworks — Audience-retention graph analysis; value proposition per channel ("why should viewers care when 500+ hours upload every minute"); Product-to-Profit audience-questioning method.
- ## Processes — know target audience (ask them) → clarify why viewers should care → brainstorm next 1-3 months of titles/thumbnails → publish on schedule → study analytics (views, watch time, CTR, impressions) → iterate.
- ## Heuristics — if retention drops at a specific moment, find the trigger (one client lost viewers every time he said "module" — removed it, graph flattened); intros decide early drop-off.
- ## Metrics — audience retention % (30s, 50%, end), watch time, CTR, impressions, suggested-traffic share, returning-viewer behavior.
- ## Decision rules
  - IF retention graph shows a repeated drop point THEN identify and cut the trigger element (word/segment). (EMPIRICAL, T2)
  - IF CTR is low but retention is fine THEN fix packaging (title/thumbnail), not content. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF channel lacks a consistent schedule THEN establish one before scaling production (suggestion-system training). (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF you don't know why viewers should care THEN answer that before producing more videos. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF analytics show a winning video type THEN make more like it; don't chase unrelated trends. (HEURISTIC, T2)
- ## Failure modes — producing for yourself not the audience; ignoring retention drop-offs; inconsistent publishing; treating shorts as a substitute for retention mechanics.
- ## Conditions — long-form educational channels; requires analytics access and iteration loops; his data is 2018-2020 era but retention mechanics persist.
- ## Limitations — T2; his claims about "top creators" are anecdotal; not strong on paid/shorts strategy.
