## Beliefs
- You cannot create desire for a product; you can only channel desire that already exists (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical — Breakthrough Advertising, 1966).
- Most copy fails because it describes the product rather than amplifying what the prospect already wants to believe (OPINION, T1-canonical).
- The headline's only job is to select the right reader and pull them into the next sentence — not to sell, not to impress (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).

## Principles
- The stage of market sophistication determines how you open your copy — not what you say about the product (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Awareness level dictates message structure: different entry points for never-heard vs six-competitors-seen prospects (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Copy fails for three recurring reasons: writing from inside the product, misjudging awareness level (closing argument for cold traffic), ignoring market sophistication (direct claim in saturated market) (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).

## Frameworks
- 5 Stages of Market Sophistication: (1) new market — direct claim works ("Lose 20 lbs in 30 days"); (2) competition appears — bigger/faster/more specific claims ("without giving up carbs"); (3) claims saturated — mechanism becomes the story (the "how"); (4) mechanisms exhausted — enlarge/name/proprietize the mechanism (branded systems, named frameworks); (5) total saturation — identification: talk about who the reader is, what they believe, what they're tired of hearing (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).
- 5 Levels of Customer Awareness: most aware → product aware → solution aware → problem aware → completely unaware; each needs a different entry point (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).
- Sophistication (market-level) vs Awareness (individual-level) are separate axes: sophistication tells you what kind of claim to make; awareness tells you where to start the conversation (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).

## Processes
- Identify the market's sophistication stage → identify the target prospect's awareness level → open copy calibrated to both → channel existing desire toward the product (PROCESS, T1-canonical).

## Heuristics
- Retargeting ad = most-aware/product-aware prospect; cold social ad = solution- or problem-aware at best; writing the same message for both is a category error (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).
- Stage 3+ means the "how" sells, not the outcome (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).
- Stage 5: stop talking about the product almost entirely (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).

## Tactics
- At stage 4: name your mechanism (branded frameworks = sophistication-stage-4 play, even in modern SaaS marketing) (TACTIC, T1-canonical).
- Longest copy for the completely unaware; shortest for the most aware (TACTIC, T1-canonical).

## Inputs
- Market sophistication assessment (how many times has this audience heard similar promises), prospect awareness segmentation (INPUT, T1-canonical).

## Outputs
- Calibrated copy structure per segment (OUTPUT, T1-canonical).

## Metrics
- Response (in his era: per-1000 response rates) (METRIC, T1-canonical).

## Decision rules
- If market is new (stage 1) → lead with the direct claim. If claims are everywhere (stage 3) → lead with mechanism. If mechanisms are everywhere (stage 4) → name and own the mechanism. If everything is exhausted (stage 5) → lead with identity/belief (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical — the single most decision-useful copy framework found in this research).
- If prospect is unaware → start with the problem; if product-aware → comparison/proof; if most-aware → price/offer/deadline (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical).

## Failure modes
- Direct claim in a saturated market: invisible (WARNING, T1-canonical).
- "Simple clear copy" advice fails in sophisticated markets — this is the direct rebuttal to the plain-language school (WARNING, T1-canonical).
- Writing for the wrong awareness level (cold traffic gets closing argument) (WARNING, T1-canonical).

## Contrarian beliefs
- The modern "be clear and simple" default is stage-1 logic applied to stage-3+ markets (OPINION, T1-canonical, as interpreted by the secondary source; the master map flags this as the single most underused concept in modern copy).

## Examples
- "Lose 20 lbs in 30 days" (stage 1) → "without giving up carbs" (stage 2) → "Japanese breathing technique that resets your metabolism" (stage 3) progression (EXAMPLE, T2 — from secondary summary of the book).

## Conditions
- Any market where claims have accumulated: supplements, SaaS, coaching, finance, real estate (CONDITION, T1-canonical).
- Least relevant in genuinely new categories (stage 1) (CONDITION, T1-canonical).
