## Beliefs
- Email marketing isn't dead: his evergreen follow-up sequences sustained a living for years without constant launches (OPINION, T2).
- Writing one email that works forever (an asset) beats daily broadcasts that die after one send (OPINION, T2).
- Story is the attention engine: soap-opera sequences keep subscribers eager for the next email (OPINION, T2).

## Principles
- Open loops create the need to read the next email (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Structure first: follow-up sequences with predefined gaps; then context; then behavior-based branching (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Empathy with the audience precedes persuasion (PRINCIPLE, T2 — podcast topics).
- Consistency beats talent: "you can do lots of things wrong and still do well... consistency wins" (PRINCIPLE, T2).

## Frameworks
- Soap Opera Sequence (SOP): story-based email sequence with open loops/cliffhangers across emails (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- Autoresponder Madness system: follow-up sequences + behavior sequences (clicks trigger sub-sequences down different paths) (FRAMEWORK, T2).

## Processes
- Map a multi-email story sequence with gaps → write each email to open a loop the next one closes → set behavior triggers (click → branch sequence) → let the system sell while you write the next asset (PROCESS, T2).

## Heuristics
- The PS section is valuable real estate (HEURISTIC, T2).
- Personal stories build the relationship that makes the pitch land (HEURISTIC, T2).
- 70%+ open rates achievable on a well-kept list with story-based sending (HEURISTIC, T2 — claimed in podcast intro; self-reported).

## Tactics
- Open loops: reference something coming later; don't resolve everything in one email (TACTIC, T2).
- Behavior-based segmentation: clicks route readers into context-appropriate sub-sequences (TACTIC, T2).
- Build evergreen assets instead of event-driven launches (TACTIC, T2).

## Tools
- ActiveCampaign (his chosen platform at relaunch), Drip (mentioned) (TOOL, T2).

## Inputs
- Audience empathy, story material, list behavior data (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Evergreen email sequences, autoresponder systems, courses (OUTPUT, T2).

## Metrics
- Open rate, click-through, conversion per sequence, list relationship (replies) (METRIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- If you want leverage → build evergreen follow-up sequences, not daily broadcasts (DECISION RULE, T2).
- If a reader's context differs → branch them into a different path rather than continuing the same sequence (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Story sequences without closure/branches (everyone gets the same path regardless of behavior) (WARNING, T2, inferred).
- Burning subscribers with constant launch-driven broadcasts (WARNING, T2, inferred).

## Contrarian beliefs
- You don't need constant launches: evergreen sequences + stories compound (OPINION, T2).

## Examples
- His own business run from Spain on evergreen funnels since 2003 (EXAMPLE, T2, self-reported).
- Autoresponder Madness used by thousands (EXAMPLE, T3, vendor claim).

## Conditions
- Info-products, courses, B2B nurture — any email business with a list that tolerates storytelling (CONDITION, T2).
- Needs consistent writing skill and story material (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- 2013-2015 era sources; current practice status unverified (LIMITATION, T3).
- Story style doesn't fit all verticals (e.g., transactional B2B support emails) (LIMITATION, T2, inferred).
