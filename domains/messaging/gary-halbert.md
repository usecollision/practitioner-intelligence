## Beliefs
- Be a "student of markets," not of products, techniques, or copywriting: the first and most important thing is learning what people WANT to buy (OPINION, T1 — The Boron Letters, Ch. 5).
- Deduce what people want to buy by observing what they DO buy — not what they SAY they buy (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- People put their money where their real desires are (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Principles
- List quality is destiny: recency, frequency, unit-of-sale are the three list-evaluation guidelines; recency is by far the most important (PRINCIPLE, T1 — "Direct Marketing to a Starving Crowd").
- Hot prospects: recent buyers, frequent buyers, high-ticket buyers of the same category (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Test results can lie — list owners rent their best names first; rollout ≠ test (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Recency/Frequency/Unit-of-Sale list evaluation (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- The Starving Crowd: sell to people already hungry for what you offer (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical, from his famous "pizza stand" framing — referenced, canonical).
- State-batch testing method to defeat cherry-picked test lists (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Find the starving crowd (who already buys this category) → test on a random sample → verify with a state-batched continuation (avoid the owner's best-names bias) → roll out only after verified (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- If test results are poor, forget the list — you just mailed their best names (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Rarer surnames outperformed common ones in name-history mailings (his wife's insight) — niche specificity pulls harder (HEURISTIC, T1, anecdotal).
- 30/60/90-day hotline buyers are the best names (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Order state-count continuations instead of "ninth-name random" rollouts to dodge list-owner bias (TACTIC, T1).
- Use SRDS list directory to size real markets before writing a word (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- SRDS Direct Mail List Book, list brokers, direct mail packages (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- List data (recency/frequency/unit of sale), market size evidence, offer (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Mailed packages, tested lists, rollouts (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Response rate per list, per package; rollout consistency vs test (METRIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- If the market isn't already buying the category (no starving crowd) → change the offer or the market before touching copy (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If rollout results crater after a great test → suspect list bias, not your package (DECISION RULE, T1).
- Choose list by recency first, then frequency, then unit of sale (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Testing on cherry-picked names and rolling out to the tail (WARNING, T1 — the $160k rollout story).
- Writing copy for markets where no one is buying yet (WARNING, T1, canonical).
- Listening to what people SAY they'll buy (WARNING, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- The offer and the list matter more than the copy; copy only channels existing hunger (OPINION, T1 — anticipates Hormozi's offer-first and Schwartz's desire-channeling).

## Examples
- The 5,000 → 50,000 → 445,000 name rollout disaster, and the state-batch fix (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Diamond buyers: 4,000 people × $5,000 = $20M market from SRDS (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Direct response with measurable lists; translates to email lists, retargeting audiences, lookalike pools today (CONDITION, T2, inferred).
- Less applicable to brand-building or inbound-only motions (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Mail-order era mechanics; list renting is less literal today (LIMITATION, T2).
- Aggressive persona; claims unmeasured (LIMITATION, T2).
