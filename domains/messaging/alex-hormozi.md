## Beliefs
- The offer is the highest-leverage variable in revenue — not closing skill, not lead volume (OPINION, T2).
- Market > Offer > Persuasion: a great closer in a bad market loses to a good offer in a good market (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- "Most things that are hard to sell are typically easy to fulfill" — flip problems into solutions (OPINION, T2).

## Principles
- If you sell a commodity, you compete on price; the goal is a "category of one" where the decision is between your offer and nothing (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Never discount the main offer — increase perceived value instead (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Price creates a virtuous cycle: higher price → more committed customers → better results → higher margins → better service (PRINCIPLE, T2).

## Frameworks
- Value Equation: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- Grand Slam Offer (5 steps): (1) identify dream outcome; (2) list obstacles before/during/after; (3) convert each obstacle into a solution; (4) define delivery vehicles via the Delivery Cube (1:1, small group, 1:many × client effort × medium × format); (5) trim costly low-value elements, stack high-value ones (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- 5 Offer Enhancements: Scarcity (capacity/cohort caps), Urgency (bonus deadlines), Bonuses (stacked), Guarantees (reverse risk), Naming (M-A-G-I-C: magnetic reason + avatar + goal + time interval + container word) (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- Success Hierarchy: Market → Offer → Persuasion (FRAMEWORK, T2).

## Processes
- Identify dream outcome → enumerate obstacles → convert to solutions → choose delivery vehicles → trim & stack → name the offer → add scarcity/urgency/bonuses/guarantees (PROCESS, T2).

## Heuristics
- Value approaches infinity as time-delay and effort approach zero (HEURISTIC, T2).
- Price comparison becomes impossible when you stack enough (HEURISTIC, T2).

## Tactics
- Bonus stacking instead of discounting (TACTIC, T2).
- Naming offers with the MAGIC formula (TACTIC, T2).
- Granular content distribution: his books free + podcast clips (TACTIC, T2 — his distribution system).

## Tools
- Books, podcast (The Game), Acquisition.com portfolio as lab (TOOL, T2).

## Inputs
- Dream outcome of the target, obstacle inventory, delivery capacity, cost structure (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Grand Slam offers, pricing structures, offer documentation (OUTPUT, T2).

## Metrics
- Revenue, margin, churn, customer commitment (METRIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- If you're in a commodity market → stop optimizing copy; rebuild the offer until you're a category of one (DECISION RULE, T2).
- If price resistance appears → add bonuses/guarantees/naming, never cut price (DECISION RULE, T2).
- If the market is bad (no pain/purchasing power) → change market before improving persuasion (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Competing on price in a commodity market (WARNING, T2).
- Discounting the main offer (WARNING, T2).
- Selling to a "dying or non-purchasing market" while polishing closing skills (WARNING, T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Persuasion/positioning work is downstream of the offer; most "marketing problems" are offer problems (OPINION, T2).

## Examples
- His gyms: from $1,036 in bank to $1.5M/month revenue in ~12 months via Grand Slam Offer (EXAMPLE, T2 — self-reported).
- Wine study: same wine tasted better at higher price (EXAMPLE, T2 — cited external study).

## Conditions
- Local services, gyms, coaching, SaaS — any business that can stack delivery (CONDITION, T2).
- Offer-stacking works best where the service is delivery-malleable (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- T3 guru-adjacent per master map: self-reported results, no independent verification, evangelical packaging (LIMITATION, T2).
- "Category of one" framing overlaps with Neumeier's onlyness but without brand craft (LIMITATION, T2).
