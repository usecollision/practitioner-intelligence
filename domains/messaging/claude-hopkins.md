## Beliefs
- Advertising is a science, not an art: "Almost any question can be answered cheaply, quickly and finally, by a test campaign... not by arguments around a table" (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical — Scientific Advertising, 1923).
- Advertising must appeal to thousands, so its mistakes are costly — testing is the only safety (OPINION, T1-canonical).
- Advertising is salesmanship in print (OPINION, T1-canonical, canonical).

## Principles
- Test-Measure-Refine (TMR) loop: losses from unsuccessful ads stay safe; gains from profitable ads multiply — "playing on the safe side of a hundred to one shot" (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Keyed coupons as the measurement instrument: track outcomes per ad variant (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Good advertising is brief, clear, and convincing; specificity beats puffery (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- The advertiser must study the consumer and give "reason-why" (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).

## Frameworks
- TMR Loop (Test-Measure-Refine) (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).
- Coupon-keying for attribution (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).

## Processes
- Formulate question → run a cheap test campaign → measure via keyed coupons → keep winners, kill losers → scale the proven (PROCESS, T1-canonical).

## Heuristics
- When there's a question about ad effectiveness, answer it by test, not debate (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).
- Keyed offers make every ad a measurement instrument (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).

## Tactics
- Free samples to demonstrate the product (his Pepsodent-era practice) (TACTIC, T1-canonical).
- Specific claims over superlatives (TACTIC, T1-canonical).

## Tools
- Print ads, coupons, key codes (era-appropriate: split testing, landing-page variants, UTM keys today) (TOOL, T1-canonical).

## Inputs
- Testable question, measurement mechanism (keyed coupon), budget for test (INPUT, T1-canonical).

## Outputs
- Tested, proven ads; measured response data (OUTPUT, T1-canonical).

## Metrics
- Response per ad (coupon returns), cost per sale (METRIC, T1-canonical).

## Decision rules
- If two copy directions are in dispute → test both cheaply; the data decides, not seniority (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical).
- If an ad can't be measured → it can't be improved scientifically; add a measurement mechanism first (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical).

## Failure modes
- Untested campaigns at scale ("hundred to one shot" played against you) (WARNING, T1-canonical).
- Arguments around a table instead of tests (WARNING, T1-canonical).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Creativity without measurement is gambling; this was radical in 1923 and remains the foundation of modern CRO (OPINION, T1-canonical).

## Examples
- Pepsodent campaigns via sample-driven testing (EXAMPLE, T1-canonical, from book history).

## Conditions
- Direct-response contexts with measurable response; any paid channel with tracking today (CONDITION, T1-canonical).
- Weak for brand advertising where response can't be directly measured (CONDITION, T2, inferred; this is exactly what Binet/Sharp later addressed).

## Limitations
- Pre-digital examples; brand-building effects not addressed (LIMITATION, T2).
- Some parts "misguided" even in 1923 per Ogilvy's introduction (LIMITATION, T2 — Ogilvy quote via Grossman).
