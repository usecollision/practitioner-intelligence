## Beliefs
- "I am helpless without research material — and the more 'motivational' the better" (OPINION, T1-canonical — his 11 rules letter).
- Nobody should be allowed to touch advertising until they've read Scientific Advertising seven times (OPINION, T1-canonical — his introduction to Hopkins).
- Research precedes writing: study every competitor ad from the past 20 years before writing (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).

## Principles
- Write the headline first, in volume: 20 alternative headlines per ad; select with others' input; split-run batteries when possible (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Define the problem and campaign purpose in writing; get client acceptance BEFORE writing copy (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Gather every fact and selling idea, organize, relate to research and copy platform, then write (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Fear of producing a lousy ad is productive: throw away the first 20 attempts (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).
- Write away from interruptions (home, not office) (PRINCIPLE, T1-canonical).

## Frameworks
- 11 Steps to Creating Good Copy (his letter): study precedents → gather research → define problem/purpose with client sign-off → list facts and selling ideas → write 20 headlines → get opinions/split-runs → write copy → rewrite (FRAMEWORK, T1-canonical).

## Processes
- Precedents study (20 years of competitor ads) → research (motivational) → problem definition + client sign-off → facts/selling ideas inventory → 20 headlines → consultation/split-run → draft → brutal rewrite (PROCESS, T1-canonical).

## Heuristics
- 20 headlines minimum per ad (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).
- If you're not terrified of producing something lousy, you're not working hard enough (HEURISTIC, T1-canonical).

## Tactics
- 104 revisions on a single Rolls-Royce headline (EXAMPLE of his revision intensity, T1-canonical).
- Split-run headline batteries via the research department (TACTIC, T1-canonical).

## Tools
- Research dept, split-run testing, client sign-off docs (TOOL, T1-canonical).

## Inputs
- Competitor ad archive, motivational research, problem definition, facts inventory (INPUT, T1-canonical).

## Outputs
- Headline batteries, copy platforms, tested ads (OUTPUT, T1-canonical).

## Metrics
- Split-run response; campaign purpose achievement (METRIC, T1-canonical).

## Decision rules
- If you haven't written 20 headlines → you haven't earned the right to pick one (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical, inferred).
- If the client hasn't signed off on problem definition → don't write copy (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical).
- If research is thin → go get research before writing (DECISION RULE, T1-canonical).

## Failure modes
- Writing before problem definition and research (WARNING, T1-canonical).
- Settling for the first headline (WARNING, T1-canonical).
- Interrupted, office-based writing (WARNING, T1-canonical).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Copywriting is mostly disciplined process and research — the "creative genius" myth is a lie (OPINION, T1-canonical).

## Examples
- Rolls-Royce "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise..." headline (EXAMPLE, T1-canonical).
- His letter to a young copywriter containing the 11 steps (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Any direct-response or brand campaign where research access exists (CONDITION, T1-canonical).
- The 20-headline discipline scales to modern ad variants and email subject-line testing (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Agency-era process; heavy by modern startup standards (LIMITATION, T2).
- "Motivational research" era methods are dated (LIMITATION, T2).
