## Beliefs
- Copy comes from research, not creativity: "research and discovery is everything... that's where your messages will come from" (OPINION, T1).
- If research isn't the biggest part of the work, 99% of the time you're doing it wrong (OPINION, T1).
- You cannot A/B test everything — "it's a beautiful dream" (OPINION, T1).
- The copywriter's job is listening, not writing: "you're there to listen, not to write" (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- Message-match: copy must match what buyers already believe and say; the message is found in voice-of-customer data (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Research is writing: time in research is writing time (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Write for "your one reader" — the ideal prospect (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- 3-Part Conversion Copywriting Process: (1) Research & discovery; (2) Writing, wireframing & editing; (3) Validation & experimentation (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Messaging hierarchy: the order messages go in — which messages must be seen first, which support them (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Research: listen to team, prospects, customers, ex-customers; collect voice-of-customer data; synthesize into docs and messaging hierarchy → Write: research open on one side of the screen, document on the other; identify important pieces (listening), identify the most engaging pieces, "slightly revise" them into copy; editing sweeps → Validate: five-second tests, observing where people get lost, experimentation where possible (PROCESS, T1).
- Steps for using VOC data: open research on one side → identify important pieces → identify most engaging → slightly revise into copy → editing sweeps (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- If research isn't the biggest phase, you're doing it wrong (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Don't pull full GA dumps when writing a sales page — ask for the one or two data points you need (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Five-second test: won't tell persuasion, but tells clarity — did people understand the headline (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Two-screen writing setup (research left, copy right) (TACTIC, T1).
- Tag/organize survey data in Google Sheets/Docs + Airstory for research (TACTIC, T1).
- Immersive "message finding" — get in character like an actor to authentically produce (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Typeform (surveys), Google Docs/Sheets, Airstory, fivesecondtest.com, Copyhackers academy/Copy School (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Voice-of-customer data: prospects, customers, ex-customers, sales team; messaging hierarchy inputs; the specific data points needed for the page (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Research synthesis docs, messaging hierarchy, conversion copy, validation results (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Clarity (five-second tests), conversion (where experiments run), message resonance (METRIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- If you're about to write before research → stop; research first (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If you can't A/B test → validate with five-second tests and observation; ship the best version for the one reader (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If a phrase from research makes you pay attention → use it, slightly revised (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Writing without research (99% of the time wrong) (WARNING, T1).
- Believing A/B testing is mandatory/always possible (WARNING, T1).
- Copy that describes the product instead of using buyer language (WARNING, T1, inferred).

## Contrarian beliefs
- "There's no such thing as too much research" — even when overwhelmed, keep going (OPINION, T1).
- A/B testing is not the default validation mechanism (OPINION, T1 — pushes back on CRO dogma).

## Examples
- Over-the-shoulder tutorial on the 3-part process (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Copyhackers mastermind anecdotes on research-first writing (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- B2B and B2C conversion copy; works whenever buyer language is accessible (support tickets, sales calls, surveys, reviews) (CONDITION, T1).
- Requires access to customers/ex-customers; weaker for brand-new categories with no existing language (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Method depends on research access; time-heavy; not designed for brand-voice or identity work (LIMITATION, T2, inferred).
