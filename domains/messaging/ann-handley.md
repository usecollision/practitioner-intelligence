## Beliefs
- Everybody writes — writing is not a specialist skill, it's a business skill (OPINION, T1).
- Good writing ≠ good grammar; it's thinking, rewriting, joy, and relentless reader focus (OPINION, T1 — Everybody Writes 2nd ed.).
- "We are all capable of producing good writing" — it's mindset + habit (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- The key to being a better writer is to be a more productive one: write (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Writing well = habit + fundamental rules + giving a damn (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- No one will ever complain that you made things too simple to understand; assume the reader knows nothing but don't assume they're stupid (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Buzzwords and jargon are "the chemical additives of writing" (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Great writers are excellent editors of their own work — the Ugly First Draft is part of the process (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- 13 Writing Rules (list of craft rules, see Principles) (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Write ugly first draft → edit for clarity (not just grammar) → ruthless self-editing (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- Editing is fixing clarity for your reader, not fixing grammar (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Deadlines are the WD-40 of writing (HEURISTIC, T1 — h/t Doug Kessler).
- "You can't rush art" is folly — you DO have to rush your art (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Keep reader focus relentlessly; cut jargon; edit aggressively (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Newsletter (fortnightly), books, MarketingProfs (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Reader understanding, draft, deadline (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Readable, human marketing writing; editorial standards (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- UNKNOWN (writing-quality field; no formal metrics published in sources checked) (METRIC, T3).

## Decision rules
- If a sentence uses jargon or buzzwords → cut it unless a reader would say it (DECISION RULE, T1, inferred).
- If you're stuck → write the ugly draft now; edit later (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Polished-but-empty prose (grammar-perfect, reader-empty) (WARNING, T1).
- Jargon/buzzword-laden corporate speak (WARNING, T1).
- Waiting for inspiration instead of writing (WARNING, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Grammar is secondary to thinking and clarity — pushes back on grammar-first writing instruction (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- Her own newsletter and books as the working example of human voice at scale (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- Any business writing; strongest for brand voice, blogs, email, social (CONDITION, T1).
- Not a conversion/DR methodology — complementary to, not a substitute for, conversion copy research (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Craft-level guidance; no research process, no testing, no offer logic (LIMITATION, T2).
