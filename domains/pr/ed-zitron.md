- ## Beliefs — Most PR fails because practitioners pitch from desperation and self-importance, not from what journalists need. Reporters are skimming; anything that adds reading friction kills the pitch. Enthusiasm doesn't sell — relevance does.
- ## Principles — Brevity is respect: a 450-word pitch about an app update is a non-starter. Plain text: no bold, no italics, no colors, no HTML-abuse signatures, no award-badge vanity. Kill exclamation marks and "revolutionary/uncanny" hype. One clear reason why THIS reporter should care.
- ## Frameworks — "Dobler-Dahmer theory of pitch length" (you'd read 450 words about something you love; a stranger's pitch gets skimmed); pitch = plain email that reads like a person.
- ## Processes — research the reporter's beat → write a short, plain, specific pitch → send individually → follow up once; never blast.
- ## Heuristics — if the pitch needs formatting to be understood, the writing is bad; if you can't say why this journalist in one sentence, don't send it.
- ## Metrics — reply rate, coverage rate per pitch, relationship persistence (reporters who come back).
- ## Decision rules
  - IF pitch >150 words THEN cut to ~100-150, one idea. (HEURISTIC, T1)
  - IF pitch contains bold/colors/logo-heavy signature THEN strip to plain text. (HEURISTIC, T1)
  - IF you can't articulate the journalist-specific angle in one sentence THEN don't send yet. (HEURISTIC, T1)
  - IF your instinct is to send to 100+ reporters THEN stop — individualization beats spray. (HEURISTIC, T1)
  - IF the news hook is weak THEN lead with the audience impact, not product features. (HEURISTIC, T2)
- ## Failure modes — mass blasts, hype language, formatting abuse, pitching irrelevant beats, follow-up spam.
- ## Conditions — consumer/tech media relations; his examples skew tech-startup; principles generalize to B2B trade press.
