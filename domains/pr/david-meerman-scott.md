- ## Beliefs — The traditional PR model (preset script + campaign timeline) is dead: "a single afternoon can blast the wheels off someone's laboriously crafted narrative." Real-time marketing/PR is the new power; anyone can newsjack, but only the observant, quick, and credible win.
- ## Principles — Newsjacking = injecting your ideas/angles into a breaking story in real time to get coverage; journalists researching a story find you if you publish the "credible second paragraph" fast. "Now doesn't mean tomorrow. It means now." Requires monitoring on two levels: your immediate sphere (trade press, known journalists) and the wider global sphere.
- ## Frameworks — Newsjacking action plan; second-paragraph content (blog post, tweet, media alert featuring the keyword of the moment); real-time marketing/PR.
- ## Processes — monitor both spheres continuously → when a story breaks with a legitimate tie → publish commentary within hours (ideally before the story is saturated) → journalists lift your content as they write.
- ## Heuristics — if the story is more than ~24h old, the newsjacking window has passed; if you have no legitimate tie, don't force it; speed to market must match crisis-communications speed.
- ## Metrics — coverage pickups from newsjacked pieces, inbound journalist inquiries, keyword-of-the-moment search visibility.
- ## Decision rules
  - IF a story breaks in your market and you have a legitimate tie THEN publish a credible take within hours. (FRAMEWORK, T1)
  - IF your org can't approve/publish within ~4h THEN build that lane before relying on newsjacking. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF no legitimate tie to the story THEN skip — credibility is the gate. (FRAMEWORK, T2)
  - IF story is >24h old THEN treat it as content, not newsjacking. (HEURISTIC, T2)
- ## Failure modes — slow internal approvals; newsjacking without a tie (tone-deaf); relying on press releases as the primary tool; long-lead campaign thinking in real-time situations.
- ## Conditions — works when you have a credible expert/executive + fast publishing; best for B2B/tech where commentary adds value.
- ## Limitations — T2: his examples are 2011-2015 era; platform mechanics have changed (X speed, AI citation), but the timing principle persists; book is from 2011.
