## Beliefs
- Positioning starts with competitive alternatives — "what would customers do if your offering didn't exist?" (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Competition is defined by the buyer, not your category map (T1).
## Principles
- Alternatives include status quo (do nothing), manual process, spreadsheet, incumbent — enterprise loses ~25% of deals to "no decision" (EMPIRICAL, T1).
- Phantom competitors (could compete, never appear in deals) weaken positioning — don't position against them (T1).
- Flow: alternatives → differentiated capabilities → value → best-fit segments → market category (FRAMEWORK, T1).
## Decision rules
- IF a competitor never appears in your deals THEN monitor only, don't position against (T1).
- IF a large % of deals end in no-decision THEN position against the status quo (T1).
- IF you can't say what customers would do without you THEN research alternatives before positioning (T1).
- IF choosing category THEN prefer existing category until you have money/momentum to re-draw it (T1).
## Failure modes
- Internet-research competitor lists instead of deal-based alternatives (T1).
- Inventing categories too early (T1).
- Positioning against phantom competitors (T1).
## Conditions
- Any stage; essential at repositioning moments and pre-PMF (T1).
