## Beliefs
- "The only person who can tell you willingness to pay is the actual customer" (T1).
- Data over gut feeling: pricing decisions from survey data + algorithms, not opinions (T1).
## Principles
- Revisit pricing every quarter — unchanged prices = years of lost revenue (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Do research, not A/B tests: need >30,000 users for a 10% conversion lift; customers find pricing A/B tests disingenuous (EMPIRICAL, T1).
- Survey 3 groups: current customers, prospects who know you, targets who never heard of you — each gives different WTP (brand raises WTP) (FRAMEWORK, T1).
## Frameworks
- 4-point WTP survey: "too expensive / getting expensive / a good deal / too cheap to trust the quality" — open-ended price answers, wide range (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Forced-choice feature-preference surveys (most/least important) beat scale ranking (TACTIC, T1).
- 4-step pricing framework: personas → WTP + feature preference data → align tiers to persona value → revisit quarterly (FRAMEWORK, T1).
## Heuristics
- No feature-preference differences between personas → undifferentiated pricing; differentiate on a value metric (HEURISTIC, T1).
- ~80% of SaaS companies discount ≥25% to acquire — discounting destroys measured WTP (EMPIRICAL, T1).
## Decision rules
- IF no WTP data THEN survey before any price change (T1).
- IF personas differ in preference THEN build tiers per persona/segment (T1).
- IF discounting >25% common THEN fix packaging instead (T1).
- IF price unchanged >1 year THEN review this quarter (T1).
## Failure modes
- Copying competitors' pricing (T2).
- Discount addiction → WTP erosion (EMPIRICAL, T1).
- Targeting customers who don't see value → LTV disappointment (T1).
## Conditions
- SaaS with customer base ≥20-30 per segment; panels for unknown targets (T2).
