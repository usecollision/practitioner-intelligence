## Beliefs
- Deliverability is largely within the marketer's control — inbox providers judge safety and relevance, and marketers can act on the seven factors that drive sender reputation (OPINION/FRAMEWORK).
- Subscriber lifecycle management (permission → onboarding → engagement → winback → re-permission) is the backbone of both revenue and deliverability (FRAMEWORK).
- Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP, 2021) "rewrote" deliverability best practices — opens are obscured, so engagement and permission strategies must change (FACT/EMPIRICAL).

## Principles
- Strengthen permissioning and onboarding: the moment of acquisition determines deliverability for months (PRINCIPLE).
- Qualify active mailable audiences; send to people who want mail, not just people who exist (PRINCIPLE).
- Design messaging to encourage clicks, not just opens (PRINCIPLE).
- Revamp reengagement programs and increase re-permissioning (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- Six stages of the subscriber lifecycle (signup → onboarding → active engagement → lapsing → winback → lost) with pages/emails/elements per stage (FRAMEWORK).
- Seven factors of sender reputation (his Litmus Live framework): content, engagement, infrastructure, authentication, list quality, complaints, bounces — with shifting weights over time (FRAMEWORK).
- MPP mitigation: 6-area playbook (permissioning/onboarding, active-audience qualification, click-encouraging messaging, reengagement, re-permissioning, MPP mitigation dashboard) (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Map each lifecycle stage's touchpoints → diagnose weakest stage → optimize that stage → measure subscriber retention & LTV (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- You can't fool mailbox providers or run away from a poor sender reputation (HEURISTIC).
- Most deliverability info online is outdated or flat-out wrong — benchmark against current provider behavior (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Build a deliverability/MPP mitigation dashboard to watch engagement changes over time (TACTIC).
- Re-permission campaigns before continued sending to stale segments (TACTIC).
- Use clicks (not opens) as the primary engagement signal post-MPP (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Litmus (testing/analytics), ESP platforms, Oracle CX Marketing tooling (historical) (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Subscriber lifecycle stage data, engagement history, permission source, inbox provider mix of audience (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Lifecycle strategy frameworks, deliverability audits, best-practice guides (Email Marketing Rules book, ~4,000 articles) (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Inbox placement rate, engagement rates (clicks primary post-MPP), subscriber retention, LTV per lifecycle stage, spam complaints, bounce rate (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If opens are unreliable (MPP audience) → optimize for clicks and conversions instead (DECISION RULE).
- If a segment is inactive past winback attempts → re-permission or prune, don't keep mailing (DECISION RULE).
- If deliverability degrades → audit the seven factors in weight order before touching copy (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Ignoring lifecycle stage: treating all subscribers as identical → engagement decay and reputation damage (FAILURE).
- Relying on open rates for engagement decisions post-MPP (FAILURE).
- "Seemingly wise" actions that make things worse (his 2026 warning — e.g., chasing AI-summary/branded-mail trends without strategy) (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- The 2021–2026 inbox wave (MPP, Hide My Email, Link Tracking Protection, AI Summaries, Branded Mail) is the biggest change in a decade — most marketers' playbooks are stale (OPINION).

## Examples
- MPP launch (fall 2021) case: entire industry's engagement visibility degraded; his playbook became the adaptation standard (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Works for any B2B/B2C email program with ESP + data access; lifecycle rigor matters most at scale (10k+ lists) (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Lifecycle optimization requires data infrastructure (behavior tracking, segmentation) small senders often lack (LIMITATIONS).
