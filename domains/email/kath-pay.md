## Beliefs
- Email marketing must be holistic: the email itself is only one step; what happens before (acquisition) and after (landing page, journey) determines success (FRAMEWORK).
- Psychology, behavioral science, and UX belong inside email strategy — not just creative and copy (OPINION).
- A beautifully designed email driving to a broken landing page earns nothing (PRINCIPLE).

## Principles
- Optimize the whole customer journey, not just the email: acquisition capture → email → click → landing page → conversion (PRINCIPLE).
- Lifecycle automation should map customer journeys and be tailored to each stage (PRINCIPLE).
- Focus on advanced strategy (lifecycle, automation, testing) rather than incremental creative tweaks for the biggest gains (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- Holistic Email Marketing (coined 2009): integrate psychology + behavioral science + UX across the customer journey, superseding "multi-channel"/"omni-channel" labels (FRAMEWORK).
- 3-step conversion journey within email: open → click → convert on landing page (FRAMEWORK).
- Cart-recovery view extended: capture more addresses upstream (checkout login barriers) before optimizing recovery emails downstream (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Audit the journey end-to-end: identify barriers (e.g., checkout login requirements, landing-page friction) → fix capture before optimizing send-side → then optimize emails per lifecycle stage (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- If conversions are flat despite good opens/clicks → the problem is after the click (landing page/journey) (HEURISTIC).
- Advanced-strategy improvements (lifecycle review) outweigh creative/copy tweaks at scale (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Remove checkout-login friction to capture more addresses (TACTIC).
- Map automated programs to customer journey stages rather than product features (TACTIC).
- A/B test as continuous optimization, not one-off (TACTIC).

## Tools
- ESP automation platforms, journey-mapping tools, A/B testing tooling (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Full-journey data (capture → email → click → landing → conversion), segment behavior, journey maps (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Strategy audits, lifecycle automation design, A/B testing programs, coaching (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Conversion per journey stage, cart recovery rate, capture rate upstream, email KPIs per lifecycle stage (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If click rate is fine but conversion lags → audit landing page before touching email (DECISION RULE).
- If a lifecycle stage underperforms → check upstream (capture/segmentation) and downstream (landing) before the email itself (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Optimizing emails in isolation from the journey (the "email silo" failure) (FAILURE).
- Chasing creative optimization while capture/journey friction remains (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- "Omnichannel" framing is insufficient; the holistic philosophy (psychology + behavior + UX) is the differentiator (OPINION).

## Examples
- Ecommerce cart-recovery case: recovery emails optimized, but biggest gains came from fixing the checkout-login barrier that lost 3%+ of potential captures upstream (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Retail/financial/travel transactional ecommerce and larger orgs with multi-stage journeys; needs journey analytics (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Holistic audits require cross-team data access (web analytics + ESP + CRM); philosophy is framework-heavy, decision-rule-light (LIMITATIONS).
