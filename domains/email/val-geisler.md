## Beliefs
- Onboarding is churn reduction: "reducing churn starts with onboarding" — strong onboarding does a huge bulk of retention work even without a long-term program (OPINION/FRAMEWORK).
- Email more often than you think you should — but make it personal and about them (PRINCIPLE).
- Readers fatigue if you ignore behavior: "If they're still on the appetizer, don't send the dessert course" (PRINCIPLE).

## Principles
- Flip the script: features → benefits; "You said you wanted to see this metric → we moved it to the top" beats "Look at this new dashboard" (PRINCIPLE).
- Welcome email must come from a person (founder/CEO), signed by a human, with a story — not "The Team" (PRINCIPLE).
- Talk to customers (JTBD interviews) to get their exact words; use their language in subject lines, body copy, CTAs (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- Dinner Party Strategy (6 steps): welcome/settle in → show around/drink → appetizers → meal → dessert → invitation to return; pace content like a host, not a shover of lasagna (FRAMEWORK).
- Onboarding = trial start → paid conversion (or money-back period); everything after payment is retention (FRAMEWORK).
- Jobs-to-be-done customer interviews: hone in on the 3 core tasks; interview customers, trial users, and churned users (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Map trial length → sequence steps (time-based drip) → layer behavior-based emails (doing well / doing nothing / ahead of schedule) → segment by behavior → end with conversion/winback (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- "It depends" is the honest answer to send frequency — depends on trial length and product complexity (HEURISTIC).
- If they haven't done the first step, don't keep offering more courses (HEURISTIC).
- Don't ask for the same action repeatedly; vary CTA by behavior state (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Behavior-based branching: moving-along, ahead-of-schedule, doing-nothing branches (TACTIC).
- Customer-interview verbatim language in subject lines and CTAs (TACTIC).
- Storytelling welcome from the founder (TACTIC).

## Tools
- ESPs (ConvertKit alumni), Customer.io (event-based), Trello, Baremetrics (analytics) (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Trial length, activation milestones, customer interview transcripts, behavior/segmentation data (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Onboarding sequence designs, email teardowns (blog), Dinner Party Strategy framework, swipe copy (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Trial→paid conversion, activation (first value milestone), churn by cohort, email engagement per sequence step, unsubscribes per step (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If a subscriber is inactive → switch branch to reactivation/benefit, not more features (DECISION RULE).
- If a sequence step has high drop-off → teardown that step's CTA/copy, not the whole sequence (DECISION RULE).
- If you don't know customers' 3 core jobs → interview before writing (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Shoving the whole product at people immediately (the "lasagna in the face" popup problem) (FAILURE).
- "Welcome to [Company]" subject lines — closed statements with no reason to open (FAILURE).
- Continuing to send advanced content to users stuck on step one (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Most companies don't email enough; the problem is relevance, not frequency (OPINION).

## Examples
- Onboarding teardowns (Mixmax, DeepDyve, Baremetrics, GrooveHQ) — public dissections that became the industry reference (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- SaaS with free trial or money-back window; any size, but needs event/behavior tracking to run branches (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Behavior-based branching needs tracking infrastructure; dinner-party pacing is vague on exact timing — must be derived from trial length (LIMITATIONS).
