## Beliefs
- Generic "best time to send" advice is wrong: your emails aren't all the same — newsletter vs offer vs announcement emails have different optimal times (OPINION, data-backed).
- Recipients consume email in a fixed order: from name → first half of subject line → preheader → headline → CTA button (FRAMEWORK).
- Telling people how long something takes works because it lowers perceived cost of engagement (EMPIRICAL — his data).

## Principles
- Separate email types into buckets and benchmark each bucket separately (PRINCIPLE).
- Don't send on the hour: >80% of all email sends on the hour; the first 10 minutes of each hour are flooded with promos (EMPIRICAL).
- Test everything — including "crazy" times (Thursday 8pm consumer offers, Sunday 2pm) (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- Read-order model: From → subject (first half) → preheader → headline → CTA (FRAMEWORK).
- Sender-type timing: early-riser industries (education, home services, hospitality) = 6-7am; midday warriors (healthcare, financial advisors) = 12-2pm; consumer = by generation, boomers earliest → Gen Z latest (FRAMEWORK, EMPIRICAL).

## Processes
- Bucket sends by type → benchmark own performance per bucket → test off-hour send times → optimize per industry/generation (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- Newsletters: Mon-Wed 5-8am, Thu-Sun 8-11am; offers: 10am-2pm general; Thursday 8pm consumer offers and Sunday 2pm offers are outperforming (EMPIRICAL — his data).
- Putting time-to-consume in subject line ("2 minute read", "3 minute demo") lifts opens ~28%; in preheader ~19% (EMPIRICAL — last 90 days of his data).
- Preheader is the second subject line — never waste it on "view in browser" (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Use your ESP's per-recipient optimal-send-time analytics if available — most marketers don't know they have it (TACTIC).
- Write subject lines with time commitment framing (TACTIC).

## Tools
- SubjectLine.com (subject line testing), OutMarket data, ESP send-time optimization features (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Email-type classification, industry of audience, age/generation mix, own historical send benchmarks (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Data reports on subject lines/send times, podcast + conference talks (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Open rate (per bucket), engagement by send time, subject-line score, reply/click for offers (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If send is a newsletter → morning (5-8am Mon-Wed); if offer → mid-day or tested off-hours; never the same time for both (DECISION RULE).
- If audience is early-riser industry → send 6-7am; financial/healthcare → 12-2pm (DECISION RULE).
- If you're sending on the hour → shift by 5-15 minutes for ~15% open uplift (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Applying one "best time" to all sends (FAILURE).
- Long subject lines / no time-commitment framing (FAILURE).
- Ignoring preheader (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Open rates are still highly actionable even in the MPP era — via subject-line and send-time levers (OPINION; he pushes back on "opens are dead").

## Examples
- 80%+ of email sent on the hour; off-hour sends lift opens ~15% (EMPIRICAL, his aggregate data) (EXAMPLE).
- Time-commitment subject lines: +28% opens (subject) / +19% (preheader) in last 90 days of his data (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Works for B2B and B2C bulk sends with volume data; individual-per-recipient send-time optimization requires ESP feature access (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Vendor data is aggregated and self-reported; open rates are directional post-MPP; timing findings shift — need periodic re-testing (LIMITATIONS).
