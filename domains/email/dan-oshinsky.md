## Beliefs
- "Email is an owned channel, but ownership belongs to your reader" (PRINCIPLE — rule #1 of 25).
- Most newsletters are built around what the sender wants to promote, not what the reader needs — that's the core mistake (OPINION).
- Launch fast, then make things better; "all the good stuff happens after the idea is out in the world" (PRINCIPLE).

## Principles
- Identify a clear audience; "everybody" is not an audience (PRINCIPLE).
- Understand the job of the newsletter: what does it do for the reader? Decide based on what best serves the audience (FRAMEWORK).
- Use AI for productivity, not creativity (PRINCIPLE).
- Every year, identify one new way to make money (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- 25 Rules for Running a Great Newsletter: audience clarity, job-to-be-done, launch fast, AI-for-productivity, go where others aren't, peak-newsletter categories, hyperscale-vs-hyperniche choice, one new revenue stream/year (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Define audience + job → launch minimal viable newsletter quickly → iterate on reader response → survey readers for feedback → grow via recommendation/forward loops (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- Four newsletter categories are at "peak newsletter" (crowded): don't launch into them without a differentiated angle (HEURISTIC — specific categories in his post).
- Pricing rule: chasing $1/mo crowd can lead to lower annual revenue than fewer subscribers at $5/mo — value-aligned pricing beats volume pricing (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Reader surveys as a feedback engine; use AI to sort through survey responses (TACTIC).
- Choose hyperscale (big-audience broad) OR hyperniche (small, passionate) deliberately (TACTIC).
- Find spaces where others aren't — "others move in packs" (TACTIC).

## Tools
- ESP/newsletter platforms, reader survey tools, AI for transcription/survey sorting (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Reader interviews/surveys, audience definition, category landscape scan (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Newsletter strategy and creative, growth planning, 25-rules canon (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Reader retention (open/click trends per issue), forward/referral rates, conversion to paid, survey response quality (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If you can't name the reader's job → don't launch yet; define it first (DECISION RULE).
- If your topic is in a peak-newsletter category → differentiate angle or pick another space (DECISION RULE).
- If revenue is the goal → add one new monetization method per year rather than raising frequency (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Building around sender's promotion goals instead of reader needs (FAILURE).
- Waiting too long to launch while polishing (FAILURE).
- "For everybody" positioning (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Growth is not the primary goal; reader ownership and job clarity are; monetization is a yearly add-on, not the launch driver (OPINION).

## Examples
- BuzzFeed's first newsletter program (2012) built from scratch without rules; the 25 rules emerged from a dozen years of trial and error (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Editorial/B2C/B2B newsletters alike; any size; works best when the operator can iterate quickly on reader feedback (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Creative/editorial judgment is hard to proceduralize; his rules are observational, not controlled experiments (LIMITATIONS).
