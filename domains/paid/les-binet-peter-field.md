# Les Binet & Peter Field

Effectiveness researchers; the empirical backbone of the brand-vs-activation budget debate. Their analyses of the IPA Effectiveness Databank (1,000+ campaigns, 30+ years, 700+ brands, 80+ categories) power the 60/40 rule, the long/short framework, and most of what Mark Ritson and the industry repeat.

## Beliefs
- Advertising has two distinct jobs — brand building (long-term, 3+ years, profit) and sales activation (short-term, <6 months, sales) — that work on different timescales and must both be funded (FACT/EMPIRICAL, *The Long and the Short of It*).
- Long-term brand investment delivers roughly double the profit of short-term-only approaches; investing in both delivers the highest returns (EMPIRICAL, Thinkbox summary of the report).
- "The most reliable way to boost efficiency is to increase activation — but activation needs brand building to work, so brands always need both" (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context* 2022).
- Short-term metrics (direct response rates) are not a good guide to long-term success; using them as primary performance measures has "dangerous implications" (EMPIRICAL, IPA).
- The 60/40 ratio is a *sweet spot in aggregate*, not a universal law — it varies by sector, category, brand stage, and channel mix (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context* + IPA "Three common mistakes" blog).

## Principles
- **Balance by difficulty, not by preference**: "When brand building is hard, you need to spend MORE of your budget on brand. When activation is less responsive, spend more on activation." Marketers instinctively do the opposite — turning to activation when brand building gets hard — which the data shows is a mistake that has destroyed value (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context*).
- **Balance varies by context**: all-context optimum is now 62:38 brand:activation (not 60:40). Peaks in Financial Services; lowest in Perishable Services (Travel). High-consideration/"high research" categories need *more* brand, not more activation (counterintuitive: rational categories still need brand, because brand is the hard job there) (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context*).
- **Online brands need a higher brand proportion than offline brands**: online brands in the databank ran ~56% brand (below the ~62% optimum); offline ~64% (slight overshoot) (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context*).
- **Reach beats loyalty targeting**: campaigns targeting the whole market achieve 3x as many large business effects as those targeting existing customers; new-customer campaigns report 60% more large sales effects in the first six months; tight audience targeting "does not help long-term success" (EMPIRICAL, *Long and the Short of It*).
- **Fame and emotion pay**: emotional advertising is ~2x as efficient as rational and delivers ~2x the profit; highly creative, famous campaigns outperform (EMPIRICAL, *Long and the Short of It*).
- **Share of voice matters**: SOV effects are bigger than ever; brands should balance brand and activation SOV (EMPIRICAL, 10 principles list).
- **TV is the most effective brand medium** and increases campaign efficiency ~6-fold (EMPIRICAL, Thinkbox; note: the 2022 context report shows channel mix shifting, treat TV-specific claims as era-dependent).
- There is **no universal best-practice spend ratio** — marketers have misinterpreted the work; ratio must be derived from context (FRAMEWORK, IPA blog 2024).

## Frameworks
- **Long vs Short framework** — campaigns classified by timescale of effect; brand = long (3+ yr), activation = short (<6 months); different creative, media, and metrics for each.
- **60/40 (now 62:38) brand:activation split** — aggregate optimum for balancing long/short spend; the starting point, then adjusted by context factors.
- **Effectiveness in Context factors** — seven context variables that modify the rules: nature of choice (consideration/emotion), how people buy (online/subscription), pricing (premium vs value), innovation, category life stage, brand life stage (launch years), sector (EMPIRICAL, 2022 report).
- **IPA 10 key principles** — support volume and price; build sales and saleability; talk to all prospects; balance head and heart; aim for fame; creativity increases efficiency; SOV matters more than ever; integrate brand and activation; balance brand and activation SOV; measure short AND long-term effects (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Analyze the databank by campaign input → outcome pairings; compare effectiveness across timescale, balance of brand/activation, targeting breadth, emotional vs rational, creativity, SOV.
- Derive "ready reckoner" adjustments so practitioners can modify rules for their own situation (Effectiveness in Context ends with a ready reckoner — i.e., a lookup table of context → ratio adjustment).

## Heuristics
- Start at 62:38 brand:activation for a typical established B2C brand; raise brand share for financial services, high-consideration, online-first, or where brand building is hard; lower it for travel/perishable services (EMPIRICAL).
- "When brand building is difficult, do not reach for sales activation" — that instinct is the documented mistake (EMPIRICAL).
- Budget "at least 60% of advertising budget in brand-building media" (EMPIRICAL, Thinkbox/Long and Short).

## Tactics
- Use mass-reach media for brand (whole-market targeting); use tight targeting only for activation (EMPIRICAL).
- Run brand and activation as distinct, integrated streams (integration of brand + activation is itself one of the 10 principles — activation works better when linked to brand assets).
- Measure short-term effects with response metrics AND long-term effects with brand/profit metrics — never one for the other (FRAMEWORK).

## Tools
- IPA Effectiveness Databank (their dataset); econometric/statistical analysis of campaign cases. No proprietary software.

## Inputs
- Category, sector, brand age/stage, online vs offline split, consideration level of purchase, emotional vs rational category, pricing strategy, innovation rate, category life stage, SOV relative to market (from *Effectiveness in Context* factor list).

## Outputs
- Research reports (Long and the Short of It 2013; Effectiveness in Context 2022; Media in Focus 2024); ratio guidance ("ready reckoner"); the 10 principles.

## Metrics
- Business effects size (sales, profit, market share, penetration), timescale of effect, efficiency (effect per spend), SOV, brand vs activation split of spend, emotional vs rational content share (EMPIRICAL set).

## Decision rules
- **Set the brand:activation split from context, not from the headline number**: if category is high-consideration/rational → increase brand share; if low-consideration/emotional → increase activation share; online business → increase brand share; sector benchmarks: Financial Services highest brand optimum, Travel/perishable lowest (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context*).
- **When in doubt between brand and activation, lean brand for any business that must survive beyond this quarter**: the documented dominant failure is under-investing in brand, not over-investing (EMPIRICAL, 2013 + 2022 reports).
- **Do not judge long-term work on short-term ROI**: short-term response metrics mislead on long-term effect (EMPIRICAL).
- **Target the whole market for brand; segment only for activation** (EMPIRICAL).

## Failure modes
- **Short-termism**: "widespread destruction of value" from marketers abandoning brand when brand building is difficult, and from 12-month performance cycles (EMPIRICAL, 2022 report).
- **Misreading 60/40 as universal**: IPA explicitly warns the ratio has been misinterpreted as a fixed law; applying it without context adjustments misallocates spend (EMPIRICAL, IPA blog).
- **Activation-only or activation-heavy budgets**: they can look efficient while eroding long-term profit; efficiency gains from activation require brand to be present (EMPIRICAL).
- **Loyalty marketing**: "no empirical justification for this in any context" (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context* finding 10).
- **Heavy targeting of existing customers** — misses the 3x whole-market effect (EMPIRICAL).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Rational/high-consideration categories need MORE brand, not more activation — directly against the common practitioner intuition that rational buyers should get rational, activation-heavy marketing (EMPIRICAL, *Effectiveness in Context*).
- Even online/digital-first businesses need a higher (not lower) brand share than offline ones (EMPIRICAL).

## Examples
- EasyJet: moved spend from sales activation toward brand building (cited approvingly in *Effectiveness in Context*) (EMPIRICAL).
- Online brands at ~56% brand share underperforming the ~62% optimum; offline brands overshooting at ~64% — the databank itself is the case base (EMPIRICAL).

## Conditions
- Their method works best for: established brands with multi-year horizons, markets where the IPA databank has representation (mostly B2C, UK-centric, TV-era heavy, larger budgets), decisions with a 3+ year payback tolerance.
- Their data does NOT cleanly cover: early-stage startups, pure performance businesses with <18 months runway, very small budgets where brand media minimums are unaffordable, some B2B niches (though Binet's B2B guidance exists: 46% brand per Ritson's summary of their data).

## Limitations
- IPA databank = award entries; self-selected, may over-represent successful campaigns (selection bias) (OPINION/UNVERIFIED — widely discussed critique, not confirmed in fetched sources).
- Correlational case-study analysis, not RCTs (UNVERIFIED as a claim about their method, standard critique).
- 60/40 aggregate hides massive sector variance; applying the aggregate without the context adjustments is a documented misuse (EMPIRICAL, IPA blog).
- Does not give a media-plan structure or channel-buying workflow — it is a budget-balance layer, not a media plan (FRAMEWORK gap).
