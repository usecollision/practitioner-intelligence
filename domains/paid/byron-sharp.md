# Byron Sharp

The evidence-based marketing counterweight to performance-only practice. *How Brands Grow* (2010) and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute's empirical laws (double jeopardy, NBD-Dirichlet, mental availability) define how advertising and media actually grow brands.

## Beliefs
- Brands grow primarily by increasing **penetration** (buying more people), not by increasing loyalty/frequency among current buyers (EMPIRICAL, *How Brands Grow*; "Answering critics").
- Advertising works through **memory**, not persuasion: it refreshes and occasionally builds memory structures that make the brand noticed/recalled in buying situations — most sales come from people who had NOT formed an intention (EMPIRICAL, "Does advertising only work via driving intentions and preference? No!").
- **Mental availability** — the probability a buyer notices, recognizes, or thinks of the brand in a buying situation — is the main advertising-relevant asset. It is NOT awareness (a-situation single-cue measures are flawed) and NOT attitude (EMPIRICAL, "Mental availability is not awareness").
- Growth requires reaching **category buyers broadly** — "If you want to grow you must increase penetration… you need to seriously reach out beyond 'loyalists', indeed beyond current customers" (EMPIRICAL, "Answering critics").
- "When budgets are limited (i.e. always) it's tempting to think small and go for low reach, but this isn't a recipe for growth, or even maintenance" (EMPIRICAL, "Answering critics").

## Principles
- **Double Jeopardy law**: smaller brands have both fewer buyers AND lower loyalty — the two penalties travel together. Because DJ is observed everywhere, real-world differentiation is mild; mental and physical availability explain far more of brand performance than differentiated appeals (EMPIRICAL, "What causes the Double Jeopardy law?").
- **Penetration is the growth lever**: for typical brands, growth targets require doubling/tripling quarterly penetration while lifting average purchase rate by a fraction of one purchase occasion (EMPIRICAL, "Answering critics").
- **Reach over frequency in media planning**: reach the largest possible proportion of category buyers; niche media cannot reach the whole target group alone — broad channels must be in the plan (EMPIRICAL, Ehrenberg-Bass media planning article).
- **Distinctiveness over differentiation**: distinctive brand assets (DBAs — logo, name, colors, cues) are what advertising should consistently communicate; campaigns that change look/feel each time fail to refresh memory structures (EMPIRICAL, "Does advertising only work…").
- **Advertising effectiveness ≠ intention/preference shift**: copy tests and evaluations based on intention shift are biased and misjudge sales effectiveness (EMPIRICAL).

## Frameworks
- **How Brands Grow laws** — double jeopardy, NBD-Dirichlet buying distribution, Ehrenberg's law of buying frequencies: buying propensities follow known distributions; most buyers are extremely light buyers (EMPIRICAL).
- **Mental availability + Physical (and digital) availability** — the two market-based assets that determine brand performance; growth = improving both across the whole market (EMPIRICAL).
- **Category Entry Points (CEPs)** — buying situations to which brands attach memory structures; basis for mental-availability measurement (FRAMEWORK, cited by IPA blog).
- **Distinctive Brand Assets (DBAs)** — the consistently used brand cues advertising must refresh (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Research-based process (how they operate as scientists): measure buying behavior distributions (NBD-Dirichlet), diagnose penetration vs frequency gaps, identify CEPs and DBAs, brief media to maximize reach of category buyers, brief creative to refresh DBA-linked memory structures, evaluate via mental-availability metrics — not short-term sales or attitude shifts.

## Heuristics
- "A typical brand hitting even modest sales/share targets requires doubling or tripling quarterly penetration" (EMPIRICAL).
- When planning media: include the broad channels the target group actually uses (e.g., "if your target group is on Instagram, be present on Instagram") — niche media only as supplements (EMPIRICAL, Ehrenberg-Bass media planning article).
- If you cannot afford the reach, you are not funding brand maintenance — low reach is not a scaled-down brand strategy (EMPIRICAL).

## Tactics
- Target category buyers (people who buy the category from ANY brand), not your current customers (EMPIRICAL).
- Use consistent DBAs across all advertising, including activation (FRAMEWORK/EMPIRICAL).
- For advertising evaluation: use mental-availability metrics / CEP benchmarks rather than awareness or attitude trackers (EMPIRICAL, IPA blog cross-reference).

## Tools
- Ehrenberg-Bass Institute datasets; NBD-Dirichlet model; mental availability measures (EMPIRICAL).

## Inputs
- Category buying frequency distributions, penetration vs share data, CEP research, DBA recognition data, media consumption of category buyers.

## Outputs
- Laws/principles, media planning guidance (reach objectives), advertising brief guidance, evaluation frameworks.

## Metrics
- Penetration, category buying rates, mental availability (via CEP-linked measures), DBA recognition, SOV needed vs share (EMPIRICAL set).

## Decision rules
- **Choose reach over frequency and over loyalty-targeting**: if a media plan targets current buyers or heavy users instead of maximizing category-buyer reach, reject it as a growth plan (EMPIRICAL).
- **When budgets are limited, concentrate on the broadest reach you can afford — do not go "niche/small"**: low-reach plans fail at growth AND maintenance (EMPIRICAL).
- **Spend to build mental + physical availability, not to shift attitudes**: if the creative brief is persuasive-benefit-led and drops DBAs, it is misallocated (EMPIRICAL).
- **Evaluate advertising on memory-structure refresh, not intention shift**: if your copy testing rewards intention-forming rational ads, your measurement is biased (EMPIRICAL).

## Failure modes
- **Loyalty marketing / CRM-heavy retention strategies as the growth engine**: loyalty programs don't create loyalty; retention strategies miss the penetration lever (EMPIRICAL, *How Brands Grow* ch. 11; "Answering critics").
- **Targeting current buyers**: "It's rare that it's a perceptual problem holding a brand back… the big impediment to growth is usually that most buyers seldom notice or think of our brand" (EMPIRICAL).
- **Low-reach, low-budget "efficiency" plans**: think-small media plans don't maintain or grow brands (EMPIRICAL).
- **Inconsistent creative** — every campaign different, "as if each were for a different brand" — fails to refresh memory structures (EMPIRICAL).
- **Evaluating ads on intention/preference shift** — systematically biases creative toward persuasive rational ads that underperform (EMPIRICAL).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Differentiation is massively overrated: "We wouldn't get Double Jeopardy if brands were highly differentiated… real-world differentiation is pretty mild. Mental and physical availability must be a much bigger story than differentiation" (EMPIRICAL).
- Attitudes and intentions are weakly motivational and unstable (~50% chance a person states the same attitude twice in two surveys); advertising that changes attitudes is not necessarily more effective (EMPIRICAL).
- Brand growth comes mostly from light and non-buyers — not from "making loyal customers more loyal" (EMPIRICAL).

## Examples
- Coke: built broad memory structures (beach, nightclubs, pizza, parties, red, swirl) that make it noticed/recalled across buying situations — the canonical mental-availability case (EMPIRICAL, cited in his essay).
- The DJ law observed across categories as evidence that availability >> differentiation (EMPIRICAL).

## Conditions
- Works for: established categories with stable buying habits, FMCG/retail/B2C, any business with a category where repeat purchase exists; also applied to B2B and services with adaptation (UNVERIFIED for B2B depth).
- Media-planning implications (broad reach) assume brand-building budgets exist; pure early-stage performance businesses with no category presence are partially outside the evidence base (OPINION).

## Limitations
- His laws describe steady-state markets; brand launches, category creation, and fast-changing digital categories are less covered (OPINION).
- Reach-maximization guidance is about brand/awareness media; he acknowledges direct-response advertising is "a very small amount" of advertising that works differently (EMPIRICAL, "Does advertising only work…").
- No guidance on the *size* of budget or the brand:activation ratio — Sharp is silent on the 60/40 question; his work is a reach/memory layer, not a budget-allocation layer (FRAMEWORK gap).
