# Mark Ritson

The translator of Binet/Field and Sharp into operational brand and media-planning practice. His two-speed model and 14-step marketing planning framework are the most concrete "how to build a media/marketing plan" procedures in public discourse.

## Beliefs
- "The most important word in 'The Long and the Short of It' is **and**. You need them both. Brand sets up the sale, activation/performance closes the sale" (FRAMEWORK, quoted in Marketing Week 2022).
- Long and short are so different they are "almost alien to each other": brand = sophisticated mass market, one long-running theme, years; activation = multiple target segments, many overlapping campaigns, product benefits, ROI metrics. Trying to do both in one execution ("double duty") is "not impossible, but highly unlikely to be as effective" (OPINION/EMPIRICAL — he cites Binet/Field comparisons favoring two-speed).
- Businesses are "trapped" in a 12-month cycle of short-term results; "You will get better ROI by dumping all of your money into performance marketing… [but] the long term is not simply the adding up of short-term ROI, and a great ironic move is costing our companies billions of dollars in lost revenue" (EMPIRICAL/OPINION, Marketing Week 2022).
- Stop trying to measure the ROI of long-term brand spend: "Let's stop measuring the long of it and brand building with stupidly complex, just ridiculous dollar estimates" (OPINION). ROI is "absolutely" the right measure for short-term performance marketing only.
- Segmentation is a map of the market (diagnosis), not strategy; targeting is strategy (FRAMEWORK, 14-steps article).

## Principles
- **Plan in 12-month increments**; agility is deviation from a plan, not a substitute for one (FRAMEWORK).
- **Marketing planning is a triptych**: diagnosis (data) → strategy → tactics; then back to diagnosis (circular; open by closing out last year's objectives) (FRAMEWORK).
- **Two-speed targeting**: whole market for long-term brand; multiple specific segments for short-term activation (FRAMEWORK).
- **Segmentation rules**: name segments by behavior, give each a population size, value, and your estimated share — nothing else; one slide; update sizes/shares yearly (FRAMEWORK).
- **Positioning = what / versus / is**, and the "is" must pass the three Cs test (FRAMEWORK).
- **Pointy objectives**: use funnel conversion rates vs competitors to isolate the weak step, then set one clear objective per target; tactics listed per target with costs (FRAMEWORK).

## Frameworks
- **Two-speed approach** — separate budgets, creative, media, and metrics for long-term brand (mass, emotional, distinctive-assets-linked) and short-term activation (segmented, product-benefit, ROI-measured) (FRAMEWORK).
- **14-step marketing plan** — 12-month increment; triptych structure; previous-year review; segmentation slide; targeting arms; positioning; objectives; tactics per target; hyperlinked evidence backup (FRAMEWORK, Marketing Week 2021).
- **Foolproof budget system** (Marketing Week, Oct 2022) — 10% of turnover to marketing; then find your brand:activation percent from the Binet/Field evidence ("Find your category, the age of your brand, how much is online, how much is offline? It's going to give you a percent"); hold it "for 10 years to properly work" (HEURISTIC).
- **Three axioms / brand strategy summary** — (1) set the long/short split; (2) decide if the long part targets the whole market; (3) choose which segments the short part targets — "You have yourself targeting clarity" (FRAMEWORK, Marketing Week 2021).

## Processes (the 14 steps, condensed)
1. Lock the 12-month planning period. 2. Open with last year's objectives vs results. 3. Diagnose with data. 4. Build/update the segmentation (map of market; behavior-named segments; size, value, share). 5. Choose targeting (start of strategy; selfish; two-speed). 6. Structure the plan as "arms" per target segment, returning to the segmentation slide between arms. 7. Write positioning per target (what / versus / is; three Cs test). 8. Set pointy funnel-based objectives per target. 9. List tactics per target with costs. 10. Link supporting evidence via hyperlinks. (Circular: next year, diagnose again.)

## Heuristics
- B2C optimum ≈ 62% brand / 38% activation; B2B ≈ 46% brand / 54% activation (from Binet/Field data, as summarized by Ritson) (EMPIRICAL).
- Marketing budget ≈ 10% of turnover (HEURISTIC — his "foolproof system" starting point).
- "If the customer would have ordered from you anyway without the call, don't target them" — sales teams love revisiting loyal customers, but it's non-incremental (HEURISTIC, 14-steps article).
- Beware plans that target all segments or rank 10 priorities; be proud of the segments you won't go after (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Separate brand and activation budgets and agency/brief structures (he is a self-declared "Zealot" against double-duty ads) (OPINION/FRAMEWORK).
- Use distinctive assets to link the two streams ("the codes conjured up in the brand work are then immediately activated in all the short promotions") (FRAMEWORK).
- For the brand stream: long-running single theme, mass media, emotional creative. For activation: multiple short campaigns, specific segments, product benefits, simple ROI (FRAMEWORK).

## Tools
- Segmentation data (behavioral), funnel conversion-rate benchmarks vs competitors, econometrics references (Binet/Field "ready reckoner" style percent lookup). No proprietary software.

## Inputs
- Last year's results, market data, up-to-date segmentation (segment size/value/share), funnel conversion rates by segment, category + brand age + online/offline mix (for the ratio), competitor targeting picture.

## Outputs
- The marketing/brand plan (annual); budget split recommendation (brand:activation %); targeting map; positioning statements; objectives; tactic lists with costs; media plan philosophy (mass vs segmented).

## Metrics
- For activation: simple ROI (cost vs revenue). For brand: brand metrics, NOT dollar ROI ("stop measuring the long of it with dollar estimates"). Long-term: whether the strategy worked (annual diagnosis loop) (FRAMEWORK).

## Decision rules
- **Split first, then target**: decide the long/short budget split before any channel decisions; then decide whole-market vs segments for the long part; then pick activation segments (FRAMEWORK).
- **Apply the Binet/Field percent for your context** (category, brand age, online/offline) and hold it for years — do not rebalance on quarterly ROI swings (HEURISTIC).
- **Brand stream: measure with brand metrics; activation stream: measure with ROI** — mixing them produces misallocation (FRAMEWORK).
- **Prefer two separate executions over hybrid ads** when budget allows (OPINION, evidence-cited).
- **Do not target customers who would buy anyway** — that spend is non-incremental (HEURISTIC).

## Failure modes
- **The 12-month performance trap**: optimizing to annual/quarterly ROI incentives starves brand and "costs billions in lost revenue" (EMPIRICAL/OPINION).
- **Double-duty advertising**: single executions trying to build brand and sell — statistically less effective than split approaches (EMPIRICAL via Binet/Field, as cited by Ritson).
- **Measuring brand with ROI estimates**: produces "ridiculous dollar estimates" and leads finance to defund brand (OPINION).
- **Segmentation narcissism**: building segmentation around your company instead of the market; or targeting everything (FRAMEWORK warnings).
- **Sales teams re-targeting loyal customers** — pleasant, easy, non-incremental (HEURISTIC).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Against "agility junkies": you need a 12-month plan; agility is deviation, not absence (OPINION).
- Against the Double-Duty Squad (e.g., senior marketers who insist one ad can build brand and sell): the odds are stacked against you (OPINION, evidence-cited).
- Against measuring brand ROI at all — in direct tension with the MMM/econometrics school (see Eric Seufert) (OPINION).

## Examples
- B2B: build brand to the whole market while sales/ABM activates specific accounts — his canonical two-speed example (FRAMEWORK).
- His own consulting: challenges clients to separate budgets and approach long/short differently (OPINION).

## Conditions
- Works for: companies with a real marketing budget (~10% of turnover), a category with a defined market, 3+ year horizons, B2C and B2B alike (his B2B two-speed account example).
- Requires: behavioral segmentation data; brand metrics infrastructure; leadership willing to hold a ratio for years.

## Limitations
- The 14-step plan presumes a marketing department and annual planning cadence; heavy for a 2-person startup (OPINION).
- The 10%-of-turnover and B2C/B2B percentages are summary heuristics from Binet/Field data — treat as starting points, not laws (HEURISTIC).
- No guidance on channel-level execution, creative production, or incrementality testing — planning layer, not ops layer (FRAMEWORK gap).
