- ## Beliefs — "Invest in the brief, not just the creator" (Moburst #1 UGC best practice). A great brief is the single most important factor in UGC campaign success; creators want clarity on non-negotiables vs freedom. Creator fit beats follower count: effective UGC comes from people whose personality/communication style feels genuine to the buyer (Moburst). UGC wins on authenticity, not production polish.
- ## Frameworks
  - **High-Converting UGC Brief** (ATTN, from 2,000+ creator relationships): 1–2 pages max; brand context (30s read) → audience → product facts + honest limitations → pain points → 2–3 angles with example hooks → reference videos (embedded/linked) → red flags/content to avoid → deliverables + specs → revision limits → compensation + usage rights → single point of contact. Both "make it authentic!" (vague) and 17-bullet requirement lists (restrictive) fail.
  - **Brief-word-count rule** (InfluenceFlow): 400–1,200 words, absorbable in <5 minutes; detailed briefs = 73% faster completions.
  - **Script vs freedom** (Pixis/InfluenceFlow): brief the angle and the emotional beat, let the creator find authentic phrasing; give 3–5 example phrases, never a full script — except where compliance/demo precision demands scripting.
  - **Creator-persona fit explanation** (InfluenceFlow): tell the creator WHY they were chosen — builds buy-in and calibrates performance.
- ## Heuristics
  - 5s of reference video communicates more than 200 words of description (InfluenceFlow).
  - "Don't use comparison language to competitors" beats "stay authentic" — specific guardrails > vague tone instructions (InfluenceFlow).
  - Revision allowances upfront: "2 rounds included; minor timing fixes free, major re-shoots count" (InfluenceFlow). One paid revision round is the agency norm (existing skill).
  - Platform lengths: TikTok 21–34s, Reels 21–60s, Shorts longer (InfluenceFlow, T3).
  - UGC with optimized hooks/formats on TikTok: +30% completion, +142% engagement, +43% conversions vs standard ads (Whop 2026 stats via Moburst — T3, vendor-sourced).
- ## Decision rules
  - IF running a UGC program THEN write the full brief (audience, objective, product + limitations, angles, refs, red flags, deliverables, rights, revisions) before sourcing creators (Moburst/ATTN — HEURISTIC, T2).
  - IF the deliverable needs authenticity THEN brief angle + beats + 3–5 example phrases, not a script (Pixis/InfluenceFlow — HEURISTIC, T2); IF compliance/demo-critical THEN provide the script and mandate claims rules (consensus — HEURISTIC, T3).
  - IF choosing creators THEN rank by persona fit + audience overlap, not follower count (Moburst — HEURISTIC, T2).
  - IF briefing THEN include usage rights, window, exclusivity, and revision terms in the brief itself (SideShift/InfluenceFlow — HEURISTIC, T2).
  - IF the brief is >2 pages or the read >5 min THEN cut it; compliance drops with complexity (ATTN/InfluenceFlow — HEURISTIC, T2/T3).
  - IF briefs get low-compliance footage THEN fix the brief before ordering more volume (existing skill QA gate + ATTN case — EMPIRICAL, T2).
  - IF measuring the program THEN use hook rate, CTR, CPA, completion vs control ads — not likes/views (Moburst — HEURISTIC, T2).
- ## Metrics — brief-to-approved-asset turnaround, compliance rate (QA pass %), hook rate/thumb-stop, CTR, CPA, ROAS vs control; program-level: % of winning creative traceable to a brief element.
- ## Failure modes — vague briefs ("be creative"), overloaded briefs, full scripts killing authenticity, no rights/revision terms → disputes + scope creep, creator-follower buying, one-off drops instead of systems (Moburst: systems beat one-time content drops), judging on vanity metrics.
- ## Conditions — DTC/ecom paid social on native surfaces (TikTok, Reels); works at any budget via nano-creators; needs QA pipeline to protect brand.
- ## Limitations — +340% (ATTN), 73% (InfluenceFlow), Whop stats (Moburst) are single-source/vendor figures (T3); Chappell's 3–5x ROAS TikTok claims are course-promotional (T3); platform algorithm shifts change length/spec norms.
