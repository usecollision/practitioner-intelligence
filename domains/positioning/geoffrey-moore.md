## Beliefs
- Positioning must be lifecycle-dependent: the same product needs different positioning for early adopters vs the early majority (OPINION, T1).
- Successful positioning = unique identity in the target market's mind + understanding competition + niche focus + compelling value proposition (OPINION, T1).
- Positioning is ongoing and dynamic, not a one-time artifact (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- Market sequence is the big decision in chasm strategy: focus on one beachhead, win it, then expand ("Act locally, then globally") (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- First accounts must succeed or they can never become references — reference-ability is the bridge across the chasm (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Don't let the skeptic block the sale; be prepared for the whole product (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Technology Adoption Life Cycle + the Chasm (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- D-Day analogy: concentrated attack on a single beachhead segment of the early majority (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Whole Product concept: the complete solution (support, services, complements) beyond the core technology (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Zone to Win: four-zone operating model (Performance, Productivity, Incubation, Transformation) (FRAMEWORK, T2, from his consulting page).
- Positioning statement discipline for lifecycle-dependent strategy (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Pick beachhead → assemble whole product → position for easy comparison vs alternatives → build reference customers → expand to adjacent segments (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- Target segment selection: small, well-defined slice of early majority with a compelling need (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Make it easy for prospects to compare you with alternatives — reduces perceived risk of adoption (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Early majority buys "safe," not "best" (HEURISTIC, T1, canonical).

## Tactics
- Executive strategy workshops; QBR participation as external counsel; thought-leadership position papers to develop markets (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Workshops, position papers, QBRs (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Adoption lifecycle position (which segment you're in), competitive set, segment needs, whole-product gaps (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Positioning statements, beachhead selection, whole-product roadmap, zone strategy (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Reference-customer creation; segment dominance before expansion (METRIC, T1, inferred).

## Decision rules
- If you haven't crossed the chasm → single beachhead, whole product, referenceable customers — not broad messaging (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If first accounts don't purchase and succeed → stop scaling, fix adoption risk (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If positioning must evolve → re-position per lifecycle stage, don't freeze (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Skipping the beachhead and going broad before references exist (WARNING, T1).
- Selling the technology instead of the whole product (WARNING, T1).
- Positioning for early adopters when trying to reach the pragmatist majority (WARNING, T1, canonical).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Positioning isn't just messaging — it's a market-entry sequencing strategy (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- D-Day as the canonical market-entry metaphor (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Tornado/Inside the Tornado for post-chasm category lifecycle (EXAMPLE, T2, referenced).

## Conditions
- Technology products with a real adoption gap; enterprise/tech B2B (CONDITION, T1).
- Less applicable to consumer products with viral distribution (no chasm) (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Model assumes segmentable markets and reference-driven buying; newer SaaS/PLG motions challenge parts of it (LIMITATION, T2).
- Little on copy/messaging mechanics — strategic layer only (LIMITATION, T2).
