## Beliefs
- Positioning in B2B software is complicated because software is "amorphous" — a malleable tool that looks different depending on who uses it (OPINION, T2).
- But the core is simple: answer four questions (OPINION, T2 — LinkedIn essay, 2026-07).
- Narrative beats feature lists for PMM: people don't buy feature lists; they buy a story about what the product is for (OPINION, T2, from his book/essays as referenced by master map).

## Principles
- "What is it?" determines everything: naming the product category OR the leading use case answers "who is it for?" implicitly (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Competitive alternatives follow from the "what is it" answer: category → other vendors; use case → other tools/methods/processes the segment uses today (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Clarity beats buzzwords: vague language is the failure mode of B2B positioning (PRINCIPLE, T2).

## Frameworks
- 4 Questions: (1) What is your product? (category OR leading use case); (2) Who is it for?; (3) What does it replace?; (4) Why are you better? (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- Product Category vs Use Case decision as the master fork (FRAMEWORK, T2).

## Processes
- Answer Q1 via category name or use case; derive Q2 from Q1; identify alternatives from Q1's frame; then state differentiation (features+capabilities+benefits) (PROCESS, T2).

## Heuristics
- If you state the use case, the audience narrows itself (e.g., "building with Notion" → Notion builders) (HEURISTIC, T2).
- Community add-on observed in the thread: add "Why now?" — without a trigger to switch, clarity doesn't convert (HEURISTIC, T3 — from commenter on his post, not Pierri himself; tag accordingly).

## Tactics
- Position around the category name to inherit market context, or around the use case to escape incumbent-category comparisons (TACTIC, T2, inferred).

## Decision rules
- If you want to inherit an existing market's context → name the category; if you want to escape incumbent comparisons and differentiate on scenario → name the use case (DECISION RULE, T2, inferred from framework logic).
- If no urgency/trigger to switch exists, positioning clarity alone won't sell — pair with a "why now" (DECISION RULE, T3, community-sourced).

## Failure modes
- Vague, buzzword-y positioning when the product is amorphous (WARNING, T2).
- Positioning before you know whether you're a category play or use-case play (WARNING, T2, inferred).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Positioning can be done pre-PMF for early-stage products — it's simpler than the industry makes it (OPINION, T2; master map notes "how to position without PMF" as his angle).
