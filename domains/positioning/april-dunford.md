## Beliefs
- Positioning is a choice made against competitive alternatives — not a description of your product (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- "Competitive alternatives" are broader than direct competitors but narrower than imagined threats: only what actually shows up on customer shortlists right now (OPINION/FRAMEWORK, T1 — podcast ep "Competition in a Positioning Exercise", 2026-02).
- Starting a positioning exercise from "the problem" produces vague, misleading inputs; start from competitive alternatives (OPINION, T1).
- Any product can be positioned in a dozen different markets; there is never only one market frame of reference (OPINION, T1 — "A Product Positioning Exercise").
- JTBD thinking reshaped her methodology — what customers compare is a jobs-to-be-done frame, not your vendor list (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- Positioning is a team alignment problem, not a solo creative act — the deliverable is consensus (TACTIC, T1).
- "The steps are easy but the work is not" — 5-step method, judgment-heavy (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- AI tools (ChatGPT) cannot tell you who your real competitors are; sales teams are the most reliable source (OPINION, T1).
- Features ≠ value: uniqueness can be a "fault" (e.g., complexity) that still wins deals in the right context (OPINION, T1).

## Frameworks
- Market Options method: 5 steps — (1) competitive alternatives, (2) unique attributes, (3) value of those attributes, (4) who cares most ("best fit" prospect), (5) choose the market frame of reference that makes your value obvious (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Sales Narrative / pitch structure derived from positioning components (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Facilitated 3-day executive workshop (flagship): works through the 5 components, then translates positioning into a sales pitch (PROCESS, T1).
- Half-day positioning course for larger marketing/product teams (PROCESS, T1).
- Special engagements: positioning after acquisitions/divestitures, market shifts, new strong entrants (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- When marketing message tells a different story than the sales pitch → positioning is broken (HEURISTIC, T1).
- When you keep losing to competitors with inferior products → positioning problem, not product problem (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Sales team knowledge of real shortlists beats exec intuition (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Market frame determines prospect assumptions about value, competitors, features, even pricing (HEURISTIC, T1 — database-vs-BI example).

## Tactics
- List everything unique without judging whether uniqueness is plus or minus; evaluate later (TACTIC, T1).
- For "who cares": don't stop at industry/size — name the specific characteristics of the customers who love you most (TACTIC, T1).
- Choose the frame where you're an AWESOME player in a segment, not a lousy player in a big market ("lousy database, awesome BI tool for machine-generated data") (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Facilitated workshops (in-person/virtual), whiteboard exercises (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Customer shortlists (who you actually lose to/win against); feature inventory; customer evidence of value; target-prospect characteristics; exec alignment (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Component positioning + sales narrative/pitch; team alignment; positioning statement inputs (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Deal win rate vs. inferior competitors; sales/marketing story consistency; internal alignment (METRIC, HEURISTIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- Choose the market frame that makes your strongest unique value obvious to your best-fit segment — even if it's not the market you started in (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If your sales pitch and marketing tell different stories → redo positioning before touching copy (DECISION RULE, T1).
- Start with competitive alternatives; only move on when the shortlist reality is agreed (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Starting from "the problem" → vague positioning (WARNING, T1).
- Staying in the market you started with out of habit (WARNING, T1).
- Teams each skew the competitive picture differently (product/marketing/founders) — unmanaged, this blocks everything downstream (WARNING, T1).
- Using AI tools to identify competitors (WARNING, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Positioning is NOT about "problem statement" templates; it's a market choice (OPINION, T1).
- Uniqueness can be negative-sounding (complexity) and still be the position (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- IBM database product: "database" frame lost to Oracle; "BI for machine-generated data" frame won deals (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Milkshake/JTBD story as the comparison-frame lesson (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- Works best with an opinionated exec team that can't agree on why customers choose them; Series A → global tech giants (CONDITION, T1).
- Workshop fit requires discussion — not for every startup (CONDITION, T1).

## Limitations
- 3-day workshop is expensive/heavy for tiny teams (LIMITATION, T2, inferred).
- Method assumes customer shortlist data exists; pre-PMF companies may lack it (LIMITATION, T2, inferred).
