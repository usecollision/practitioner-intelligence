## Beliefs
- Categories are real and matter — but "category design" as a deliberate act is mostly bunk: categories emerge from a "rugby scrum" of technology, customers, vendors, partners, standards bodies, investors, analysts, and media (OPINION, T1 — "Why I Believe in Categories, Just Not Category Design", 2026-08-13).
- Inferring a designer from an evolved market is like intelligent design for software (OPINION, T1).
- Classic category creation (pre-2010s): find a problem, build software, assign a TLA ending in "M" (management), convince analysts — that worked in a smaller Valley; today's kingmaking is different (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- Never position as "the next [leader]" — it guarantees you won't be (PRINCIPLE, T1 — "The Powerset Launch", 2007).
- Position on the actual job/buying frame, not the fashionable technology angle ("it's not natural language search, it's database query") (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- "Say your name" test (referenced in master map as his positioning reality check) — FRAMEWORK, T2, not directly verified this session.
- The rugby-scrum model of category emergence (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes

- "The next X" positioning → certain failure (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Mature category on a beach = evolved, not designed — treat "category design" claims with suspicion (HEURISTIC, T1).
- SaaS as "one-success category" for the Salesforce-next claim (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- If your positioning is "the next [big name]" → change it; you will not be the next anything (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If a competitor's positioning rests on a fashionable tech angle but the buyer's job is different → position on the buyer's job (Powerset/BI lesson) (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If someone is selling you category design → ask whether you can influence the whole rugby scrum; if you can't fund the education, stay in an existing category (DECISION RULE, T1, inferred).

## Failure modes
- "Next X" positioning (object DBMS as next Oracle; SaaS as next Salesforce) (WARNING, T1).
- Category-design hype: spending on designing a category you don't have the muscle to own (WARNING, T1).
- Confusing the analyst-friendly TLA game with real category leadership (WARNING, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Category design's core premise (you can design a category) is wrong — you can influence emergence, not design it (OPINION, T1 — direct counter to Lochhead/Play Bigger).

## Examples
- Powerset "next Google" launch failure; DataTalker/NLI natural-language BI history (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Object DBMS vendors as "next Oracle" (EXAMPLE, T1).

- His skepticism applies most to late/enterprise markets; may be less applicable to truly new zero-market products (CONDITION, T2, inferred).
