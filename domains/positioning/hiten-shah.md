## Beliefs
- Product/market fit is the #1 topic for early startups; positioning work without fit is premature (OPINION, T1 — PMF thread).
- Market size matters first: investors are right to demand large markets; small opportunities decrease success odds from the start (OPINION, T1 — "The Three Product Rules", from dogo/Draftsend failures).
- You'll never make everyone happy — stop trying (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- Measure fit multiple ways: word of mouth/referrals, retention (quant), surveys (qual), unit economics (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Customers' own words are the positioning/copy source: "very disappointed" segment language goes straight into marketing copy (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Sean Ellis PMF Survey applied: (1) "How would you feel if you could no longer use X?" — ≥40% "very disappointed" = fit; (2) open-ended "why" segmented by fit; (3) "What would you use as an alternative?" — reveals the market frame customers actually use (may differ from your belief); (4) "Primary benefit received" — exact benefit language; (5) "What type of person benefits most?" — validates/refutes your ICP; (6) "Have you recommended / how did you describe it?" — referral language for ads; (7) "How can we improve?" (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Three Product Rules: market size, [problem size], [execution] (FRAMEWORK, T1, partial).

## Processes
- Run PMF survey; segment by "very disappointed" vs rest; mine open-ended fields for positioning and copy; iterate product priorities from the segmented feedback (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- 40% "very disappointed" threshold for PMF (HEURISTIC, T1 — Ellis's rule, endorsed by Shah).
- Alternatives question reveals your REAL market frame — the products you consider competitors ≠ what customers think (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Use survey responses to write ads and website copy verbatim (TACTIC, T1).
- Analyze popular products with the survey publicly (Tradeoffs Show with Patrick McKenzie) (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Typeform surveys, FYI as his live example product (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Customer base to survey, retention data, referral behavior (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- PMF diagnosis; customer-language bank; ICP validation (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- % very disappointed; retention; referrals; unit economics (METRIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- If <40% very disappointed → product/market fit work before positioning work (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If the "alternative" answers don't match your assumed competitors → reposition to the frame customers actually use (DECISION RULE, T1, inferred).
- If market is small (hundreds of thousands potential) → reconsider the business before polishing positioning (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Building products in small markets (dogo/Draftsend lessons) (WARNING, T1).
- Assuming your competitor set matches customers' (WARNING, T1).
- Treating positioning as creative work instead of mining customer language (WARNING, T1, inferred).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Positioning is downstream of PMF; most "positioning problems" are fit problems (OPINION, T1, inferred).

## Examples
- FYI as the live worked example of the PMF survey (EXAMPLE, T1).
- dogo and Draftsend failures → Three Product Rules (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Early-stage SaaS with an existing (even small) customer base to survey (CONDITION, T1).
- Works pre- and post-fit; less useful for enterprise companies with long sales cycles and few customers (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Survey-based; assumes customers will answer (respondent bias) (LIMITATION, T2).
- Not a positioning methodology per se — it's the evidence layer beneath positioning (LIMITATION, T2).
