## Beliefs
- Three things make a legendary company: legendary business model, legendary products, legendary category — if you don't believe category creation is one of the most important things a company does, "there is no basis for further discussion" (OPINION, T2 — Category Pirates podcast #180).
- The problem is the proxy for the category: the unsolved problem you see in the world is the strategic element to anchor on (OPINION, T2).
- Category kings capture ~76% of category economics; the rest fight over 24% (OPINION, T2 — Play Bigger; from Geroski's research framing).
- Disruption should never be a goal; it's a by-product of category creation (OPINION, T2 — Play Bigger excerpt).

## Principles
- Frame, name, and claim the problem: language is the category's container (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- A unique POV changes the premise of an existing market (Airbnb changed the premise of hotels; Uber of cabs) (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- The company that evangelizes the POV is immediately viewed as the leader (PRINCIPLE, T2).
- Mobilize the whole company — and ideally an industry — around the category problem (PRINCIPLE, T2).

## Frameworks
- Category lifecycle (after Geroski): Define (companies swarm) → Develop (king emerges) → Dominate (king holds 76%) (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- Category Design steps: (1) understand/articulate the problem; (2) name the category (market type + nature of problem + audience); (3) blueprint/ecosystem vision; (4) Point of View (frames problem, ramifications, vision, your unique answer); (5) mobilization; (6) Lightning Strike execution; (7) ongoing Strike Ops / category flywheel (FRAMEWORK, T2).
- POV architecture: "Legendary POVs have a simple architecture" (FRAMEWORK, T2, details not fetched).
- Problem Awareness Curve (referenced in master map; not directly verified this session) (FRAMEWORK, T3).

## Processes
- Deep-dive to discover the category (problem-defined or tech-defined) → build POV → evangelize internally → generate Lightning Strikes (standalone events, conference hijacks, blow-out launches) → de-position previous categories → Strike Ops continuous capability (PROCESS, T2).

## Heuristics
- The POV is the script/soundbites customers use in word-of-mouth marketing (HEURISTIC, T2).
- 76/24: design for the king's share, not the better-mousetrap share (HEURISTIC, T2).
- If you can't articulate the problem, value gets diluted with coworkers, investors, customers (HEURISTIC, T2).

## Tactics
- Lightning strikes targeting cognitive biases of the whole category ecosystem (anchoring, group-think, conformity, commitment) (TACTIC, T2).
- Naming the category as a container for the problem (TACTIC, T2).
- De-position the old category (e.g., Salesforce emasculating on-prem CRM) (TACTIC, T2).

## Tools
- Category Pirates podcast/newsletter; Play Bigger playbook; Strikes playbook (TOOL, T2).

## Inputs
- Deep market insight; belief in category design (his stated precondition); problem articulation (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Category name, POV, blueprint, strike calendar, company-wide mobilization (OUTPUT, T2).

## Metrics
- Share of category economics (76% goal); thought-leadership position; word-of-mouth adoption of your language (METRIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- If you won't take authorship/ownership of the category → don't attempt category design (DECISION RULE, T2).
- If you can't fund sustained strikes → category design will fail (DECISION RULE, T2, inferred; master-map consensus).
- If content with fighting for the smaller share → traditional strategy suffices (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Companies struggle to articulate their problem → diluted value, no premium, no investor multiple (WARNING, T2).
- "Build it and they will come" — the opposite of category design (WARNING, T2).
- Category design without company-wide mobilization (WARNING, T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Better/faster/cheaper competition is the loser's game; the winner designs the category (OPINION, T2).
- Directly opposed by Dave Kellogg: categories emerge from a rugby scrum; they are not designed (OPINION, T2 — see kellogg.md).

## Examples
- Airbnb (hotel lawsuits), Uber (cab protests) as POV-premise changes (EXAMPLE, T2).
- Salesforce as category king of cloud CRM (EXAMPLE, T2).
- iPad as a created category between phone and laptop (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- New or re-framable problems; companies with resources for sustained strikes; founder/CEO willingness to lead (CONDITION, T2).
- Weakest when the market is mature, regulated, or when the company can't fund education (CONDITION, T2; Kellogg's critique).

## Limitations
- Evidence is self-referential (the authors sell the method) — treat claims as practitioner heuristic, not data (LIMITATION, master-map guidance).
- No public failure cases of the method (survivorship bias) (LIMITATION, T2).
