## Beliefs
- In an age of me-too products, keeping up with competitors is a losing strategy; radical differentiation is the only strategy (OPINION, T1 — Zag).
- "Onlyness" is the most powerful test of a strategic position: if you don't stand out, you lose (OPINION, T1).
- You can't advertise your way to onlyness — you have to start with it (OPINION, T1).

## Principles
- A zag is RADICAL differentiation, not mere differentiation (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- It's not what YOU say but what THEY say that counts — positioning lives in customer perception (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- If you can't say why your brand is both different and compelling in a few words: don't fix your positioning statement, fix your company (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Onliness statement: "Our brand is the ONLY [category] that [zag]" (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Trueline: the one true thing you can say about your brand, derived from the onliness statement; competitors can't claim it (or won't); customers find it valuable and credible (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Zag process: 17 checkpoints (from the book) — e.g., Checkpoint 6 (Onlyness), Checkpoint 11 (Trueline) (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Liquid agency takes clients through steps to discover what makes them "the only" — a journey to the core of the business (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- If you can't complete "only ______ that ______" briefly, you don't have a zag — list competitors who could make the same claim and shift strategy away from theirs (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Brand communications emanate from the trueline (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Write the onliness sentence; test every claim against "could a competitor claim this?" (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- The Zag book's checkpoint system; Liquid Agency workshops (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Category knowledge, competitor claims inventory, customer-value evidence (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Onliness statement, trueline, differentiated brand position (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Onlyness test pass/fail; claim-ownership (can competitors claim it?) (METRIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- If a competitor could make the same claim → not a position; shift (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If the statement can't be brief + include ONLY → no zag yet; keep working or fix the company (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Me-too positioning ("better, cheaper, faster" in an undifferentiated market) (WARNING, T1).
- Advertising before onlyness exists (WARNING, T1).
- Positioning statements nobody inside the company can prove or use (WARNING, T1, inferred).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Differentiation is not a nice-to-have; "keeping up" actively loses (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- Southwest Airlines trueline example (what people might say about Southwest: low fares anywhere you can drive, fun experience) (EXAMPLE, T1).
- His bank's generic tagline poster as a counter-example of non-zag (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Consumer and B2B brands; most powerful when the category is crowded and undifferentiated (CONDITION, T1).
- Works at brand level; less specific about product-market mechanics or enterprise buying frames (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- Brand-craft orientation: low operational detail on research, workshops, or evidence requirements (LIMITATION, T2).
- "Radical differentiation" not always available or credible in regulated/commodity B2B (LIMITATION, T2, inferred).
