## Beliefs
- The best pitch/narrative names a big, relevant change in the world BEFORE introducing your product (FRAMEWORK, T1 — "The Greatest Sales Deck").
- Story must be told by the whole company — CEO, marketing, product, recruiters — or it fails (OPINION, T1).
- For savvy prospects, the deck is a framework, not a template: harvest the stakes language from customers themselves (OPINION, T1 — "Is the Greatest Sales Deck Great for Savvy Prospects?").

## Principles
- Loss aversion is the engine: show winners AND losers from the change (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Product features appear late, framed as "Magic Gifts" that overcome obstacles to the Promised Land (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Differentiation is established through a differentiated Promised Land, not feature comparisons (OPINION, T1).
- "Tease" the Promised Land before explaining how you get there (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Frameworks
- 5 Elements of a Strategic Narrative (Greatest Sales Deck): (1) Name a big, relevant change in the world; (2) Show there'll be winners and losers; (3) Tease the Promised Land; (4) Introduce features as Magic Gifts overcoming obstacles; (5) Present evidence you can make the story come true (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Discovery conversation first (ask how prospects do X today), then build the narrative from their answers (PROCESS, T1).
- Get to the product in ≤8 slides; 12 slides before product is almost always too many (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Heuristics
- "Product in eight slides, max" (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Ask customers literally: "What changes in your world are creating opportunity and risk?" — write down their answers, use their language (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Lead with stakes, not features; tailor flow to the buyer's actual story (TACTIC, T1).
- Convey stakes across channels, not just the deck — everyone tells the same story (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Sales decks, whiteboards, spoken narrative; medium essays as the distribution (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Customer/prospect language about changes creating opportunity and risk; company alignment (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Strategic narrative + sales deck; company-wide story alignment (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Narrative adoption by the org; sales conversion from narrative decks (METRIC, T2, inferred).

## Decision rules
- If prospects are savvy and want differentiation → establish the differentiated Promised Land first; features only as obstacles-overcome (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If you can't name a big, relevant change with stakes → you're not ready for a narrative; product-first pitch will underperform (DECISION RULE, T2, inferred).

## Failure modes
- "Adding storytelling" as garnish without the stakes structure (WARNING, T1).
- Feature-comparison positioning for sophisticated buyers (WARNING, T1).
- BEFORE decks that lead with logos/credentials instead of the promised land (WARNING, T1 — Uberflip case).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Positioning's job is to create urgency via change, not to describe the market as-is (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- Zuora deck deconstruction (Greatest Sales Deck, 1M+ views) (EXAMPLE, T1).
- Uberflip AFTER vs BEFORE deck rebuild — after-deck more successful (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Works when the company genuinely sits in a shifting market (change exists to name); less applicable to stable, mature categories where no "big change" is credible (CONDITION, T2, inferred).

## Limitations
- Narrative without evidence (element 5) collapses with skeptical buyers (LIMITATION, T1).
- Over-length decks (12+ slides of stakes) lose prospects (LIMITATION, T1).
