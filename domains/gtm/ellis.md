## Beliefs
- PMF must be measured, not felt; growth before PMF is wasted spend (EMPIRICAL).
- The single best PMF question: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?" (FRAMEWORK, T1).
## Principles
- Survey only recent active users ("real usage" — e.g., Uber riders, not signups) (T1).
- Threshold: ~40% "very disappointed" → sustainable growth becomes possible. Admittedly arbitrary, set from ~100 startups (EMPIRICAL, T1).
- Asymmetric signal: <40% is a reliable warning; ≥40% is encouraging, not conclusive (T2).
## Frameworks
- PMF Survey (now PMFSurvey.com): core question + follow-ups on main benefit, who benefits most, why (FRAMEWORK, T1).
## Heuristics
- Minimum 30 responses directionally useful; 100+ for confidence (HEURISTIC, T1).
- A few "very disappointed" responses early can be enough to start learning (T1).
- Fix targeting/positioning/onboarding from follow-up answers; example: 7% → 40% in weeks (TACTIC, T1).
## Decision rules
- IF PMF < 40% THEN pause paid acquisition; run interviews + activation fixes (T1).
- IF PMF ≥ 40% THEN growth spend is justified (T1).
- IF sample < 30 THEN report score with confidence caveat, don't gate strategy on it (T1).
- IF respondents haven't used product in 2+ weeks THEN exclude (T1).
## Failure modes
- Surveying all users incl. dormant → score meaningless (T1).
- Treating 40% as a calibrated benchmark for your market — Ellis never published the verticals (T3 caution).
- Scaling before PMF → leaky bucket, churn (T2).
## Conditions
- Products with a definable "real usage" event; not for pre-launch concepts (T1).
