## Beliefs
- JTBD is a theory of consumer action — it describes the mechanisms that cause a consumer to adopt an innovation, not a needs taxonomy (FRAMEWORK, T1 — jtbd.info "What is Jobs to be Done?").
- Definition: "A Job to be Done is the process a consumer goes through whenever she aims to change her existing life-situation into a preferred one, but cannot because there are constraints that stop her" (FRAMEWORK, T1 — jtbd.info + Medium essay).
- JTBD's purpose is growth, not needs: it exists to understand why people buy and use products so you can create business growth (OPINION, T1 — Medium essay).

## Principles
- A JTBD describes the "better me": it answers "how are you better since you started using [product]?" — always frame in terms of progress (PRINCIPLE, T1 — jtbd.info).
- If you're not describing progress, you're describing something else (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Customers are "proactive, aspiring organisms" (Bandura) — we buy to improve ourselves (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Customer Jobs (JTBD): progress-based unit of analysis for innovation and marketing (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Four Forces of Progress (with Moesta lineage): push, pull, anxiety, habit (FRAMEWORK, T2 — book canon).

## Processes
- Identify the Job for a market/product; describe the change journey from current situation to preferred one; identify the constraints blocking progress; design product and message around removing constraints (PROCESS, T2 — book canon).

## Heuristics
- The biggest mistake: thinking of a JTBD as an activity or task ("store and retrieve music") — tasks describe product use; jobs describe desired change (HEURISTIC, T1 — jtbd.info).
- There are no "types" of jobs (functional/emotional/social is a bad taxonomy): each Job is a unique combination of desires; typing jobs creates false consensus and no action (HEURISTIC, T1 — jtbd.info).
- Competition comes from anywhere (Schumpeter): your real competitor may not be in your category at all — decreasing sales is your only signal (HEURISTIC, T1 — jtbd.info).

## Tactics
- Ask "how are you better since you started using X?" to test whether you've captured a real Job (TACTIC, T1 — jtbd.info).
- Describe jobs in terms of the change sought, not the product used (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- jtbd.info (essay canon); When Coffee and Kale Compete (book) (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- The customer journey of change; evidence of purchase/usage; competitive substitutes of any kind (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Job statements; market structure maps (jobs as markets); messaging inputs (OUTPUT, T2).

## Metrics
- Adoption/switching behavior (growth) — JTBD is judged by whether it produces growth, not by explanatory elegance (METRIC, OPINION, T1 — Medium essay).

## Decision rules
- If your "job" is an activity ("listen to music"), reframe until it's a change ("make my commute enjoyable") — activities are not jobs (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If labeling a job's "type" produces no decision, drop the label (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- Task/activity framing of jobs (FAILURE, T1 — jtbd.info, his "biggest mistake").
- Typing jobs into functional/emotional/social buckets (FAILURE, T1).
- Using JTBD to study needs for their own sake instead of growth (FAILURE, T1 — Medium).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Explicitly argues against the popular "types of jobs" framing used by some JTBD practitioners (OPINION, T1 — jtbd.info).
- Falsifiability argument: jobs are design problems (artificial), not natural problems — so objective job taxonomies are impossible (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- Music streaming: Pandora/Spotify succeeded by eliminating the "store and retrieve" task entirely — proof that tasks ≠ jobs (EXAMPLE, T1 — jtbd.info).
- Facebook taps desires (control, self-expression, belonging) in its own unique way — every job is a unique combination (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Product strategy, positioning, messaging; works best when a real switching/change behavior exists (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- Theory-heavy; lighter on interview procedure than Moesta/Ulwick (LIMITATION, T2 — his writing focuses on theory over script).
