## Beliefs
- Customers systematically lie to you — not maliciously, but out of politeness, optimism, and self-deception; interview method must be designed for a world where "everyone is lying to you" (FRAMEWORK, T1 — momtestbook.com).
- Compliments, fluff, ideas, and hypotheticals are worthless data; only facts about the customer's past behavior and commitments count (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- You can lead people to any response if you start pitching — pitching contaminates the interview (OPINION, T1 — Indie Hackers interview).
- 5 good conversations can save months of development (HEURISTIC, T1 — Indie Hackers).

## Principles
- The Mom Test: ask questions even your mom can't lie to you about (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- 3 rules of customer conversation: (1) talk less, listen more; (2) draw out specific facts from their past, not their opinions about the future; (3) talk about them and their life, not your idea (FRAMEWORK, T1 — Indie Hackers; book canon).
- Focus on current facts in their lives rather than future predictions (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- "Show me how much effort you're already putting toward this. Show me what you've tried that's failed" — hard evidence of existing behavior, not stated interest (TACTIC, T1 — Indie Hackers).

## Frameworks
- The Mom Test (book): rules + question types for unbiased customer conversations (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Commitment & advancement: an interview is only useful if you leave with a concrete commitment, advancement (next step), or referral; otherwise you learned nothing (FRAMEWORK, T2 — book canon; UNVERIFIED as exact quote).

## Processes
- Interview flow: validate the person is actually in your target segment first → move generic to specific → dig into how they currently solve the problem → look for money/time already spent → end by asking for commitment/advancement (PROCESS, T1 — Indie Hackers).

## Heuristics
- If they haven't given time, money, or a referral, their praise is worthless (HEURISTIC, T2 — book canon).
- Whenever someone says they have a problem, dig for specifics: "How does that actually work?", "Talk me through your process", "Tell me more about that" (TACTIC, T1 — Indie Hackers).

## Tactics
- Bad questions to never ask: "Would you buy this?", "How much would you pay for this?", "Do you like this idea?" (TACTIC, T1 — Indie Hackers).
- Deflect questions about your idea ("what do you think of X?") — park them and return to their life (TACTIC, T2 — book canon).
- Email outreach that makes people want to talk to you (a dedicated chapter/method in the book) (TACTIC, T2).

## Tools
- The Mom Test book + course; used at Harvard/UCL, Seedcamp, Microsoft Ventures, Shopify (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Access to potential customers (even friends' networks — hence "Mom"); a problem hypothesis; willingness to hear no (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Facts about customer behavior: how they solve the problem today, what they tried, what failed, what they spend (OUTPUT, T1).
- Segmented learning about who is/isn't a real customer (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Number of interviews; ratio of useful (fact-yielding) conversations; commitments/advancements obtained (METRIC, HEURISTIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- If you hear your product idea back from the customer in their words, you're doing it right; if you hear compliments, treat the interview as failed and re-run (DECISION RULE, T2 — book canon).
- Ask about the past, never the future: "what did you do last time X happened" beats "would you use X" (DECISION RULE, T1).
- No commitment/advancement obtained → do not count the conversation as validation; continue interviewing (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Asking "would you buy" / "how much would you pay" — produces polite lies (FAILURE, T1 — Indie Hackers).
- Pitching instead of listening — you lead the customer to any answer you want (FAILURE, T1).
- Treating compliments and interest as evidence (FAILURE, T1).
- Asking about hypothetical futures instead of past specifics (FAILURE, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- You don't need many interviews if they're done right (implicitly, quality over quantity; he doesn't fix a number) (OPINION, T2).

## Examples
- His own first company forced him into customer-facing work; books on sales/custdev didn't tell him how to actually do it (EXAMPLE, T1 — momtestbook.com).
- The "mom" test framing: your mom loves you too much to tell you the truth (EXAMPLE, T1).

## Conditions
- Works for early-stage product validation, pre-PMF; also taught inside corporates (Shopify) (CONDITION, T1).
- Best for: deciding what to build, who to build for, whether anyone cares (CONDITION, T1).

## Limitations
- Doesn't give you market size or statistical confidence — it's qualitative direction (LIMITATION, T2 — book canon).
- Less relevant once you need representative sampling or quantitative prioritization (LIMITATION, T2).
