## Beliefs
- Most products fail because they don't meet customer needs better than alternatives — and companies can't even agree on what a "need" is: "90% of product teams say there's no agreement on what a customer need even is"; 12 experts gave 12 different definitions (FACT/OPINION, T1 — AgileData podcast + Produx Labs).
- Since needs are undefinable in the abstract, define a need operationally as an outcome: the metrics customers use to measure success getting a job done (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Customers cannot articulate needs in the abstract ("latent needs" don't exist in his method — customers know exactly what they're trying to achieve; they just don't know solutions) (OPINION, T1 — AgileData).
- The goal: conceptualize a product that will win BEFORE development starts — separate the "front end of innovation" from development (OPINION, T1 — Produx Labs).

## Principles
- Keep customers in problem space the whole interview — never talk products (PRINCIPLE, T1 — AgileData).
- Outcome statements need a strict syntax to be useful from start to finish and to travel through the org (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Produx Labs).
- Needs are discovered qualitatively, then quantified with surveys (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI): market = group of people + job they're trying to get done; needs = outcome statements; job map = all steps of the job (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Outcome statement syntax: direction of improvement (minimize/maximize) + metric (time or likelihood) + object of control + contextual clarifier. E.g., "minimize the time it takes to heat water to the desired temperature" (FRAMEWORK, T1 — Produx Labs; he narrowed metrics to time and likelihood: "faster, more predictably, without defects").
- Opportunity landscape: importance × satisfaction gap across outcomes → prioritized unmet needs (FRAMEWORK, T2 — book canon).

## Processes
1. Define the market: pick the people and the job (from customer input) (PROCESS, T1 — Produx Labs).
2. 2–3 hour interviews, 2–3 people at a time; validate the job; build a job map step by step; under each step ask "what are you trying to accomplish?" and "anything you're trying to avoid?"; capture 5–6 outcome statements per step; type in real time so customers see and correct (PROCESS, T1 — AgileData).
3. Quantitative phase: survey hundreds of respondents on importance and satisfaction of the outcome statements (PROCESS, T1 — Produx Labs).
4. Compute opportunity (unmet need) scores; ideate solutions against underserved outcomes (e.g., Bosch case: 14 unmet needs).
5. Verify: return to customers with feature descriptions and measure predicted satisfaction lift (PROCESS, T1 — AgileData).

## Heuristics
- Threshold: if the new solution gets the job done 15%+ better, it's a winning product; 1–5% better won't make anyone switch (HEURISTIC, T1 — AgileData; company-internal empirical claim, treat as T2-ish).
- Interview lengths: 2–3 hours — "not half-hour interviews where we barely get to know the person" (HEURISTIC, T1).
- Address all 10 unmet needs, not one — one is incremental improvement, all is breakthrough (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Show customers what you're typing in real time; they start talking in outcome language (TACTIC, T1 — AgileData).
- Never ask "what do you want?" — ask what they're trying to accomplish and avoid at each job step (TACTIC, T1).

## Tools
- Strategyn ODI method + software; outcome statement glossaries (TOOL, T2).

## Inputs
- A chosen market (people + job); access to customers for long interviews; survey panel of hundreds (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Job maps; quantified outcome statements; opportunity landscape (priority-ranked unmet needs); validated product concepts (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Importance, satisfaction, opportunity scores per outcome; predicted vs actual satisfaction lift; "15% better" win threshold (METRIC, T1/T2).

## Decision rules
- Don't start development until a concept addresses the top unmet outcomes with ≥15% predicted improvement (DECISION RULE, T2 — AgileData).
- If your team can't define a need in outcome syntax, you're not ready to research — fix the definition first (DECISION RULE, T1 — Produx Labs).
- Use qualitative interviews to discover outcomes; use surveys to prioritize them — never skip the quant phase (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- The IBM PCjr failure that started it all: no agreed metrics to judge value, so development proceeded blind (FAILURE, T1 — AgileData).
- "Ideas first" or vague "needs first" approaches without an operational need definition (FAILURE, T1 — AgileData).
- Half-hour interviews that never leave problem space properly (FAILURE, T1).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Directly contradicts the "talk to customers, they'll tell you" school: customers can't articulate needs — you must define needs for them (outcome statements) and validate (OPINION, T1).
- Contrasts with Moesta/Klement story-first JTBD: ODI is metric-first (OPINION, T1).

## Examples
- IBM PCjr (his origin story); Bosch (14 unmet needs → winning solutions); thousands of ODI projects via Strategyn (EXAMPLE, T1 — AgileData; Bosch specifics from podcast).

## Conditions
- Best for established categories with measurable jobs (B2B tools, appliances, software); requires budget for long interviews + large surveys (CONDITION, T1/T2).

## Limitations
- Expensive and heavy (3-hour interviews, hundreds of surveys); hard for brand-new categories with no existing job language; outcome statements can feel mechanistic to creative teams (LIMITATION, T2).
- "15% threshold" is company-internal empirical claim — not independently replicated (LIMITATION, T2 — mark as company evidence).
