## Beliefs
- Discovery should be continuous, not a project: "at a minimum, weekly touchpoints with customers by the team building the product, where they conduct small research activities in pursuit of a desired outcome" (FRAMEWORK, T1 — Productboard interview).
- The team building the product must do the discovery themselves — it cannot be delegated to a separate research team (OPINION, T1 — Continuous Discovery Habits canon; Productboard).
- The shift from project mindset to continuous mindset is the hard part, not the techniques (OPINION, T1 — Productboard).

## Principles
- Small research activities every week beat big studies every quarter (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Focus on the "opportunity" (customer need/desire) before jumping to solutions; teams default to solutioning too early (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Lenny episode outline §14:17 "Why do we usually approach opportunity finding wrong?").

## Frameworks
- Opportunity Solution Tree (OST): desired outcome at top → opportunities (customer needs) → possible solutions → assumption tests; maps decisions so every test ties to an outcome (FRAMEWORK, T1 — producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-tree).
- Continuous Discovery Habits: weekly cadence of interviewing, opportunity mapping, assumption testing (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Weekly: interview 3–5 customers (small, continuous sample); map what you learn onto the opportunity tree; design small assumption tests; share with the team (PROCESS, T2 — book canon + Productboard).

## Heuristics
- Interview one person at a time; ask about a recent, specific episode in their life (storytelling), not general opinions (HEURISTIC, T2 — her published interviewing guidance).
- Recruit representative users across the spectrum — happy, unhappy, and non-users — not just fans (HEURISTIC, T2).
- When leaders say "no time for user research," automate the recruiting/reminder loop so conversations are already scheduled (HEURISTIC, T2 — Lenny outline §25:55 "How can you automate weekly conversations with customers?").

## Tactics
- Interviewing tips she teaches (Lenny outline §35:33): concrete behaviors, storytelling, specific past events (TACTIC, T2).
- Most common interviewing mistake (Lenny outline §39:57): (per her teaching) asking customers what they want / solution-idea questions instead of exploring their experience (TACTIC, T2 — exact phrasing UNVERIFIED).
- Decision rule between research vs experiment (Lenny outline §43:27): use interviews to reduce unknowns about the problem; use experiments to validate solution behavior (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Tools
- Opportunity solution tree templates (producttalk.org); Product Talk Academy (11,000+ students) (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- A desired outcome; customer access; team time budgeted weekly (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Opportunity trees; weekly learning artifacts; prioritized opportunities; test plans (OUTPUT, T2).

## Metrics
- Weekly cadence adherence; number of opportunities mapped vs solutions shipped; % decisions informed by customer contact (METRIC, HEURISTIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- If you can't name the customer opportunity behind a feature, the feature shouldn't be built yet (DECISION RULE, T2 — OST logic).
- Choose research when the unknown is the problem; choose an experiment when the unknown is whether a solution changes behavior (DECISION RULE, T2 — Lenny outline §43:27).

## Failure modes
- Feature-factory mode: shipping solutions without connecting them to opportunities (FAILURE, T1 — Lenny outline §18:10 "What should you do if your company is a feature factory?").
- Skipping discovery entirely because "leaders say there's no time" (FAILURE, T2).
- Interviewing with confirmation bias toward your preferred solution (FAILURE, T2 — Lenny outline §29:04 "How do you stay unbiased as a PM").

## Contrarian beliefs
- Discovery is not the researcher's job — PMs/engineers/designers must interview weekly themselves; most orgs treat research as a service to delegate (OPINION, T1 — Productboard).

## Examples
- Coached hundreds of teams from early startups to global enterprises; taught 11,000+ product people; Netflix OST example used in teaching (EXAMPLE, T1 — Lenny page).

## Conditions
- Product teams with ongoing customer access; works at any company size (she says discovery must adapt as companies grow — Lenny outline §40:25) (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- Weekly cadence is hard in zero-customer-access situations (pre-launch, enterprise sales-controlled); requires product org maturity to sustain (LIMITATION, T2).
