## Beliefs
- Buyer personas built from demographics and internal guesses are fiction; personas must be built from interviews with real buyers (OPINION, T1 — Wellspring podcast).
- "We want to hear this directly from the buyers and not from the customers at all" — interview people who recently went through a purchase evaluation, not your own happy customers (OPINION, T1 — Wellspring podcast). This is her signature move and it doubles as win/loss data.
- The persona's job is to reveal why buyers buy and why they don't — the buying decision, not the person's biography (OPINION, T1 — Klue podcast).

## Principles
- Fewer, evidence-based personas beat many demographic personas; the 5 Rings method "eliminates irrelevant data and reduces the number of buyer personas" (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Klue podcast).
- Interviewing requires a larger time commitment than clients assume — the depth is the point (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Wellspring podcast).

## Frameworks
- The 5 Rings of Buying Insight (FRAMEWORK, T1 — Klue + Wellspring):
  1. Priority Initiatives — the trigger moment: "take me back to the day when you first decided you needed X" — buyers give a list of benefits, but the benefits were needed 6–12 months before; what CHANGE made it a priority? The "über"-drivers so painful they can't go on.
  2. Success Factors — what must change after implementing your solution, and why.
  3. Perceived Barriers — what makes buyers hesitate or choose status quo.
  4. Decision Criteria — how they'll judge options.
  5. Buyer's Journey — how the decision process actually unfolded (people involved, steps, time).

## Processes
- Interview 10–20+ buyers who recently completed an evaluation in your category (including non-buyers/status-quo choosers); code answers into the 5 rings; look for patterns that segment by buying behavior, not demographics (PROCESS, T1 — Klue podcast + BPI method; interview count UNVERIFIED exact number).

## Heuristics
- Buyers can't tell you the trigger in the moment — they'll answer with benefits; push for "what happened that made this a priority NOW?" (HEURISTIC, T1 — Wellspring).
- The moment of first deciding you need X is usually 6–12 months before the evaluation (HEURISTIC, T1 — Wellspring).

## Tactics
- Ask "take me back to the day you first decided you needed X and tell me what happened" (TACTIC, T1 — Wellspring).
- Interview lost/status-quo buyers too — they carry the barriers ring (TACTIC, T2).

## Tools
- Buyer Persona Institute workshops; persona reports structured by the 5 rings (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Access to recent buyers in the category (won AND lost, including chose-status-quo); evaluation history (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- 5-ring buyer personas; prioritized initiatives/barriers/criteria per segment; content/messaging inputs (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Pattern frequency across interviews per ring; number of distinct buying segments (METRIC, HEURISTIC, T2).

## Decision rules
- Segment buyers by differences in the 5 rings (especially initiatives and barriers), not by title/industry — if two titles show the same rings, they're one persona (DECISION RULE, T2 — BPI method).
- If your persona can't name the trigger event and the barrier, it's not research-based; rebuild it (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Interviewing your own sales team or customers instead of category buyers (FAILURE, T1 — Wellspring: "not from the customers at all").
- Demographic personas with no buying insight (FAILURE, T2 — BPI positioning vs generic persona vendors).
- Accepting "list of benefits" as the trigger (FAILURE, T1 — Wellspring).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Personas should be built from buyers who chose competitors or status quo, not just your customers — most persona practice interviews the wrong people (OPINION, T1 — Wellspring).

## Examples
- Her consulting practice: hundreds of B2B persona projects across enterprise tech; 5 Rings used by clients like (UNVERIFIED specific names) (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- B2B with real, findable buyers; requires willingness to pay for interviews and time (CONDITION, T1 — Wellspring: "larger time commitment than they assume").

## Limitations
- Cost/time heavy; best for mature products with an existing category; thin for brand-new categories with no evaluations yet (LIMITATION, T2).
