## Beliefs
- The switch interview is "the engine of Jobs-to-be-Done": a forensic, backward walk through a real purchase that surfaces the true reasons someone changed (FRAMEWORK, T1 — jobstobedone.org/switch-interview).
- It exists to do "one thing surveys never could": reconstruct the actual story of a decision in the customer's own words, detailed enough to retell in 30 seconds and hear every force at play (OPINION, T1).
- The struggle is the data — recruit switchers, including people who almost switched and didn't (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- You can't describe what satisfies a customer without the context of the moment of purchase — standards of quality/performance/satisfaction are set at purchase time (OPINION, T1 — Mattress Interview Q&A).

## Principles
- Memory fades fast: interview people who switched in the last 30–90 days (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Capture events, not opinions; build a timeline: first thought → passive looking → event → active looking → event → decision → purchase (PRINCIPLE, T1).
- Never feed the customer your words; their language becomes positioning, copy, and product vocabulary (PRINCIPLE, T1).

## Frameworks
- Switch Interview (6 steps, T1 — jobstobedone.org):
  1. Recruit recent switchers (30–90 days; include almost-switched-and-didn't; exclude happy "best customers")
  2. Set the stage like a documentary ("no wrong answers, boring details matter")
  3. Find the first thought: "When did you first start thinking about this?" (anchor; often weeks/months before purchase)
  4. Walk the timeline event by event ("what happened next? where were you? who was with you?")
  5. Surface the Four Forces of Progress (push of the old, pull of the new, anxiety, habit) — they emerge from events, never ask "what were the forces?"
  6. Use their language, not yours
- Four Forces of Progress: push (what started it), pull (of the new option), anxiety (what gave pause), habit (what had to be broken) (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Demand-Side Sales: sales approach built on understanding the buyer's timeline and forces (FRAMEWORK, T2).

## Processes
- Interview technique: 45–90 minutes; play dumb (assume nothing, make them explain the obvious); connect the wrong dots deliberately (people correct you with the real story); interview in pairs (one drives, one listens for forces and gaps); pause and recant (contradictions are refinements, not errors) (PROCESS, T1 — switch-interview page).
- Recruit in rounds: first 5–7 interviewees = people who recently switched TO your product; then people who switched AWAY (they carry anxieties and habits) — "to hear all four forces you need both sides" (PROCESS, T1 — Mattress Interview Q&A).
- After interviews: map each onto a timeline + forces diagram; look across interviews for repeating jobs (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- Patterns emerge within 5–8 interviews for a given job — listen for repeated forces and timelines, not statistical significance (HEURISTIC, T1 — switch-interview FAQ).
- Interview-count by purchase type: meaningful purchases (laptop, house, car) ~10 interviews; low-risk/low-emotion habitual purchases may need 20–30 to find people who can articulate them (HEURISTIC, T1 — Mattress Interview Q&A).
- Stopping rules: you have enough when (a) you keep getting the same kinds of information, (b) the interviewee is tired, or (c) "I can play their role in the movie" — you could direct an actor to play that buyer (HEURISTIC, T1 — Mattress Interview Q&A).
- "If I know why they bought that product at that exact minute, I understand the purchase — then shake their hand and let them go" (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Ask "weird" disconnected questions (where they sat, whether the door was open) — detail rebuilds memory and emotion (TACTIC, T1 — Mattress Interview Q&A).
- Recruit people who switched away from your product too (TACTIC, T1).
- Record and interview remotely via video when needed (TACTIC, T1 — FAQ).

## Tools
- JTBD Radio archive (mattress interview, etc.); jobstobedone.org courses; timeline/forces mapping worksheets (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Access to recent switchers (30–90 days) on both sides of the decision; 45–90 min per interview (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Per-interview timeline + forces diagram; cross-interview job patterns; customer language/quotables; market structure (jobs vs segments) (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- Number of interviews until pattern repetition; forces appearing consistently; retellability of the story (METRIC, HEURISTIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- Don't recruit happy best customers — recruit switchers and non-switchers (DECISION RULE, T1).
- If you can't retell the story in 30 seconds with all forces present, keep interviewing (DECISION RULE, T1 — switch-interview page).
- Stop when stories repeat and you could direct the buyer's role in a movie (DECISION RULE, T1 — Mattress Q&A).

## Failure modes
- Rushing produces opinions, not events (FAILURE, T1 — FAQ).
- Asking about the future/hypotheticals instead of the past timeline (FAILURE, T1).
- Surveying instead of interviewing for purchase decisions — surveys can't reconstruct the story (FAILURE, T1 — switch-interview page).
- Only talking to happy customers → half the forces diagram (FAILURE, T1 — Mattress Q&A).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Survey research is the wrong tool for understanding purchase decisions (OPINION, T1).
- The detail that looks irrelevant (where you sat, what you ate) is the most valuable data (OPINION, T1 — Mattress Q&A).

## Examples
- The Mattress Interview (in Competing Against Luck): reconstructing a mattress purchase to the level of the couple's struggles and timeline (EXAMPLE, T1 — JTBD Radio).
- Consulting work with companies on demand-side sales (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- Best when a category/behavior exists to switch within; works for B2B and consumer (CONDITION, T1 — FAQ).
- Requires willingness to spend 45–90 min per interview and recruit both winners and losers (CONDITION, T1).

## Limitations
- Sample is deliberately non-representative — no market sizing (LIMITATION, T1).
- Low-stakes/habitual purchases yield thin stories; may need 20–30 interviews (LIMITATION, T1 — Mattress Q&A).
- Skills-heavy: bad interviewers get opinions, not events (LIMITATION, T1).
