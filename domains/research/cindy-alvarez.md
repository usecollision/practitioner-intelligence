## Beliefs
- Customers can reliably tell you about their problems and current behavior; they cannot reliably design solutions — research should focus on problem space (OPINION, T2 — Lean Customer Development canon).
- You don't need a big research budget or permission — customer development thinking can kick off in under 5 minutes (OPINION, T1 — cindyalvarez.com "3 ways to kickstart").
- Interviews don't need a rigid script; they need a model + a few reliable questions that work across contexts (OPINION, T1 — first-minute blog post).

## Principles
- Same questions are effective across industries because human brains are similar; questions should be open-sourced and remixed (PRINCIPLE, T1 — github.com/cindyalvarez/customerdevelopment).
- Set the tone in the first minute: exploratory/early framing, "the more detail the better, nothing you say will be boring", you are the expert, invite their questions (PRINCIPLE, T1 — first-minute post).
- Ask about the past and frequency, not the future and opinions (PRINCIPLE, T2 — book canon).

## Frameworks
- Lean Customer Development (book): lightweight, continuous customer learning inside product orgs (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Open-source Customer Development Questions repo — canonical question bank (FRAMEWORK/TOOL, T1).

## Processes
- Interview opening model: (1) confirm "now is still a good time"; (2) use the word "exploratory"/"early"; (3) use "people"/"individuals" not "customers"; (4) "nothing you say will be boring, I'm here to listen, you're the expert"; (5) invite them to ask you questions (PROCESS, T1 — first-minute post).
- After the opening, adapt direction to what matters to this person — that's exactly as it should be (PROCESS, T1).

## Heuristics
- Question bank core (HEURISTIC set, T1 — GitHub repo):
  - "Tell me about how you do/think about [topic]" (open, behavioral)
  - "In the past [time period], how often has [problem] happened?" (frequency = priority proxy)
  - "Last time you did [task], what was the most frustrating part?" (past-specific)
  - "What's the easiest part about [topic]?" (don't only mine pain)
  - "What other workarounds have you used to try and solve for [problem]?" (workarounds = demand evidence)
  - "What advice would you give to a friend getting started with [topic]?" (normative, low-threat)
  - "How high a priority is it for you to solve [problem]?" (stated priority)
  - "If you could wave a magic wand..." and "If [problem] was magically solved, how would it make your life better?" (outcome elicitation)
- Workarounds and frequency are the two highest-signal question types (HEURISTIC, T2 — book canon).

## Tactics
- Interview one person at a time; avoid groups (TACTIC, T2).
- Use interviews to generate hypotheses, then validate with usage analytics (quant) (TACTIC, T2 — book: "watch what people do, not what they say").
- Sprinkling these questions into existing customer conversations counts (TACTIC, T1 — repo).

## Tools
- cindyalvarez/customerdevelopment GitHub repo (open-source question bank) (TOOL, T1).
- Lean Customer Development book (O'Reilly) (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Access to users/customers; a topic; zero prerequisites otherwise ("Prerequisites: None!" — repo) (INPUT, T1).

## Outputs
- Behavioral facts about how customers do the job today; problem priority signals; workaround inventory (OUTPUT, T1).

## Metrics
- How often the problem occurs (frequency); number of workarounds in use; stated priority level (METRIC, T1 — repo).

## Decision rules
- Frequency question decides whether the problem is real and frequent enough to matter — low frequency + no workarounds → deprioritize (DECISION RULE, T2 — book canon).
- If you hear no workarounds and no past frustration, you're likely chasing a non-problem (DECISION RULE, T2).

## Failure modes
- Talking about your solution instead of their problem (FAILURE, T2).
- Asking "would you use X?" style hypotheticals (FAILURE, T2).
- Treating one interview as evidence (FAILURE, T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Customer development needs no prerequisites — you can start today with existing conversations, no budget, no research team (OPINION, T1 — repo + kickstart post). Mildly contrarian vs enterprise UX-research orthodoxy.

## Examples
- Worked at Yammer (freemium B2B SaaS) using continuous lightweight interviews; now runs research org at Microsoft (EXAMPLE, T2).

## Conditions
- Product teams with continuous access to users; B2B and B2C both (CONDITION, T2).
- Best when paired with analytics — interviews for why, analytics for how many (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- Frequency/priority self-reports still suffer stated-vs-revealed gaps; needs quant to confirm (LIMITATION, T2).
