## Beliefs
- "There are no facts inside the building" — all assumptions about customers must be tested in front of customers by the founders themselves (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Customer Development Manifesto, 2009).
- Startups fail from executing a business model no one wants, not from bad product engineering; Customer Development exists to fix that (OPINION, T1).
- Startups are chaotic and never run from a spreadsheet; CustDev turns chaos into actionable data (OPINION, T1 — steveblank.com manifesto part 5).

## Principles
- Customer Development runs in parallel with Product Development, with measurable checkpoints tied to customer milestones, not feature milestones (PRINCIPLE, T1 — UCSD deck).
- Customer Discovery must be done by founders, not delegated to sales/marketing (PRINCIPLE, T1 — manifesto).
- Failure is an option: Validation failing loops you back to Discovery ("rediscover what you failed to hear") (PRINCIPLE, T1 — manifesto).
- Low burn by design: don't build sales/marketing/business-dev teams until you have proof — a tested sales roadmap and valid purchase orders (PRINCIPLE, T1 — manifesto).

## Frameworks
- The Four Steps: Customer Discovery (test problem/need hypotheses in front of customers), Customer Validation (find a repeatable, scalable sales model), Customer Creation (drive end-user demand), Company Building (transition to execution machine) (FRAMEWORK, T1 — manifesto).
- Market Types: existing/resegmented/new markets change what validation means (FRAMEWORK, T2 — UCSD deck).
- Business Model Canvas (co-created with Alex Osterwalder's circle) as the hypothesis record (FRAMEWORK, T2).

## Processes
- Discovery: founders get out of the building, interview prospects, test the "who will buy, why, how, what are their other options" questions; iterate hypotheses (PROCESS, T1 — "Customer Development in a Diagram" comment thread).
- Validation: attempt early sales (pre-orders, pilot orders) with a minimum viable product; if you can't find enough paying customers, loop back to Discovery (PROCESS, T1 — manifesto).
- Stop rules per step: Discovery is done when responses converge and additional interviews produce no significantly new information; Validation is done when you have enough early sales to declare a repeatable/scalable sales process (DECISION RULE, T1 — manifesto Q&A).

## Heuristics
- In enterprise software, 3–5 early customers may be enough for Validation; for web products it may be 300–500 — the number depends on channel and industry (HEURISTIC, T1 — manifesto Q&A).
- The four discovery questions: Who will buy? Why will they buy? How will they buy? What are their other options (why might they NOT buy)? (HEURISTIC, T1 — diagram post).
- If you can't answer those four, you have the wrong idea or the wrong business model (HEURISTIC, T1).

## Tactics
- Founder-led interviews; write hypotheses down before talking to anyone (TACTIC, T2).
- Use early customer feedback to refine the problem statement, not the pitch (TACTIC, T2).

## Tools
- The Four Steps to the Epiphany; Customer Development worksheets/checklists; UCSD startup toolkit PDF (TOOL, T1).

## Inputs
- Stated hypotheses about customer, problem, channel, pricing, business model; willingness to be wrong (INPUT, T2).

## Outputs
- Validated/invalidated business model hypotheses; tested sales roadmap; purchase orders (OUTPUT, T1 — manifesto).

## Metrics
- Convergence of customer responses (Discovery); number of early paying customers (Validation); cash burn rate (METRIC, T1).

## Decision rules
- When Discovery responses stop yielding new information → move to Validation (DECISION RULE, T1).
- When Validation can't produce paying customers → return to Discovery; do not push forward (DECISION RULE, T1).
- Don't hire sales/marketing/BD until purchase orders exist — cash raised only obscures whether you found a market (DECISION RULE, T1).

## Failure modes
- The James Dean School of Management: raising lots of money and buying early business via discounts/giveaways ("we'll make it up later") (FAILURE, T1 — manifesto).
- Being enamored with "the idea" instead of the evidence (FAILURE, T1 — diagram post comment).
- Delegating discovery to non-founders (FAILURE, T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Even infinite cash is useless in the first two steps — it can only obscure whether you found a market (OPINION, T1 — manifesto). Directly contradicts "raise more and figure it out" startup orthodoxy.

## Examples
- Design Within Reach: succeeded by testing a real furniture-industry problem (4-month shipping delays for designer furniture) while Furniture.com/Living.com burned VC cash and died (EXAMPLE, T1 — O'Reilly chapter 2).

## Conditions
- Early-stage startups (pre-PMF); also adapted for corporate "intrepreneuring" (CONDITION, T1 — diagram post).
- Requires founder time and access to real prospects (CONDITION, T2).

## Limitations
- Not a marketing research method for existing products/known markets; heavy founder involvement is a bottleneck at scale (LIMITATION, T2).
- Convergence is fuzzy — no fixed sample size; relies on interviewer judgment (LIMITATION, T2).
