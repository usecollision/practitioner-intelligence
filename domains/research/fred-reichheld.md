## Beliefs
- One question — "how likely are you to recommend us?" (0–10) — is "the one number you need to grow"; recommenders drive profitable growth (FRAMEWORK, T1 — HBR 2003, "The One Number You Need to Grow").
- NPS is not just a metric but a system: the Net Promoter System with an inner loop (getting back to customers who gave feedback, coaching employees) and an outer loop (identifying, prioritizing, fixing systemic issues) (FRAMEWORK, T1 — Forrester summary of his correction).
- "Fight the temptation to let it become just a score" (PRINCIPLE, T1 — Ultimate Question 2.0, quoted in MIT Sloan Review).

## Principles
- Score categories: 9–10 promoters, 7–8 passives, 0–6 detractors; NPS = % promoters − % detractors (FRAMEWORK, T1 — canon).
- The value is in the follow-up and the loops, not the score itself (PRINCIPLE, T1 — MIT Sloan).
- He later corrected the "one number" claim: NPS should be part of a broader system (FACT, T1 — Forrester).

## Frameworks
- NPS metric; Net Promoter System (inner/outer loops); NPS-based management (FRAMEWORK, T1 — MIT Sloan + Forrester).
- Ultimate Question 2.0 with Rob Markey (FRAMEWORK, T1).

## Processes
- Survey at key touchpoints (not just annual) → close the loop with individual detractors (inner loop) → aggregate and prioritize systemic fixes (outer loop) (PROCESS, T2 — canon).

## Heuristics
- NPS is a loyalty/outcome measure, not a CX-quality measure — treat it accordingly (HEURISTIC, T1 — Forrester).
- If you tie compensation to the score, you invite gaming (HEURISTIC, T1 — Forrester).

## Decision rules
- Adopt NPS as a metric only if you commit to the loops; as a bare score it has no more empirical support than other satisfaction metrics (DECISION RULE, T1 — MIT Sloan synthesis).

## Failure modes (documented by critics, acknowledged in part by advocates)
- NPS-as-score: firms track the number without the system (FAILURE, T1 — Forrester: "controversy stems mostly from how it's implemented").
- Treating NPS as measuring CX quality directly when it measures loyalty outcomes (FAILURE, T1 — Forrester).
- Tying compensation to NPS creates gaming pressure (FAILURE, T1 — Forrester).
- Adopting NPS without checking whether it predicts business results in YOUR firm (FAILURE, T1 — Forrester).

## Contrarian beliefs (vs the evidence)
- Empirical challenge: Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen & Aksoy (Journal of Marketing 2007) challenged NPS's predictive superiority over other metrics; MIT Sloan Review's examination "failed to confirm its superiority" over other satisfaction scores (FACT, T1 — MIT Sloan + JCSDCB).
- Conceptual critique: "The Single-Question Trap" (Pingitore et al.) — single-item measures are less reliable (FACT, T1 — MIT Sloan footnote).
- Reichheld's response: dismisses critics as "net pro-moaners" — which MIT Sloan calls an inadequate answer (OPINION, T1 — MIT Sloan).

## Examples
- GE's Jeff Immelt championed NPS in 2005: "the best customer-relationship metric I've seen" (EXAMPLE, T1 — Forrester).
- Companies crediting NPS with cultural shift toward customer-centricity (EXAMPLE, T1 — MIT Sloan interviewee).

## Conditions
- Works as a management system in firms with customer feedback loops and executive commitment; works as a directional benchmark across firms (CONDITION, T1 — Forrester).

## Limitations
- The "will NPS improve performance?" question is untestable (no control group possible) — neither critics nor advocates can settle it (LIMITATION, T1 — MIT Sloan).
- Metric-level superiority over alternatives (CSAT, CES) is disputed (LIMITATION, T1).
