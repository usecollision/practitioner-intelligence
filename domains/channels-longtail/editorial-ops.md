# PANEL — Editorial Operations (calendar + newsletter)

## Consensus (T1 convergence: Oshinsky, White, Schwedelson, Geisler, Law)
- **Cadence is a commitment.** Consistent beats bursts; gaps train the audience to forget you. Weekly is the default B2B newsletter heuristic; daily only with a dedicated operator.
- **A job, not a feed.** Newsletter needs a one-sentence promise ("job of the newsletter" — Oshinsky); every content piece must serve a pillar or an audience pain (Law). Promise-less channels compete with everything and win nothing.
- **Distribution is planned.** Lead asset → 3-5+ channel-native derivatives, adapted not copy-pasted; schedule the cascade explicitly (content-strategy rules).
- **Behavior over calendar.** Branch content/CTA by subscriber state (Geisler); qualify active audiences, winback, re-permission, prune (White).
- **Format-matched timing.** Newsletters: Mon-Wed 5-8am / Thu-Sun 8-11am; offers 10am-2pm; never on the hour (Schwedelson — EMPIRICAL T2: ~15% open uplift off-hour, +28%/+19% time-commitment subject lines).

## Disagreement & resolution
1. **Frequency** (Geisler "more than you think" vs Atkins "more isn't better"): high-intent onboarding lists absorb more; reputation-sensitive bulk sends don't. Decision: match frequency to engagement, not to ambition.
2. **Open rate value post-MPP** (Schwedelson optimizes opens vs White: clicks/engagement): opens stay directional on Gmail/Outlook-heavy lists; engagement is the goal either way.
3. **Monetization timing** (launch fast vs monetize late): Oshinsky's one-new-revenue-stream-per-year is the operational middle: launch fast, prove format, add one stream/year.

## Failure knowledge (T1/T2)
- Cadence chaos (gaps train forgetfulness); overcommitting then quietly dying (publish less, consistently).
- Low-intent subscriber giveaways → deliverability decay + fake engagement (White/Atkins).
- Monetizing before trust (one trick per year — Oshinsky).
- No promise; no DRI; no repurposing cascade (calendar = suggestion box).
- Pruning without re-permissioning first (White treats re-permission as a growth lever).

## Collision Method (editorial ops) — decision rules
1. IF no one-sentence promise THEN define it before cadence or format (Oshinsky, FRAMEWORK, T1).
2. IF capacity < cadence THEN halve cadence — consistency is the habit mechanism (White/Oshinsky, HEURISTIC, T1).
3. IF same audience + same channel THEN freeze day/time after one test (Schwedelson/White, EMPIRICAL, T2).
4. IF a format/stream fails twice THEN retire it (synthesis, HEURISTIC, T2).
5. IF dormant segment THEN winback → re-permission → prune, in that order (White, FRAMEWORK, T1).
6. IF monetizing THEN one new stream per year; sponsors need scale, paid tier needs depth, product cross-sell needs ICP fit (Oshinsky, HEURISTIC, T2).

## Metrics
- North star: active subscribers (30-90d opened) or weekly replies — not raw list size.
- Guardrails: spam <0.3%, per-issue unsubscribes, bounce rate; delivery health checked per send.
- Velocity vs capacity: publish rate sustained ≥3 months before scaling.
- Re-measure: weekly engagement snapshot; monthly retro feeding backlog scoring.
