- ## Beliefs — Translation converts words; transcreation converts intent. Localization adapts for legal/technical/UX fit; transcreation rebuilds for cultural impact and brand voice (Lokalise, PoliLingua). The depth ladder: plain translation < localization < transcreation.
- ## Principles
  - Lock the narrative frame, let each market rewrite the punch line (Mastercard "Priceless" → 53 languages, 112 countries, 80%+ awareness — EMPIRICAL, T2).
  - Same insight, local words, local casting, same setup/payoff (Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry" → +15.9% global sales year one ≈ $376M — EMPIRICAL, T2).
  - Visual structure can travel unchanged; surrounding words must not (Stabilo: kept the English memeable headline, localized body copy; 10M impressions, Cannes Gold — EMPIRICAL, T2).
  - Humor transfers poorly; rewrite the punch line in words people actually use, or cut it.
  - Every brand that failed had the budget to transcreate correctly; none did — the cost of a transcreation brief is trivial vs a global rebrand (HSBC: "Assume nothing" → "Do nothing", $10M reprint/rebrand — EMPIRICAL, T2).
  - 29% of marketers admit a translation or cultural blunder damaged brand reputation (Lokalise survey 2026 — EMPIRICAL, T2).
- ## Failure knowledge (documented cases)
  - Pepsi China: "Come alive! You're in the Pepsi generation" → "Pepsi brings your ancestors back from the grave" (literal slogan translation inverting meaning).
  - HSBC: "Assume nothing" → "Do nothing" in non-English markets; $10M rebrand.
  - Pampers Japan: stork delivering babies is US folklore; Japanese folklore uses a giant peach — sales slumped until research caught it; competitors captured the niche.
  - Coca-Cola China: first transliteration ≈ "wax-filled toad" → corrected to "happy feeling in the mouth" after a costly correction campaign.
  - GM Nova (Spanish: "no va" = doesn't go); Schweppes "Tonic Water" → "Toilet Water" in Italy; American Airlines "Fly in Leather" → "fly naked" in LatAm.
  - German ATM button "abnehmen" (lose weight) instead of "abheben" (withdraw) — register/terminology errors in UI.
  - Failure mechanism (2025-26 note, PoliLingua): raw AI translation accelerates the same failure mode — unvetted MT ships cultural errors at machine speed; requires human post-editing and in-context testing.
- ## Decision rules
  - IF the message's emotional weight must survive (slogans, campaigns, humor) THEN transcreate — rewrite, don't translate (Lokalise/PoliLingua — FRAMEWORK, T2).
  - IF a joke/idiom needs a footnote to make sense THEN cut it (Lokalise — HEURISTIC).
  - IF the claim itself doesn't survive the market THEN the problem is the value prop, not the copy (localization skill standard — HEURISTIC).
  - IF content is high-risk (legal, medical, financial, safety) THEN back-translation review is mandatory (Lokalise — HEURISTIC).
  - IF using AI translation THEN require in-market human review of anything emotional or brand-critical (PoliLingua 2025-26 — HEURISTIC, T2).
- ## Conditions — transcreation pays most for consumer brands with emotional campaigns; localization depth should vary per market (mature market = translation; new market = transcreation).
- ## Limitations — campaign revenue attributions (Snickers +15.9%) are vendor/press-reported, not independent audits; classic failure anecdotes are pre-digital era but the mechanism replicates (PoliLingua).
