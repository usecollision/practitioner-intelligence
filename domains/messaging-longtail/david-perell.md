- ## Beliefs — Surprise is the raw ingredient of internet writing: "surprise and insight and entertainment are all very tightly correlated" (citing Shannon information theory). Ideas are gathered by "echolocation" — testing verbal candidates in conversation and on Twitter, watching for eyebrow-raising reactions. Essays should "meander toward surprise" (Paul Graham's line, which he follows). Writing is social, not solitary: the model of the writer as a Thoreau-like recluse is wrong.
- ## Principles
  - Test ideas live before writing: talk through them with trusted, opinionated people; watch reactions. CRIBS filter: Confusing, Repeated, Interesting, Boring, Surprising — pursue Interesting/Surprising, drop Confusing/Repeated/Boring.
  - Twitter as an idea-validation lab: throw out ideas irreverently and off-schedule; the ones that pop (e.g. a tweet that got 500 retweets) become essay building blocks.
  - Routine beats inspiration: 90 minutes every morning before email or social; no word count, just distraction-free writing ("You have permission to not write, but you don't have permission to do anything else" — Neil Gaiman).
  - LEGO-block structure: sort notes into thematic sections, each a mini-essay; finish blocks, then fit them together like Sudoku — when one piece changes, the others must follow; orphan blocks go back to the idea bank, not forced in.
  - "Simplicity on the far side of complexity" (Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.): research until the urge to write overpowers you, then write through several rounds of editing to arrive at the essence.
  - Fast-writing drill: 3-minute timer, explain the essay to an imaginary friend, transcribe (Otter) — talking forces structure at the speed of thought; long essays take 100+ hours but the drill builds the skill.
- ## Decision rules
  - IF an idea tests as confusing/repeated/boring in conversation THEN drop it; IF interesting/surprising THEN dig deeper (Perell CRIBS — FRAMEWORK, T1).
  - IF a piece of the essay doesn't fit the narrative THEN save it for a future piece instead of forcing it (Perell — HEURISTIC, T1).
  - IF stuck on structure THEN talk the idea out loud on a timer and transcribe before typing (Perell — HEURISTIC, T1).
  - IF writing for reach THEN put the most surprising sentence/claim where it's seen first (Perell — HEURISTIC, T1).
- ## Metrics — reaction quality in conversation (CRIBS), tweet engagement as idea validation, essay completion via block assembly; he does not track content schedules — "I write what stirs my soul."
- ## Failure modes — writer's block from unorganized notes (his stated reason for blocks); writing without testing ideas in conversation; polished writing with no surprise; forcing orphan blocks into the narrative.
- ## Conditions — works for individual writers/founders building a long-form essay practice; requires a trusted conversation network; 90-minute daily routine assumes schedule control.
- ## Limitations — 100-hour essays are self-reported and not a throughput model for teams; his model sells writing education (incentive bias); conversation-network requirement is a precondition many practitioners lack.
