## Beliefs
- AI search is a structural transition publishers must navigate strategically, not a disaster happening to them. "The biggest mistake publishers made in Q1 wasn't AI. It was treating AI as something happening to them instead of something they can navigate strategically." (SEJ Live talk, Mar 2026) — OPINION/HEURISTIC
- SEO in 2026 includes visibility in ALL discovery engines; being part of AI conversations is now part of relevance. (FACT-adjacent framing, her position)
- Publisher business models that depend on one channel (Google organic) have a single point of failure; the goal is becoming "channel agnostic."
- The field is terminologically and methodologically confused: "whenever I have five SEOs in the room, I have more than 11 opinions... We cannot even agree on the name of GEO, LLMO, AEO, AI SEO." (Aug 2025) — OPINION

## Principles
- Reduce dependence on any single discovery channel; own your audience.
- Experience-first editorial (interviews, analysis, original research) survives AI aggregation better than recycled informational content.
- Direct audience is the metric that outlasts algorithm changes.
- SEOs alone can't fix AI visibility: Reddit, community, and product challenges shape answers — cross-team work (community, product) is required. (Aug 2025) — HEURISTIC

## Frameworks
- "3 Strategies That Can Survive AI Search": (1) AI-proof content, (2) value-based clicks, (3) SERP opportunities resistant to AI. (Mar 2026)
- Publisher resilience model: editorial formats + revenue model + owned audience.

## Processes
- At SEJ: shifted editorial toward experience-first formats; moved from programmatic revenue to asset-based sponsorship; made direct-audience growth the top metric priority. (Mar 2026)
- As consultant: helps brands audit which prompts matter and where they appear before optimizing content.

## Heuristics
- Treat AI visibility as multi-engine; don't assume one optimization works everywhere.
- Measure what matters: not all prompts are equal — track which prompts convert ("If you track 700 prompts... which ones are actually important" — she flags this as the open measurement challenge, Aug 2025).

## Tactics
- Publish original research and interviews (formats AI engines and humans both cite).
- Run editorial experiments on format mix; cut content that only served Google rankings.
- Build consensus with your website as a hub (her SEJ strategy framing).

## Tools
- SEJ's own publishing stack; GA/console data; prompt-tracking tools (she cites "ZIP data" prompt sources as an emerging signal, Aug 2025).

## Inputs
- Organic traffic trends by content format; audience engagement data; understanding of which AI platforms matter to the buyer.

## Outputs
- SEJ editorial strategy, columns, conference talks (SEJ Live), consulting guidance.

## Metrics
- Direct audience growth (her top metric for publishers); organic traffic mix; AI answer presence per engine; conversion attribution of prompts (open problem).

## Decision rules
- When a content format's Google traffic collapses AND AI engines don't cite it → retire or rebuild it as experience-first (interviews/analysis/original research).
- When revenue depends on one channel → diversify revenue (sponsorships/assets) before audience declines further.
- When you can't verify which prompts matter → start with the customer journey, not keyword volume.

## Failure modes
- Treating AI as an external event rather than a strategic variable (her #1 named publisher mistake).
- Optimizing for "AI" generically without deciding which engines, which prompts, which journey stages.
- Continuing to chase clicks when zero-click AI answers change what "traffic" means.

## Contrarian beliefs
- Publishers should stop treating programmatic ad revenue as primary; asset-based sponsorship + owned audience is the resilient model. — OPINION (unusual for a media exec to say publicly)

## Examples
- SEJ itself: after Google organic declines, editorial + revenue + audience changes (documented in her SEJ Live talk).
- Her CEO/CMO roundup (Aug 2025) documenting industry-wide confusion on naming and measurement.

## Conditions
- Works for publishers and content-led brands with editorial teams; less relevant for transactional ecommerce where product feeds matter more.
- Her measurement skepticism applies most when teams lack prompt-level conversion data.

## Limitations
- Publisher-centric; her concrete tactics are editorial, not technical (she points to others for schema/crawl mechanics).
- Her diagnosis is stronger than her prescription: she names the measurement problem but doesn't publish a closed-loop measurement method.
