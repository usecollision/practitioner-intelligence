## Beliefs
- The value of a market map "is almost entirely in the variables you couldn't scrape" — primary research is what separates a board-quality map from an analyst exercise (senior research director, via Infomineo) (T2).
- A market map is a maintained decision artifact, not a one-off deck: competitors move on their own clock (industry-lens) (T2).
- Positioning maps (perceptual maps, 1960s-70s marketing research roots) exist to anchor internal debates in how customers perceive alternatives, not how teams wish to be seen (Umbrex) (T2).

## Principles
- Scope before data: explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria for players (direct vs adjacent vs entrants; revenue thresholds; subsidiaries), geographic and segment perimeter (Infomineo) (T2).
- Choose axes before touching data; axes must be the dimensions buyers actually decide on (2-3 max — "a landscape that tries to score 30 attributes hides the three that move deals") (industry-lens; Umbrex) (T2).
- Date every claim: an undated assertion is the fastest way to ship a stale or fabricated landscape (industry-lens) (T2).
- Competitive set comes from win/loss notes (5-8 names), not an exhaustive scrape (industry-lens) (T2).

## Frameworks
- Competitive positioning map (Price vs Perceived Benefit default; TCO vs Reliability for B2B infra; Net Price/user vs depth for SaaS) (Umbrex) (T2).
- Market gap analysis view: overlay customer needs vs current offerings to make whitespace explicit (Infomineo) (T2).
- Multi-sided/market-structure views for "where to play" questions (Infomineo) (T2).

## Processes
1. State the strategic question the map answers (Infomineo: mandatory methodology note) (T2).
2. Define player universe with inclusion/exclusion criteria + geographic/segment perimeter (T2).
3. Gather public data (pricing pages, changelogs, comparison copy, G2 themes, job posts as roadmap leaks) — dated (T2).
4. Validate positioning claims with primary research: 8-12 expert interviews minimum for board quality; more in thin-data markets (Infomineo) (T2).
5. Plot on 2-3 decision axes; mark claimed vs actual positions (reviews vs marketing) (existing skill; Umbrex) (T2).
6. Find gaps with demand evidence; name the move each gap implies (industry-lens) (T2).
7. Refresh quarterly in fast sectors; event-triggered updates (funding round, entrant, M&A, regulatory shift) (Infomineo) (T2).

## Heuristics
- 8-12 expert interviews = standard for a defensible board-level map (T2).
- 5-8 competitors from won/lost notes is the right map size (T2).
- Include indirect competitors and the status quo/do-nothing option (industry-lens; existing skill) (T2).

## Tools
Klue/Kompyte for continuous CI feeds; SEMrush for visibility/authority axes; MDS (multidimensional scaling) for statistical positioning (T3 vendor claims; treat as options not requirements).

## Inputs
Win/loss shortlists, competitor pricing, review themes, job posts, analyst/listicle coverage, expert interview capacity.

## Outputs
Positioning map + annotation sheet, whitespace candidates with demand vs supply evidence, methodology note, refresh schedule.

## Metrics
Positioning accuracy vs verified sources; market coverage %; # viable whitespace opportunities; staleness (days since last refresh).

## Decision rules
- IF the map feeds a board/exec decision THEN budget 8-12 expert interviews (Infomineo, T2).
- IF the competitor list doesn't match win/loss names THEN rebuild it from won/lost notes (industry-lens, T2).
- IF >3 candidate axes THEN cut to the 2-3 buyers trade off (industry-lens/Umbrex, T2).
- IF a quadrant is empty THEN require demand evidence (search/ review complaints) before calling it whitespace (existing + Umbrex, T2).
- IF the category moves fast (tech, fintech, regulatory flux) THEN quarterly refresh + event triggers (Infomineo, T2).
- IF the map has no methodology note THEN add one: question, sources, interview count, coverage period (Infomineo, T2).

## Failure modes
- Ambiguous player definitions → rework (Infomineo) (T2).
- Global perimeter for a local decision (or vice versa) → map is neither complete nor useful (Infomineo) (T2).
- Mapping by vendor claims alone; junk axes ("easy to use"); declaring empty quadrants whitespace without demand evidence (Umbrex; existing skill) (T2).
- Static snapshot that outlives its freshness (Umbrex; Infomineo) (T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- AI accelerates map building but primary interviews remain the differentiator — "human judgment still wins" on the variables you can't scrape (Infomineo) (T2).

## Conditions
Works when buyer decision dimensions are knowable and a real strategic question exists. Fails when used as decoration, when perimeter is ambiguous, or in thin-data emerging markets without local primary research.

## Limitations
Positioning maps oversimplify multi-dimensional value; benefit measurement is perception-dependent; maps need discipline to stay current (Umbrex) (T2).
