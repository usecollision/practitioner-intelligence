## Beliefs
- TAM quantifies opportunity, but sizing an *existing* market can understate new business models (SaaS vs on-premise; eBay vs collectible dealers expand the market) (FRAMEWORK, T1).
- Bottom-up TAM is the analysis they want to see: it forces you to think about target customer profile, willingness to pay, and the shape/skills of your sales & marketing org (T1).
- Don't "game" the TAM: many of the best internet companies (eBay, Airbnb) started with modest TAMs and expanded use cases (T1).

## Principles
- Top-down ("1.36B people × $1 × 40%") tends to overstate and ignores distribution reality ("How would they learn about your product?") (T1).
- Market *structure* (concentration/fragmentation of the system of record, who owns data/distribution) can matter more than market *size* — "TAM slides get funded; market structure analysis gets built" (a16z Haber, T2 OPINION).
- Investors routinely see: TAM too small (non-starter) or SAM presented as TAM (TheVCfactory) (T2).

## Frameworks
- TAM/SAM/SOM funnel; bottom-up (countable customers × revenue per customer) vs top-down (share of an industry number); value/benefit method for displacing incumbents (TechCrunch/Radlo-Zandi) (T1/T2).

## Processes
1. Define the market and unit of account (job, not product category).
2. Build bottom-up first: segment → count buyers → price per segment (ARPU/ACV) → multiply.
3. Cross-check top-down with analyst/industry reports; normalize definitions, dates, geography.
4. SOM from GTM capacity: sales headcount × quota, channels; B2B SaaS year-3 planning range 1-5% of SAM; consumer 0.1-1% (T2 heuristic).
5. Present ranges/scenarios with sourced assumptions; keep the working number and the public number distinct.

## Heuristics
- Convergence within 20-30% between methods = solid estimate; divergence = productive question to investigate (Zimt) (T2).
- A gap >10x means a definition or source error, not a special market (consensus) (T2).
- Sources older than 12-18 months are stale (Zimt) (T2).

## Tactics
- Use free abstracts of Gartner/Frost & Sullivan/Statista before buying reports (Radlo-Zandi) (T2).
- Name actual accounts for SOM; if you can't name enough, shrink SOM (T2).

## Inputs
ICP, pricing (or planned), segment counts (Sales Navigator-style filters, business registers), analyst data, GTM capacity plan.

## Outputs
TAM/SAM/SOM model, scenario table, assumption log, market-shape overlay (concentration, who owns distribution/data).

## Metrics
Method-gap ratio; % inputs sourced+dated; SOM coverage by named accounts; sensitivity swing of critical assumption (price or account count).

## Decision rules
- IF investors are the audience THEN lead with bottom-up (VC consensus, T2).
- IF category has no countable buyer population THEN use value method + analogs, not analyst reports (T2).
- IF one incumbent owns 50%+ of a system of record THEN flag structure risk: size the complement/services wedge, not the head-on TAM (a16z Haber, T2).
- IF methods diverge >10x THEN re-examine definitions before re-running numbers (T1 consensus).
- IF SOM can't be backed by named accounts THEN shrink SOM (T2).
- IF data is >18 months old THEN re-source before presenting (T2).

## Failure modes
- "1% of a huge market" — dismissed as wishful thinking (T1).
- Confirmation bias: choosing numbers that support the conclusion you want; "everyone is our customer" (Ainna) (T2).
- Single-method reliance; false precision (a single point estimate); stale data (Zimt) (T2).
- Ignoring willingness to pay: "80% want the feature" ≠ 0% will pay (Zimt) (T2).

## Contrarian beliefs
- TAM size is losing its luster as an investment filter in vertical AI — structure first (T2, 2026).

## Conditions
- Bottom-up needs an identifiable ICP and pricing. Pre-product concepts: use value method. Fundraising: bottom-up leads. Board narrative: top-down context + bottom-up defense.

## Limitations
- Sizing methods are heuristics, not measurements; SOM % ranges are planning conventions; structure-over-TAM is a thesis, not data.
