- ## Beliefs — Referral campaigns are diagnosable funnels, not wishful virality. You can predict growth from the K-factor before launch, and you fix the leaky stage, not the reward.
- ## Principles — The viral coefficient (K) = invitations sent per user × invitation conversion rate. A K of 1.0 = self-sustaining loop; below 1, growth decays. Consumer-internet benchmarks: 0.15-0.25 good, 0.4 great, ~0.7 outstanding. Measure 5 stages, not one number.
- ## Frameworks — 5 must-watch referral metrics: (1) K-factor, (2) participant conversion rate, (3) participant share rate, (4) invitation CTR, (5) invitation conversion rate. Campaign analytics (Viral Loops dashboard taxonomy).
- ## Processes — launch → read the 5 metrics → identify the leak (not sharing? not clicking? not converting?) → change one variable (incentive, channel, message, timing) → re-measure.
- ## Heuristics — if share rate is high but conversion low, the offer/landing page is the problem; if CTR low, the share message/channel is the problem; if K < 0.15, don't budget for a loop.
- ## Metrics — K-factor (registrations per unique inviting user), participant conversion, share rate, invite CTR, invite conversion, activated referrals (practice: reward on activation to prevent gaming).
- ## Decision rules
  - IF K < 0.15 THEN treat referral as an assist channel — pair with paid/other acquisition; don't staff for virality. (EMPIRICAL, T1)
  - IF share rate < target THEN redesign share message/channels before touching the reward amount. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF invite CTR is high but conversion low THEN fix landing page/offer friction, not sharing. (HEURISTIC, T2)
  - IF reward is paid on signup THEN switch to activation-gated rewards for SaaS to cut fraud/quality issues. (PRACTICE, T2)
  - IF K ≥ 0.4 THEN double down: increase share surfaces (in-product prompts, email signatures). (EMPIRICAL, T1)
- ## Failure modes — rewarding signups not activations; one-metric management (K only); ignoring the share step; expecting virality from a non-viral product.
- ## Conditions — consumer internet / SaaS with high retention; benchmarks are consumer-internet specific, not B2B enterprise.
- ## Limitations — T2: vendor perspective (Viral Loops sells the tool); benchmarks published by the vendor, directionally credible but not independently audited.
