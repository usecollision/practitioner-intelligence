## Beliefs
- The dominant CRO answer to "why aren't people converting?" is wrong: "the answer is not 'because you have two call-to-action buttons'" [3]
- Emotion is "in the decision-making seat" — research (Antonio Damasio) shows decisions are impossible without emotion, so pure-data CRO that ignores emotional drivers optimizes the wrong layer [4]
- Most CRO audits check the page; hers check the customer's decision process first [3]
- CRO's job is not just lift but answering business questions about customers, products and the future of the business [1]

## Principles
- Customer-driven, not product-driven: "make it about the customer" (pillar 1) [2]
- "Don't just show it, make 'em feel it" (pillar 2): images and color are processed faster than text — visual elements must carry emotional triggers [2]
- "Test strategies, not elements" (pillar 3): single-element tests (buttons, headlines) produce results that are hard to analyze, understand and scale; test whole strategies tied to emotional triggers [2]
- Research quality determines everything: personas built in an hour-long workshop ("Jane, 32, white picket fence") are worthless; she wants top-3 pains, top-3 desired emotional outcomes, real customer language [2,4]

## Frameworks
- **Emotional Targeting Framework™** (her named methodology, 4 phases) [2]:
  1. Find the funnel leak (GA, heatmaps, behavioral analysis)
  2. Emotional targeting research — the differentiator: customer emotional triggers, buying decision process, psychological profile (interviews, visitor + customer surveys, review mining, social listening, emotional competitive analysis); mine for common themes: top 3 pains, top 3 desired outcomes, words customers use to describe the solution
  3. Build the experiment: conversion copy → wireframe/design → email sequence → dev brief → QA → launch
  4. Test, analyze, report, iterate (monitoring, mid-test QA, documented reports, next-test planning)
- **3-pillar strategic website audit** (2025): 1) messaging/emotional resonance 2) design (imagery, colors, hierarchy) 3) UX (seamless, simple, intuitive) — done *without* conversion data; the input is customer motivations/emotions/outcomes [3]

## Heuristics
- Ask customers "what key problem does our solution eliminate or lessen for you?" — NOT "why did you sign up?" (feature/price answers) [4]
- Ask "how would you describe this solution to a colleague?" — gets testimonial language and customer vocabulary [4]
- If copy doesn't evoke emotion, it isn't converting; "a value proposition that resonates will outperform a dozen button color tests" [3]

## Tactics
- Interview clients AND team members; competitor research; customer segmentation [2]
- Review mining (thousands of reviews), social listening for emotional language [4]
- Reframe feature statements to outcome statements: "Easy to Use Infographic Creator" → "Make Impressive Infographics" [2]
- Report after every test with records for future reference; feed learnings to retention/email/ads teams [2]

## Tools
- GA, heatmaps/behavioral tools, survey tools, A/B tools (e.g., Google Optimize-class), QA processes [2]

## Inputs
- Customer pains, desired emotional outcomes, buying-decision process, customer language — before any page audit or test design [1,2,3]

## Outputs
- Strategic site audits, emotional-targeting test roadmaps, copy + design treatments, post-test reports, her book (The Emotional Targeting Framework book, 2025) [2,3]

## Metrics
- Conversions (claims repeated 10X–20X client lifts), but equally: insight quality and cross-team learning ("whether the new variation wins or loses, the test must have material impact") [2]

## Decision rules
- When you know the customer's emotional drivers → audit messaging/design/UX against them → test strategies that trigger those emotions [2,3]
- When research shows a gap between "what you're saying" and "what customers want to hear" → rewrite copy/design before testing anything cosmetic [3]
- Test whole strategies (headline + imagery + proof aligned to one emotional trigger), not isolated elements [2]
- Never guess the change; research determines what to change [2]

## Failure modes
- Testing button colors, CTAs and single elements ("surface-level elements") — untestable in meaning, hard to scale [1,2]
- Demographic personas ("age, location, gender, browser") instead of psychological profiles [4]
- Auditing pages without knowing the customer's decision process [3]
- Best-practice checklists ("CRO best practices") as substitutes for customer research [3]

## Contrarian beliefs
- Pure-data CRO (analytics-first, element-testing, best-practice audits) misses the actual decision driver: emotion [1,2,4]
- CRO should be a strategic function feeding product, lifecycle and retention — not a page-level testing function [1]

## Examples
- Client list: Bitly, Strata, Atera, Thinkific, SproutSocial [1]
- Infographic tool case: reframed feature headline to outcome headline + empowering subtitle — her canonical example of emotional reframing [2]

## Conditions
- Works when the business has access to customers (interviews, surveys, reviews) and willingness to invest in foundational research ("you don't need deep pockets, but there's a lot of work involved") [4]
- Best in DTC/SaaS/B2B where purchase decisions are emotionally loaded [1,2]

## Limitations
- The 10X–20X conversion claims are agency-reported, not published controlled evidence [2]
- Strategy-level tests sacrifice attribution (she acknowledges single-element tests are easier to analyze) [2]
- Emotional research is qualitative; her framework still relies on A/B testing to validate — but the hypotheses come from emotion research, not analytics [2]
