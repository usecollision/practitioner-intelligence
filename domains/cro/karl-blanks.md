## Beliefs
- "Most websites lose. Almost all of them." — success requires a scientific approach to web design, not decoration [2]
- "Conversion is not an afterthought. Conversion is identifying what type of company your visitors would ideally love to do business with… and then becoming that company." [2]
- CRO grows profits "scientifically": analyze visitors, create optimized pages, A/B-test to measure the increase [3]
- Codified methodology is the product: Queen's Award for Innovation was for "codifying the scientific methodology that companies like Google and Amazon now use to improve websites"; Google made CRE its first international A/B-testing partner [3]

## Principles
- Design for the "moron in a hurry": "Design your processes for what you perceive to be a busy, lazy, drunk, amnesiac idiot… Even geniuses with time on their hands will be grateful that you did." [2]
- Focus means neglect: "Focus means saying no to the hundred other good ideas" (Jobs, via his book) [2]
- Redefine competition: "The best way to 'beat your competitors' is often to redefine yourself so that you have fewer of them" (niching) [2]
- Guarantees are powerful but dangerous: "Used right, it can cut through customer objections. Used wrong, it can cut through profits." [2]
- Customer-centricity is the whole methodology: understand the visitors, then become the company they'd love to buy from [2]

## Frameworks
- **CRE scientific CRO methodology**: visitor analysis → page optimization → A/B testing to measure the sales increase; applied across 80+ verticals including the world's largest web companies [3]
- **Conversion heuristics lineage** — note: the master map tags Blanks "MECLABS-era"; the MECLABS heuristics (Conversion Index, Test Protocol, single/multi-factor testing, validity-threat monitoring, 3-stage testing cycle) belong to Flint McGlaughlin's MECLABS/MarketingExperiments institute, not CRE. Blanks' canon is *Making Websites Win* (with Ben Jesson). Both share the 2005-2015 "scientific testing rigor" era. [2,3,4]
- Business-level frames from *Making Websites Win*: niching for success, fulfilling more customer needs (raising LTV, amortizing acquisition costs), building a company "deserving of success" rather than CRO as bolt-on [2]

## Processes
- Analyze → hypothesize → optimize → A/B test → measure → iterate; the test protocol discipline is the document-of-record (MECLABS' Test Protocol is the canonical version of this; CRE ran equivalent rigor in-house) [2,3,4]

## Heuristics
- If you can't explain why a guarantee will work for your business, don't add one [2]
- Design for the worst-case visitor, not the ideal one [2]
- Say no to 100 good ideas to make one great page [2]

## Tactics
- Deep customer/visitor analysis before any design work (their differentiating step) [3]
- A/B testing as the measurement of every change [3]
- Write for clarity and simplicity (his book's praised style: simple, clear, concise) [2]

## Tools
- Google's first international A/B-testing partner (2008-era Optimizely predecessor / Google Website Optimizer) [3]

## Inputs
- Visitor behavior analysis, business model context (LTV, acquisition costs), competitive landscape [2,3]

## Outputs
- Optimized websites with measured sales increases ("doubled the sales of many leading websites"), the book *Making Websites Win* (Amazon bestseller, 15 categories), training for Google's team [2,3]

## Metrics
- Sales/profit increases measured via A/B tests; 2x-5x results reported by reviewers of his book [2,3]

## Decision rules
- When a page underperforms → analyze visitors first, never guess [3]
- When considering a guarantee → only use it if it cuts objections without cutting profits (test it) [2]
- When the market is crowded → niche/redefine to reduce competitors [2]
- When deciding what to build → neglect the 100 good ideas; focus on the few that matter [2]

## Failure modes
- Treating conversion as an afterthought/bolt-on [2]
- Design for yourself (curse of knowledge) instead of the rushed, distracted visitor [2]
- Guarantees deployed without testing [2]
- Competing head-on instead of niching [2]

## Contrarian beliefs
- Most websites should not be incrementally tweaked — they should be rebuilt around what customers want the company to be [2]

## Examples
- CRE: hundreds of businesses in 80+ verticals, two Queen's Awards, Google hired CRE to optimize its websites and train its team [3]
- GoHenry case study (his book's flagship, per reviewer) [2]

## Conditions
- His method assumes B2C/ecommerce or lead-gen sites with measurable sales funnels and enough traffic to A/B test [2,3]
- Legacy positioning: his active practice ended ~2022 (CRE transitioned); treat as the classic canon [3]

## Limitations
- Results claims (2x-5x) are client-reported, not published with methodology detail [2]
- The "redefine yourself" advice is strategy-level, not testable in a normal CRO program [2]
