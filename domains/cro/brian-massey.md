## Beliefs
- CRO is applied science: formalize the process "to keep confirmation bias out of the picture — which is where the scientific method comes into play" [1]
- Most people think CRO is A/B testing; "in reality, we deal with ideas" — testing is a feature of the process, not the process [1]
- Brainstorming sessions "suffer from the biases of the group" — structured idea collection replaces them [1]
- Written his book for managers, not practitioners: "It is designed to help marketing managers and business owners identify what they should be working on" [2]

## Principles
- Hypothesis-first: every idea is written as "If I [hypothesis], I expect [behavior] to change as measured by [metric]" [1]
- Ideas are worthless without evidence ranking: "the biggest reason you're struggling to get people to take the desired action… is not what you think" — it's process, not pages [1]
- Optimize conversion rate AND the revenue quality of conversions — conversion is about more than web pages (his book's core: the Customer Creation Equation) [2]

## Frameworks
- **The Optimizer's Process** (5 steps): 1) Generate optimization ideas 2) Prioritize ideas 3) Develop the messaging 4) Design the page for conversions 5) Test and optimize [1]
- **Hypothesis buckets**: Messaging / Layout-UX / Credibility / Social proof / Security / Fix-it (broken page) — "you should have ideas for each of these five buckets" [1]
- **Prioritization criteria** (1-5 scale each): Evidence · Impact · Effort · Traffic · ROI [1]
- **9-step A/B testing framework**: evaluate existing data → collect additional quant & qual → (develop hypotheses) → develop testing strategy → design tests → run & monitor → assess results → implement/harvest [3]
- **4 buyer personalities** (with Jeffrey Eisenberg, *Waiting for Your Cat to Bark?*): research modes aligned to how different personalities decide [1]

## Processes
- Scientific method cycle: observe → hypothesize → experiment → analyze → implement (his "Digital Laboratory" framing) [1,2]
- Idea capture spreadsheet → bucket → score 1-5 on the five criteria → pick high-ranking idea → design test → run → harvest winners into production [1,3]

## Heuristics
- Have ideas in every bucket before testing; a page strong in messaging but weak in social proof points to the next test [1]
- "Fix it" items (broken pages) outrank everything — fix before testing [1]
- Rank by ROI last among criteria but weigh it — a test that wins but doesn't pay isn't a win [1]

## Tactics
- Use hypothesis spreadsheets as the program's shared artifact (public template) [1]
- Pair quant (analytics, heatmaps) with qual (user testing, surveys) before designing [3]
- Harvest/implement winners: the process doesn't end at "statistically significant" — deployment is step 9 [3]

## Tools
- Analytics, user testing, heatmaps, A/B platforms; public hypothesis prioritization spreadsheet [1,3]

## Inputs
- Existing data (analytics), user testing, user intelligence tools/reports, page inventory, business ROI targets [1,3]

## Outputs
- Hypothesis backlog, prioritized test roadmap, messaging/design treatments, harvested winners; book *Your Customer Creation Equation*; CRO podcast/course [1,2]

## Metrics
- Conversion rate, leads, revenue per visitor; process metrics: % ideas with evidence, test velocity, harvest rate [1,2,3]

## Decision rules
- Score every idea on Evidence, Impact, Effort, Traffic, ROI (1-5); highest total runs next [1]
- Fix broken things before testing anything [1]
- If you lack ideas in a bucket → research that dimension (e.g., no credibility ideas → do social-proof research) [1]
- If a test wins → harvest (implement) it; if it loses → record and move on; "we need to test and optimize" continuously [1,3]

## Failure modes
- Confirmation bias and group-think in idea generation ("brainstorming sessions… suffer from the biases of the group") [1]
- Treating A/B testing as the whole discipline (missing messaging, credibility, security work) [1]
- Not harvesting winners (running tests that never ship) [3]
- Optimizing pages in isolation from the "customer creation" system (traffic → conversion → retention) [2]

## Contrarian beliefs
- The problem is never what you think it is — the "biggest reason" people don't convert is usually not the obvious page element; process discipline uncovers it [1]

## Examples
- Conversion Sciences client practice: "multi-million dollar clients", consistent repeatable results via the 9-step framework [3]
- His process claims: confirmation-bias removal via scientific method as the agency's differentiator [1]

## Conditions
- Fits orgs with enough traffic to test and a team that can implement (agency model); his frameworks are designed for ongoing programs, not one-off audits [1,3]

## Limitations
- The 1-5 scoring is subjective (unlike PXL's binary-with-weights); no published win-rate data; agency claims [1]
- 5-bucket model predates the DXO/journey framing (his book: 2011-era) [2]
