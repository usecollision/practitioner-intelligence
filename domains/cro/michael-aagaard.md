## Beliefs
- CRO revolves around research and consumer psychology, not design taste [3]
- A negative test result is as valuable as a major lift: "experience from almost 400 split tests tells me otherwise" (that failed tests are waste) [2]
- Copy is a direct, measurable conversion lever: "the copy you use in your signup form has direct and measurable effect on your conversion rate" [1]
- You only gain certainty by testing copy on real prospects: "you need to test your copy on your prospects in real life, that's the only way to gain certainty" [3]

## Principles
- Message match: landing page copy must match the phrasing of the ad/link that brought the visitor — reassures people they've come to the right place [5]
- Value before action: CTA copy should communicate the benefit of joining, not the act itself [4]
- Continuity of messaging from ad → landing page → form; a clear value prop answering "why should I choose you instead of your competitors?" is the core of conversion [6]
- Small copy changes can have huge, counterintuitive effects — never trust your assumption; test [1,2]

## Frameworks
- **Flexible landing page copy framework** (2012): structure copy to logically guide prospects to the conversion goal; adaptable to any landing page; produced a 19.94% lift when applied to an existing page [3]
- **5 landing page headline formulas tested** (2013): headline patterns as testable templates [4]
- **CTA critique criteria**: Value — does the copy convey value? Relevance — is it relevant to the scenario? [4]
- Message Match (Unbounce glossary, with Oli Gardner) [5]

## Heuristics
- "Get" consistently beats "Order/Buy/Download/Sign-Up/Join" on CTAs — 14.79% lift case, replicated on dozens of sites and in several languages; audiences voted almost unanimously for "Get" variants [4]
- "My" beats "Your" in CTA possessives: 90% CTR lift testing "Get my free 30-day trial" vs "Get your…"; repeated across sites [2]
- Privacy policy wording is a major lever: adding one hurt sign-ups 18.70%; "We guarantee 100% privacy. Your information will not be shared." won with 19.47% lift; avoid the word "spam" — it backfires (24% download drop) [2,7]
- A guarantee word ("we guarantee") beat plain assurance in privacy microcopy [7]

## Processes
- Research (analytics + psychology + customer understanding) → hypothesis → test on real traffic → document → follow-up tests (he ran 3 follow-up experiments after the privacy-policy surprise) [1,2,7]
- Publishes full test data: duration, sample size, conversions, confidence, standard error [1,7]

## Tactics
- Test one variable at a time for clean attribution (his documented tests are single-factor) [1,2]
- Run tests for 9-15 days to full sample; reports standard error and confidence [1,7]
- Turn failed tests into research: after "Your" lost 24.95%, he tested "My" across many sites to confirm the direction [2]

## Tools
- Standard A/B platforms (Google AdWords Campaign Experiments historically), GA [6]

## Inputs
- Control page data, funnel position (payment page = every click is money), customer motivations, value-exchange clarity [1,6]

## Outputs
- Case studies with full test data, copy frameworks, talks; canonical CRO copy literature [1,2,3,6,7]

## Metrics
- Sign-ups, downloads, CTR, conversions; standard error and statistical confidence reported on every test [1,7]

## Decision rules
- When the value exchange is unclear → clarify value proposition in headline + body before testing anything else (Saxo Bank: 99.4% more conversions, 48.2% lower cost-per-conversion) [6]
- When a treatment loses → don't discard; run follow-up tests to learn the boundary (privacy policy series) [2,7]
- When writing CTA copy → lead with benefit + "Get"/"My" patterns, but verify on your audience [2,4]
- When adding trust elements → test the wording; trust elements can reduce conversions [2,7]
- Full-week runs, to sample size, no early stops [1,7]

## Failure modes
- Assuming your hypothesis is right: "my hypothesis was totally off, and what I thought would increase conversions had the exact opposite effect" (Your vs My; privacy policy) [2]
- Adding reassurance that creates anxiety (privacy wording with "spam") [2,7]
- Unclear value proposition + multiple competing CTAs on PPC pages (Saxo Bank diagnosis) [6]

## Contrarian beliefs
- Failed tests are the best teachers; publish them [2]
- Trust signals are not universally good — badly worded privacy policies actively hurt [2,7]

## Examples
- Bettingexpert.com: form copy tweak +31.54% signups (9 days, 13,560 visitors, 291 conversions, 99% confidence); headline+form follow-up +3.65% [1]
- Saxo Bank forex trial PPC page: value-prop clarity → +99.4% conversions, -48.2% CPA [6]
- Unbounce PPC page: "Get my free 30-day trial" vs "Get your" → 90% CTR lift [2]
- Privacy policy 4-test series: -18.70%, inconclusive, +19.47%, inconclusive [7]

## Conditions
- His method needs enough traffic for clean single-factor tests (13-20K visitors over 9-15 days in his cases) [1,7]
- Copy-first approach best where the offer is complex enough that value must be communicated (SaaS trials, forex, communities) [6]

## Limitations
- Effects he reports (headline formulas, "Get") are context-dependent patterns from his dataset — he explicitly warns to test in your context [3,4]
- His single-factor discipline is slow on low-traffic pages (not addressed) [7]
