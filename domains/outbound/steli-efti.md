## Beliefs
- Subject line is the most important part of the email — spend 80% of your time on it; 60% of recipients never see the body (HEURISTIC).
- Follow-up strategy must exist BEFORE the first send; one-off emails are pointless (FRAMEWORK).
- "Yes is good, No is good. Maybe is where startups and everything of value goes to die" (PRINCIPLE).
- Best expert advice comes from prospects/customers, not template gurus (PRINCIPLE).

## Principles
- Write 1:1, never 1:many: all-caps ad-style subject lines are instantly recognizable and deleted (PRINCIPLE).
- Every sentence should be a pitch; scannable formatting (PRINCIPLE).
- Email structure: who are you, why now, why believe you (credibility), what you want (PRINCIPLE).
- Cold outreach needs 5–8 attempts to reach someone at the right time — timing luck dominates single sends (HEURISTIC).

## Frameworks
- Four steps: open (subject line) → respond (clear ask + why) → follow-up strategy → feedback loops (FRAMEWORK).
- Email-first cold calling: email to get the conversation, then phone; medium choice depends on market (enterprise=email, restaurant owners=phone) (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Send email → follow up 5–8x when cold → escalate to phone when silent → call non-responders for feedback ("advice" framing, not pitch) (PROCESS).
- Ask responders "what made you respond?" on the first call — build a feedback loop (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- Max 8 follow-ups for cold (personal ethics); for warm/high-value, follow up forever until response (HEURISTIC).
- If you only have one email to send, don't send it (HEURISTIC).
- "I haven't heard from you" 1-2-3 reply templates are now so common they're noise (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Call prospects who opened but didn't respond; ask for advice as a founder/senior person — people love giving advice (TACTIC).
- Problem-focused email: offer 5 minutes to vent about a problem; "could we talk Wed/Thu 11am PT?" (TACTIC).
- Specific value proposition beats generic: name what you noticed + the specific idea (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Close CRM (activity-based sales), email + phone + voicemail (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Prospect's actual situation (from research), subject line + body + recipient + results when requesting feedback (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Sales emails, follow-up sequences, scripts, feedback-loop systems; an ebook of 200 follow-up case studies (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Open rate (as subject-line score), response rate over full sequence, % of follow-ups that get replies, closed revenue attributable to follow-up persistence (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If no follow-up plan exists → don't send the first email (DECISION RULE).
- If prospect is silent after 8 cold touches → stop or switch channel (phone), or declare dead (DECISION RULE).
- If warm/high-value → never stop following up until explicit no (DECISION RULE).
- If subject line isn't written → don't send (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Writing subject lines as ads/newsletters (1:many voice) (FAILURE).
- Copying templates without understanding the design logic behind them (FAILURE).
- Generic broad pitch with no specific value proposition — looks like a BS play for a sales call (FAILURE).
- No follow-up = the single biggest missed opportunity; "you're in a race on your own" (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Open rates matter enormously (30-40% = 60% never see body) — against post-MPP "opens are dead" chorus, for subject-line design discipline (OPINION).
- Cold calling isn't dead: pick up the phone after email attempts fail (OPINION).

## Examples
- Half-million-dollar deal closed after 9 months of persistent follow-up, attributed to follow-up advice (EXAMPLE, self-reported).
- Frost & Sullivan-style targeted approach: proof of work + positioning beats enthusiasm (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Best for founder-led and SMB/startup sales where persistence is affordable; enterprise deals where relationship patience pays (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- 8-follow-up persistence is labor-intensive and can annoy markets with high spam-sensitivity; phone escalation assumes phone numbers are available (LIMITATIONS).
