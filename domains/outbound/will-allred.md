## Beliefs
- "Personalization at scale is an oxymoron" — true 1:1 personalization requires human work; AI automation raised the bar for what counts as "good" (OPINION).
- Personalization starts with the targeting hypothesis (who, why, when) — 1:1 is layered on top of a good 1:many base (FRAMEWORK).
- If you can't find enough info to personalize, that's not an excuse to mass-customize — it means you don't know your market (OPINION).
- Sellers must know: their stuff, their customer, their market (PRINCIPLE).

## Principles
- The work is the differentiator: "hard work works when nothing else works" (PRINCIPLE).
- Real personalization = trigger event + past experience comparison + competitor context woven into a short email (PRINCIPLE).
- Email must sound human; Lavender's own feedback flags overly formal tone (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- Mouse Trap framework: short, killer email built from researched context (trigger + comparison + competitor) (FRAMEWORK).
- Targeting-hypothesis-first: who/why/when defines whether you're fishing in puddles (1:many) before you add 1:1 (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Pull a pre-vetted trigger event → compare to prospect's past experience → check competitor usage → weave into a short email (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- A Lavender "A" email (human-crafted, assisted) can sustain 20%+ reply rates in SalesLoft/Outreach contexts; 12% is an updated real-world data point for strong sends (EMPIRICAL — vendor data, treat cautiously).
- IT/technical buyers don't hate salespeople — they hate templated noise (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Segment SalesLoft/Outreach users when benchmarking reply rates so data is apples-to-apples (TACTIC).
- Train sellers to write emails they themselves would respond to (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Lavender (AI email assistant, pre-vetted triggers, formality scoring), SalesLoft/Outreach sequences (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Trigger events, competitor usage data, prospect's past experience/role context (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Human-crafted 1:1 cold emails at whatever volume sellers can sustain; personalization training programs for sales teams (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Total replies (not just reply rate), pipeline generated, close rates from personalization (6x close via business-case personalization cited from Fluint research) (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If you can't genuinely personalize → lead with strong offer-first instead; never fake it (DECISION RULE).
- If targeting hypothesis is weak (puddles), fix who/why/when before investing in 1:1 (DECISION RULE).
- If AI drafts feel robotic → treat as draft; human craft required for "A" emails (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Mass-customization/templated personalization that recipients recognize (FAILURE).
- Giving up on research and defaulting to 1:many when data is thin (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Against the "personalization at scale" trend: you cannot shortcut 1:1 with automation; the market's bar for "good" has risen, not fallen (OPINION).

## Examples
- Customer campaign: 306% more emails sent vs. 1:1 emails; truly 1:1 emails produced 682% more total replies and 1,900% more pipeline (EMPIRICAL — vendor case, self-reported) (EXAMPLE).
- Fluint research: business-case personalization (customer's language, tailored to them) 6x close rates (EMPIRICAL — third-party cited) (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Works when sellers have research time and training; mid-market/enterprise with enough per-account context; fails for high-volume/low-context SMB plays without offer-first fallback (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- 1:1 at true scale is capacity-limited (humans); vendor-reported benchmarks (12%, 20%, 682%) are self-selected and not independently audited (LIMITATIONS).
