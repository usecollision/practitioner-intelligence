## Beliefs
- Offer quality is the only durable advantage in cold email; copy and infrastructure are downstream problems (FACT/OPINION — repeated across multiple posts).
- "The offer is the 80/20 of cold email, not the copy." Most people spend 90% of time on subject lines; the real failure is pitching a service instead of an offer (HEURISTIC).
- Cold email's only job is to get a reply and start a conversation — it never closes deals (FRAMEWORK).
- Deliverability problems are usually actually offer/targeting problems in disguise (OPINION).

## Principles
- Order of operations: fix offer → fix copy → scale infrastructure. Never the reverse (FRAMEWORK).
- Email copy capped at 50–60 words; if you can't say the offer in 60 words, the offer isn't clear (HEURISTIC).
- Specific beats vague at every funnel step: "We booked 23 qualified calls in 30 days" > "We help companies scale outbound" (TACTIC).
- Entry-point offer: low friction, tangible deliverable, clear outcome, near-zero commitment for the stranger (TACTIC).

## Frameworks
- Offer-First framework: audit whether a stranger with zero trust would respond to the offer; if not, rebuild as a specific, tangible, low-commitment deliverable (FRAMEWORK).
- Service vs. Offer distinction: service = what you do; offer = what prospect gets, at low risk, quickly (FRAMEWORK).
- 1-in-400 booking-rate planning: plan infrastructure backwards from worst-realistic reply-to-meeting math; campaigns with sharp offers hit 1-in-150 (HEURISTIC).

## Processes
- Validate message on small volume subsets first; only scale when message clearly works (PROCESS).
- Check domains daily; replace flagged domains fast (PROCESS).
- Consistent daily send caps per mailbox (e.g., 15 cold + 15 warm-up per mailbox/day); never spike (TACTIC).

## Heuristics
- When copy tweaks move opens but not replies, the problem is downstream of copy — it's the offer (HEURISTIC).
- Sending >2 follow-ups within a week trains spam filters to flag the domain (HEURISTIC).
- A clean offer-first email from a real domain beats a "fake-personal" email 9 times out of 10 (HEURISTIC).
- If opens are fine and replies flat → change offer, not subject lines (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- Work backwards from meetings needed → inboxes required → infrastructure, before writing copy (TACTIC).
- Cap copy at 50–60 words; lead with prospect's problem, not the solution (TACTIC).
- Either personalize genuinely (real research / Clay) OR lead with strong offer-first opener — the fake middle ground gets deleted (TACTIC).
- Build inbound signals (LinkedIn activity driving replies) to offset cold reputation (TACTIC).

## Tools
- Smartlead (inbox rotation at scale), Instantly (warming/monitoring), Clay (real context personalization at scale), ChatGPT/Claude for drafting (never as the final voice) (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Number of meetings needed (to plan infrastructure); validation data from small-volume sends; daily domain-health checks (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Cold email scripts (offer-first, <60 words), deliverability infrastructure setups, campaigns at scale (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Reply rate (north metric); opens as leading indicator only; deliverability/inbox placement per domain; spam-complaint rate; 1-in-400 → 1-in-150 booking math (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If opens rise but replies flat → the offer is broken; rewrite offer before copy (DECISION RULE).
- If a stranger can't say "yes" with zero risk/commitment → offer not ready; rebuild (DECISION RULE).
- If sending volume must grow → raise daily caps gradually; never spike on a "big push" (DECISION RULE).
- If domain gets flagged → replace fast; don't nurse it (DECISION RULE).
- If copy >60 words → cut copy, don't add charm (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- Pitching a service instead of an offer (zero responses until entry-point offer built) (FAILURE).
- Sending spikes (e.g., 1,000 emails Monday, nothing rest of week) → 99% chance spam (FAILURE).
- Faking personalization — "Curious to know..." and pretend-knowledge lines (FAILURE).
- Burning a 60-day re-contact window with an undialed message (FAILURE).
- Vague deliverables in consulting-speak (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Copy quality bar hasn't changed much; systems/targeting/offer bar has risen. Bad systems now fail faster and more publicly (OPINION).
- One email to TAM every 60 days is directionally right for deliverability, but only once offer+copy are dialed (OPINION).

## Examples
- Magazine publisher pitching full design service to 50 niche publishers: zero responses; entry-point offer (specific low-commitment deliverable) fixed it (EXAMPLE).
- Frost & Sullivan offer obtained via single targeted cold email: right person + proof of work + positioning over enthusiasm (EXAMPLE).
- Founders who rewrote subject lines 12x: opens up, replies flat — offer was the problem (EXAMPLE).

## Conditions
- Works for B2B services/agencies with a definable entry-point deliverable; any budget; needs a domain setup + 15-30/day per mailbox volume. Works at 9-figure volume for clients with 60-day cycles (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- If your product genuinely needs a long sales cycle with no low-commitment entry offer, offer-first copy can feel thin; also heavy 1-in-400 math assumes measurable reply→meeting conversion (LIMITATIONS).
