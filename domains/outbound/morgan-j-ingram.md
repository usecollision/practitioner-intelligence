## Beliefs
- Cadence length is a choice, not a rule: 6-touch, 7-touch, 11-touch, 15-touch all work — what matters is message relevance per touch (OPINION).
- "If your message doesn't matter to the prospect, it doesn't matter how many times you send it" — fit and resonance, not frequency, is the usual problem (PRINCIPLE).
- Starting with a question is the highest-ROI opener: easier to respond to, faster to read, sparks conversation (HEURISTIC).

## Principles
- Layer personalization in progressively: start light, add research as non-response accumulates (PRINCIPLE).
- Use multiple channels (email, LinkedIn, phone, video) — versatility beats volume in one channel (PRINCIPLE).
- Touch variety per cadence step: new thread vs same thread, voicemail vs no voicemail, visual vs text (PRINCIPLE).

## Frameworks
- 6-touch Question-First campaign: Day 1 Spark Question email → Day 2 "reason I asked" (same thread) → Day 3 LinkedIn blank connection request → Day 6 new-thread email → Day 10 screenshot email (new thread) → Day 15 goodbye email (FRAMEWORK).
- "Spider-Man Cadence" (7-touch, ~20 days, 11 total touches): Day 1 LI connection request (no note, higher acceptance) → Day 2 tailored email with interest CTA → Day 3 call + voicemail then same-thread email with more info → Day 5 call (no voicemail) → Day 7 call (no voicemail) → Day 10 new-thread email → Day 12 tailored video email → Day 15 email with visuals → Day 18 call + voicemail → Day 20 final email (FRAMEWORK).

## Processes
- Question-first sequence → if no reply, escalate personalization each subsequent touch → end with low-friction goodbye that invites response (PROCESS).

## Heuristics
- 20% response rate achievable with question-first 6-touch campaign (EMPIRICAL — self-reported test, AE context).
- Connection requests without notes get higher acceptance rates (HEURISTIC).
- Same-thread follow-ups feel like continuation; new threads re-surface the topic (HEURISTIC).

## Tactics
- "Spark question" opener: ask a specific, easy question about their business (TACTIC).
- Screenshot email: reference a concrete artifact (their site/feature) in a new thread (TACTIC).
- Goodbye email: close the loop politely — often gets replies from the respectful (TACTIC).
- Tailored video email at day 12 as a channel change (TACTIC).

## Tools
- LinkedIn (connection requests), email sequences (Outreach/SalesLoft class), phone, video email tools (TOOLS).

## Inputs
- Prospect research for layered personalization; question topic relevant to their role (INPUTS).

## Outputs
- Multichannel cadences; SDR training content; cadence templates (OUTPUTS).

## Metrics
- Response rate per touch (which touch converts), acceptance rate (LinkedIn), meetings booked, reply quality (METRICS).

## Decision rules
- If a touch type isn't converting, change the touch type — don't add more of the same (DECISION RULE).
- If no response by day 15, switch to goodbye/breakup frame rather than pushing harder (DECISION RULE).
- Choose cadence length by prospect patience/industry norms: 7-touch default, extend to 11-15 for long-cycle enterprise (DECISION RULE).

## Failure modes
- 11-15 touch sequences where every touch is the same irrelevant message — interrupting 11x instead of 1x (FAILURE).
- Message without fit: "No problem is illuminated, nothing meaningfully different is offered" (FAILURE).
- Asking for the meeting too early (before interest CTA) in the first touch (FAILURE).

## Contrarian beliefs
- Response rate > cadence length: most teams over-touch because they under-relevance (OPINION).
- Question-first beats value-first openers for conversation start (OPINION).

## Examples
- 6-touch question campaign tested with AEs: 20% response rate; prospects answer the spark question "which would lead right into a meeting" (EXAMPLE, self-reported).

## Conditions
- B2B SDR/AE outbound with LinkedIn as viable channel; works for both SDR and AE contexts; needs per-touch content variety budget (CONDITIONS).

## Limitations
- Self-reported response rates; question-first may underperform for very technical/enterprise buyers who prefer direct value; video/visual touches need production capacity (LIMITATIONS).
