# CUSTOMER & MARKET RESEARCH — Discipline Synthesis

Covers: customer interviews, JTBD, ICP building, personas, survey design, win/loss analysis.
Practitioners: Rob Fitzpatrick, Steve Blank, Cindy Alvarez, Adele Revella, Teresa Torres, Bob Moesta, Alan Klement, Tony Ulwick, Clayton Christensen, Momoko Price, Fred Reichheld (+ org/vendor sources: Clozd, NN/g, Krosnick, HubSpot, Clay, ziellab).
Verified: 2026-08-15. Evidence base: 15 fetched primary/secondary sources; per-practitioner files in domains/research/.

## Consensus (independent agreement, 3+ practitioners)

1. **Talk about the customer's past behavior, never their opinions about the future.** Fitzpatrick ("facts from the past, not opinions about the future"), Alvarez (question bank: frequency, last time, workarounds), Moesta (timeline of events), Revella (trigger moment), Christensen (why did she do it that way), Torres (recent specific episodes). This is the single most replicated rule in the field. (FRAMEWORK, T1)
2. **Never ask "would you buy / how much would you pay / do you like this idea."** Fitzpatrick (explicitly, Indie Hackers), Alvarez (hypotheticals banned), Christensen (feature-wishlist research failed in the milkshake case — customers answered honestly and sales didn't move). (FAILURE-KNOWLEDGE, T1)
3. **Pitching contaminates interviews; keep the conversation about them.** Fitzpatrick ("you can lead people to any response if you start pitching"), Alvarez (first-minute model explicitly avoids selling framing), Torres (stay unbiased; don't validate your solution). (PRINCIPLE, T1)
4. **Segment by job/behavior/decision, not demographics.** Christensen (demographics correlate but don't cause), Revella (5 rings segment by buying behavior), Moesta (jobs as markets), Klement ("activities are not jobs"), Clay/HubSpot (ICP from closed-won behavior, not firmographic wish-lists). (FRAMEWORK, T1)
5. **Personas/ICPs must be built from evidence (interviews + won/lost data), not internal imagination.** Revella (buyers not customers, not sales), ziellab ("persona fiction is the norm; start from 20–30 closed-won deals"), HubSpot (5-step ICP from best customers → interviews → patterns → template → quarterly refresh), Clay (ICP from closed-won attributes). (PRINCIPLE, T1)
6. **Interviews discover; surveys validate/quantify.** Ulwick (qualitative outcome discovery → quantitative importance/satisfaction), Moesta (switch interviews vs surveys — "one thing surveys never could"), Blank (Discovery = convergence; Validation = sales numbers), Krosnick/quant canon, NN/g (qual for depth, quant for generalization). (PRINCIPLE, T1)
7. **Recruit the full spectrum, not happy customers.** Moesta (switchers + almost-switched + switched-away; "happy best customers" excluded), Revella (buyers incl. status-quo choosers), ziellab (won AND lost buyers), Clozd (win + loss interviews). (PRINCIPLE, T1)
8. **Small continuous research beats big quarterly studies** where access exists. Torres (weekly touchpoints), Alvarez (5-minute kickstarts, sprinkle into existing conversations), Blank (iterative loops). (PRINCIPLE, T1/T2)
9. **Interview count is decided by saturation/pattern-repetition, not quotas.** Blank (Discovery done when responses converge), Moesta (5–8 interviews for a job; stop when stories repeat), academic evidence (9–17 for saturation in homogenous populations; Griffin & Hauser 20–30 for 90–95% of needs), Clozd (≥20 win/loss interviews per segment for themes). (DECISION RULE, T1)
10. **Record and transcribe; capture customer language verbatim.** Clozd (record, don't just note-take), Moesta (their language becomes positioning/copy), Ulwick (real-time transcription shown to customers). (TACTIC, T1)

## Disagreement

1. **Story-first vs outcome-first JTBD.** Moesta/Klement: reconstruct the story/timeline; forces emerge from events; never ask about metrics. Ulwick: needs are undefinable; define outcomes with strict syntax; customers can't articulate needs. Resolution condition: story-first when the purchase is emotional/rare (B2C, big-ticket); outcome-first when the job is operational and measurable (B2B tools, engineering workflows). Klement explicitly rejects Ulwick-style taxonomies; Ulwick dismisses vague "needs first" approaches.
2. **What customers can tell you.** Fitzpatrick/Alvarez/Moesta: customers CAN give you truth (about their past). Ulwick: customers cannot articulate needs at all (must be shown outcome statements). Condition: customers are reliable about facts of their past behavior; unreliable about abstract wants/needs. Both are right about different question types.
3. **Interview count.** JTBD school: 5–8 interviews suffice for a job (pattern repetition). Academic saturation review: 9–17; Griffin & Hauser: 20–30 for 90–95% of needs. Win/loss: 20+/segment. Condition: the narrower the job and more homogeneous the population, the fewer interviews; the more you need to *size* or *prioritize*, the more (or switch to quant).
4. **NPS as the one number.** Reichheld: NPS drives growth, use as a system. MIT Sloan/Keiningham et al.: no empirical superiority over other satisfaction metrics; untestable as a system. Condition: NPS is directionally fine as a benchmark + loop-trigger; never use it as the sole decision metric or a compensation base.
5. **Persona structure.** Revella: role-agnostic 5-rings personas built from buyer interviews. ziellab: one persona per buying-committee role (champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, end user, blocker), half a page each. Condition: Revella for message/marketing strategy; role-based for sales enablement and outbound. Both reject demographic personas.
6. **Who runs discovery.** Blank/Torres: founders/teams must do it themselves. Enterprise practice: dedicated research teams. Condition: team-led for speed and empathy when access is easy; specialist-led for scale, rigor, and win/loss neutrality (Clozd: people who sold the deal must not run the win/loss interviews).

## Conditions — when each methodology is correct

- **Mom Test rules**: any stage; indispensable pre-PMF; cheap. Fails when you need market sizing.
- **Switch interviews**: when a real switching category exists (30–90 day recruitability); B2C and B2B; 45–90 min each.
- **ODI**: established, measurable jobs + budget for 3-hour interviews and surveys of hundreds; overkill for early-stage discovery.
- **CustDev (Blank)**: earliest stage; founders with time; enterprise validation needs only 3–5 reference customers, consumer needs 300–500.
- **Continuous discovery (Torres)**: teams with standing customer access; fails pre-launch.
- **5-rings personas**: category with recent evaluations; B2B.
- **ICP from closed-won**: must have ≥20–30 won deals to mine; new products with zero wins must use proxy signals (adjacent competitors' customers).
- **Win/loss**: needs CRM access + buyer willingness + neutrality; ≥20 interviews per segment before acting.
- **NPS**: existing customer base; useless pre-launch.

## Evidence evaluation

- **Empirically supported**: saturation ranges (systematic review of 23 studies, 9–17 interviews; Griffin & Hauser modeling 20–30; Guest et al. 12); questionnaire-design effects (Krosnick: acquiescence, order effects, satisficing, DK-option effects — the strongest evidence in the discipline); NPS non-superiority (Keiningham 2007, MIT Sloan); Gartner/Forrester buying-committee stats (6–10 people, 74% unhealthy conflict — via ziellab).
- **Practitioner heuristic (widely replicated but not formal evidence)**: Mom Test rules; past-not-future; switcher recruitment; 5–8 interview patterns; interview-while-typing; "15% better" threshold (Ulwick — company-internal claim, treat as T2); "can I play their role in the movie" stopping rule (Moesta).
- **Opinion/framing**: JTBD as theory of consumer action (Klement); persona fiction prevalence; NPS-as-system.

## Outliers (worth investigating)

- **Ulwick's 15% job-done-better threshold** — a falsifiable-sounding claim from thousands of Strategyn projects; if real, it's the closest thing to a ROI rule for research.
- **Moesta's "direct the actor" test** and the 20–30 interviews needed for low-stakes habitual purchases.
- **Klement's "no types of jobs"** — rejects a taxonomy most of the JTBD world uses.
- **Blank's "even infinite cash is useless" in discovery** — anti-funding orthodoxy.
- **Clay's fit-vs-intent two-axis ICP** — signal-based ICP as an operational data product (technographics + behavior), the most modern ICP definition found.
- **Christensen's causality claim** (demographics correlate, jobs cause) — the theoretical justification for all of the above.

## Failure knowledge (what repeatedly doesn't work)

1. **Leading/hypothetical questions** ("would you buy", "how much would you pay", "do you like it") — Fitzpatrick's documented leading-to-any-answer failure; also the milkshake feature-wishlist failure. (T1)
2. **Persona fiction** — personas written from imagination/demographics, no interviews, no CRM data; "Marketing Mary" describes a person who doesn't exist; generic AI personas are the same fiction at lower cost (ziellab). (T1)
3. **Interviewing the wrong people** — your own sales team, your happy customers (Revella), instead of buyers incl. losers/status-quo. (T1)
4. **Confirmation bias / solution-love** — Torres: stay unbiased about your solution; Blank: "avoid being enamored with the idea". (T1)
5. **NPS as a bare score** — tracked without inner/outer loops, without checking firm-level predictiveness, sometimes compensated (Forrester). (T1)
6. **Surveys used for discovery** — Moesta: surveys can't reconstruct decisions; survey design flaws (acquiescence, order effects, double-barreled, DK options) documented by Krosnick. (T1)
7. **Buying early business with cash** — discounts/giveaways during validation obscure whether a market exists (Blank). (T1)
8. **Not recording interviews** — note-taking kills follow-up questions (Clozd). (T1)
9. **Win/loss run by the reps who sold the deal** — buyers won't be candid; insights get rationalized (Clozd). (T1)
10. **Treating one interview as evidence** — Clozd: don't act on 1–2 data points; 20+/segment. (T1)

## Collision Method sketch — the Marketing OS should encode:

- **Objective**: produce decision-grade customer evidence for ICP, personas, messaging, positioning, and win/loss — without fabricating segments.
- **Prerequisites**: CRM with closed-won/lost deals (if B2B); customer/buyer access; recording capability; one trained interviewer (anyone can learn, but skills matter: "bad interviewers get opinions, not events" — Moesta).
- **Inputs**: existing won/lost data; support tickets; sales call recordings; funnel analytics (Momoko: baseline conversion + traffic or don't start); a list of jobs/segments under question.
- **Diagnosis**: if no won deals exist → discovery mode (Mom Test + switch interviews on adjacent categories). If deals exist → persona/ICP mode (20–30 closed-won analysis → interviews per role). If deals are being won/lost at scale → win/loss mode (≥20 interviews/segment, neutral interviewer).
- **Decision tree**:
  1. Can we recruit people who recently switched or decided (30–90 days)? → switch-style interviews. Else → Mom Test with current-customer proxies.
  2. Do we need prioritization/market size, not just direction? → add survey/quant phase (Ulwick-style outcome statements or simple frequency/priority questions).
  3. Are we building personas? → one per buying-committee role; data from won AND lost buyers; half a page: trigger, metric they answer for, first objection, line that moves them; refresh quarterly.
  4. Are we building ICP? → mine closed-won for common attributes → interview top customers (≥10) for language → cluster firmographic+technographic+behavioral signals → score fit and intent separately → refresh quarterly.
  5. Win/loss program? → leadership/sales buy-in first; third-party interviewer; record+transcribe; tag themes; ≥20 interviews per segment; present findings with action items; tie into persona refresh.
- **Methodology**: interview rules — past facts not future opinions; specifics not generalizations; their language verbatim; deflect pitches; recruit both sides (won/lost, switched/almost-switched); 45–90 min (switch), 20–30 min (Mom Test); record everything; stop at saturation (pattern repetition; 9–17 for homogeneous groups; 20–30 if you want 90%+ of needs; 20+/segment for win/loss themes).
- **Execution**: small continuous batches (Torres/Alvarez) rather than one giant study; pair interviews (one drives, one listens — Moesta); transcribe and tag; synthesize into timeline/forces or 5-ring or role-persona format depending on goal.
- **Metrics**: saturation reached (not quota); % interviews yielding new facts; % quotes usable in copy; persona/ICP refresh recency; win/loss: theme frequency per segment, action items closed.
- **Stopping rules**: stop interviewing when stories/patterns repeat (Moesta's "could direct the actor", Blank's convergence); stop when a hypothesis is validated/invalidated enough to act; win/loss: stop per segment at 20 interviews or when themes stabilize; kill survey work if baseline analytics absent (Price).
- **Failure modes (encode as guards)**: leading questions; pitching; compliment-data; demographic personas; happy-customer-only samples; survey-for-discovery; NPS-as-score; rep-run win/loss; acting on 1–2 interviews; AI personas trained on generic internet content (AI is fine on YOUR transcripts, never as persona oracle — ziellab).
- **Conditions**: method scales from 2-person startup (Mom Test + 5–8 interviews) to enterprise (ODI-scale quant + continuous discovery + win/loss programs); the binding constraint is always honest access to recent deciders.
- **Limitations**: no method yields market size without quant; saturation ≠ representativeness; JTBD/job framing requires an existing switching category; win/loss needs deal volume.
- **Confidence**: consensus rules T1 (multiple independent sources); thresholds and counts T2 (single-source or company-internal).
